## What the role actually looks like

At frontier labs and corporate research groups, a Research Scientist (AI/ML) originates new ML techniques, owns multi-month research agendas, designs controlled ablations and publishes or ships architectural, post-training or alignment advances [A/B · T0-55 L55, L140]. Across T0-55's sample of 45 AI/ML research postings (November 2026), work distributes across five layers [M · T0-55 L346–L355]:

- **Foundation models and core research (L2: 27/45, 60.0%):** pre-training, diffusion/language architectures, reinforcement learning theory, multimodal models (8.9% explicit; 15.6% including vision/video/speech/audio) and reasoning or test-time compute (6.7%) [M · T0-55 L350, L356–L358].
- **Applied or domain research (9/45, 20.0%):** combining ML research, production software engineering and deep scientific or industry expertise — biology (Anthropic Life Sciences), finance or materials physics (Mistral Science), energy equipment (GE Vernova) and enterprise R&D (Accenture, JPMorgan) [A/B/M · T0-55 L142, L352].
- **Post-training and adaptation (L4: 6/45, 13.3%):** reward modelling, RLHF/RLVR, preference learning and agentic post-training (Anthropic Reward Models, xAI, Scale AI) [A/B/M · T0-55 L351].
- **Evaluation, safety and assurance (2/45, 4.4%) and ML systems research (1/45, 2.2%):** AI safety/alignment and systems-layer research (DeepMind, NVIDIA) [A/B/M · T0-55 L353–L354].

**Skills reality versus all-fields benchmarks (n = 12 fully read postings; ±28 pp Wilson intervals — cross-check only).** Every fully read posting names ML fundamentals (12/12) and nearly all demand **novel research and publications (11/12, 91.7%; 9 in required lists)** [M · T0-55 L90, L200, L205]. Python (8/12; the remaining 4 name JAX or PyTorch), PyTorch (8/12 — best read as PyTorch or JAX, with JAX in 5/12 and required at DeepMind and xAI), deep learning/transformers (8/12), research methods (8/12) and large-scale training (8/12) form the core technical stack, followed by LLM modelling (7/12), open-source/reproducible code evidence (6/12), broader mathematical foundations (6/12; explicit linear algebra in only 2/12), post-training (5/12) and evaluations (5/12) [M · T0-55 L91, L201–L222]. Three scorecard items are artefacts of JobsPipe's all-fields Research Scientist population (software/IT only 8.7%, publishing 6.8%, pharma 3.3%): `creativity` (0/12 vs JobsPipe 23.0%), `dsa` (0/12 vs JobsPipe 26.0% — keep for coding interviews, not posting keywords) and `llm_apis` (1/12, Anthropic Life Sciences only — JobsPipe's 24.4% "llm" reflects LLMs as a research subject, not API integration) [B/M · T0-55 L91, L166, L208–L210, L222].

**Proof and credentials.** Zero of 12 fully read postings (and none observed across 33 snippets) name any certification [M · T0-55 L317]. Employers instead ask for peer-reviewed or first-author publications at flagship venues (NeurIPS, ICML, ICLR, ACL, EMNLP, COLM), reproducible open-source code or model releases (6/12), a Google Scholar link plus a short statement of best work (xAI), or an end-to-end training run you owned and debugged (Sarvam AI); DeepMind's undated careers page adds that it reviews GitHub and side projects and does not over-index on raw publication volume [A/M · T0-55 L90, L304–L311, L317].

**Five title and search traps to avoid:**
1. **Non-ML title inflation:** T0-55 excluded 8 postings carrying "Research Scientist" that are not ML method work — Anthropic and OpenAI "People Research Scientist" (people analytics), OpenAI Learning & Cognitive Outcomes, DeepMind Philosophy & Human Values, Teva Clinical Research Scientist, and Amazon quantitative/operations-research roles [B · T0-55 L63–L70, L99, L144].
2. **Amazon's inverted convention:** Amazon titles ML method research as [Applied Scientist](applied-scientist.md) (or `MTS – Applied Science`), reserving "Research Scientist" for operations research and quantitative roles [B · T0-55 L59, L87, L143, L467].
3. **Contract "AI trainer" listings:** Glassdoor pairs the title with hourly contract work ("Human Data LLM Research Scientist" at USD 100–120/hour); verify ML method responsibilities before treating a listing as research [C · T0-55 L99, L462, L698].
4. **Expired pages that still load:** Eight Google DeepMind Greenhouse pages in search results carried passed application deadlines (e.g. job 7341608, deadline 2025-10-31) yet still served full job descriptions on 2026-11-13 — always verify dates and closing deadlines [A/B · T0-55 L88, L387, L459].
5. **Unverified labels:** "Apex / Named Frontier Researcher" from earlier records has no posting source in T0-55 [E · T0-55 L410, L439].

## Career path, India demand and market signals

**Big Tech versus frontier-lab PhD requirements.** Among 33 sampled postings with visible degree wording, 19 (57.6%) require a PhD (9 outright; 10 with an equivalent-experience or MS+experience substitute), 3 prefer a PhD, 3 ask for an advanced degree and **8 (24.2%) name no degree** [M · T0-55 L89, L648]. Splitting by employer type reveals a sharp divide: **Big Tech is 10 of 10 PhD** (Meta FAIR, NVIDIA, Microsoft Research, IBM, Amazon AGI), whereas **frontier labs are 4 of 13 PhD-or-equivalent and 6 of 13 name no degree** (Anthropic Reward Models, DeepMind Deep Learning, OpenAI RS, xAI, Mistral ×2) [M · T0-55 L89, L473, L649–L650]. Still, no stated degree does not mean an easy gate: Google DeepMind states that the majority of its research-scientist hires are post-PhD (undated page), and non-PhD postings still ask for first-author publications, influential open-source releases or owned research agendas [A/M · T0-55 L89, L107–L108, L332, L452]. Dedicated PhD new-grad pipelines exist at NVIDIA (L3), Meta FAIR, Amazon AGI and OpenAI's early-career RS/RE cohort (USD 295K base) [A/B · T0-55 L97, L323]. **Preparation rules (no model answers; see [interview loops](../../08-interviewing/interview-loops.md) and the [research prep pack](../../08-interviewing/prep-packs.md#research-pack)):**
- **Prepare to demonstrate "research taste" empirically.** Postings explicitly ask for "strong experimental taste" (DeepMind RL), "strong intuition for experimental design" (Sarvam), choosing impactful problems with minimal oversight (AIXI) and owning a research agenda (OpenAI); structure your research talk around problem selection, baselines, ablations and what changed decisions [A/B/P · T0-55 L294–L300, L692, L694].
- **Practise unaided Python coding and breadth fundamentals.** Live coding without AI assistance and maths/statistics/ML breadth quizzes remain core gates alongside research discussions [A/C/P · T0-55 L117, L286–L288, L696].
