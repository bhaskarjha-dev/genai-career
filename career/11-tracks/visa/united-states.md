# Visa Dossier: United States

> [!CAUTION]
> **Pointers to official sources, not legal or immigration advice.** US immigration rules are subject to rapid executive action, Federal Register rulemaking, monthly Visa Bulletin shifts, and active federal injunctions. Confirm every item on `uscis.gov`, `travel.state.gov`, and with licensed US immigration counsel before acting.

## Wage-weighted H-1B and active rulemaking (2026)

- **Wage-weighted selection is in force:** Finalised under `90 FR 60864` and effective `2026-02-27`, cap-subject H-1B selection weights unique beneficiaries by OEWS prevailing-wage tier: **4 entries for Level IV, 3 for Level III, 2 for Level II, and 1 for Level I** (`T0-37 L74, L191`; confirmed live `2026-11-19`, `[A]`). DHS modelled selection probabilities at roughly **61.16%** (Level IV), **45.87%** (Level III), **30.58%** (Level II), and **15.29%** (Level I), versus ~29.59% under the prior random lottery (`T0-37 L74, L191`, `[A]`).
- **FY2027 outcome:** Registrations fell roughly 38.5% to ~211,600 (down from 343,981 in FY2026, `[M]`); US advanced-degree share among selected registrants rose from 57% to **71.5%**, while Level I fell to **17.7%** of selected beneficiaries (`T0-37 L192`, `[B]`). The FY2027 cap filled on the first round with no second lottery (`T0-37 L192`, `[B]`). FY2028 registration dates are unpublished as of `2026-11-19` (historically March; live check `[B]`).
- **Litigated `$100,000` proclamation and proposed `$103,265` fee:** Proclamation 10973 (`2025-09-21`, extended by Proclamation 11069 to `2027-09-21`) targeted new cap-subject and cap-exempt H-1B petitions for workers outside the US without a valid H-1B visa — not in-US changes of status or extensions (`T0-37 L75`). Implementing agency policies remain vacated (`California v. Mullin`, D. Mass. `2026-06-08`; First Circuit stay denied `2026-07-24`) and preliminarily enjoined (`Global Nurse Force v. Trump`, N.D. Cal. `2026-09-30`), so **the `$100,000` fee is not being collected as of `2026-11-19`** (`T0-37 L75, L296`; confirmed live `2026-11-19`, `[B]`). Separately, DHS's `$103,265` cap-subject fee NPRM (`91 FR 54817`, Docket `USCIS-2026-0298`, comments closed `2026-09-24`) has no final rule as of `2026-11-19` (`T0-37 L76`; confirmed live `2026-11-19`, `[A]`).
- **Adjudication and compliance tightening in force:**
  - **PA-2026-05 (`2026-08-05`):** Restores full officer discretion to deny any benefit request immediately without first issuing an RFE or NOID; challenged in `AAUP v. Mullin` (`1:26-cv-14527`, filed `2026-10-05`, `[A · T0-37 L77–L78]`; confirmed live `2026-11-19`). Front-load every petition as if no RFE will be granted.
  - **9-11 Response and Biometric Entry-Exit fee (`91 FR 51360`, effective `2026-09-09` through `2027-09-30`):** Extended the `$4,000` H-1B / `$4,500` L-1 surcharge for 50+/50% employers to **all extensions**, not only initial petitions (`T0-37 L169`, `[A]`).
  - **EAD automatic extensions ended (`2025-10-30`):** Timely renewal filings no longer auto-extend expiring EADs (including H-4 EADs); challenged in C.D. Cal. (`T0-37 L183`, `[B]`). File renewals at the earliest window (180 days).
  - **Social-media vetting and narrowed interview waivers:** Mandatory online-presence review for H-1B/H-4 started `2025-12-15` (expanded `2026-10-01`); dropbox interview waivers were sharply narrowed from `2025-09-02` (`T0-37 L84, L176, L240`, `[B]`).
  - **`$250` Visa Integrity Fee (P.L. 119-21):** Enacted for non-immigrant visa issuance, with staggered and uneven consular payment-portal rollout as of `2026-11-19`; verify instructions with the specific US consulate before interview (`T0-37 L177`; live check `2026-11-19`, `[B]`).
- **Pending proposals (not in force as of `2026-11-19`):** Elimination of the 60-day grace period (`91 FR 57807`), removal of prior-approval deference (`USCIS-2026-0529`), DOL prevailing-wage percentile increases to 34th/52nd/70th/88th (`91 FR 15454`), and the `$70,000` initial / `$30,000` extension OPT school fee NPRM (`91 FR 64566`, `ICEB-2026-0100`, PRA comments open to `2026-12-07`) (`T0-37 L77, L82`; live check `2026-11-19`, `[B]`).

## O-1A, EB-1A, and EB-2 NIW realities

- **O-1A remains viable, with heavier scrutiny:** Combined Form I-129 O approval rate stood at **91.0%** through FY2026 Q1–Q3 (`n = 23,417`), down from 93.9% in FY2025, with a **24.2%** RFE rate (`T0-37 L78, L118, L209`, `[B]`; FY2026 Q4 figures not yet published as of `2026-11-19`). Under PA-2026-05, incomplete O-1A packets risk outright denial rather than an RFE. For the eight criteria translated for an industry GenAI engineer, see [O-1A criteria on the hub](../visa-and-relocation.md#o-1a-the-eight-criteria-for-an-industry-engineer).
- **EB-1A and NIW approval rates dropped sharply:** Secondary USCIS data compilations show EB-1A approvals falling from 66.9% (FY2025) to **32.2%** in FY2026 Q3, and EB-2 NIW standing at **55.3%** in FY2026 Q3 after a 35.7% trough in FY2025 Q4 (`T0-37 L79, L120–L121`, `[C]` — underlying USCIS tables unread). In `Mukherji v. Miller` (D. Neb., `2026-01-28`), a district court held the *Kazarian* step-two final-merits determination unlawful; USCIS withdrew its appeal (`2026-06-10`), yet the AAO rejected a *Mukherji* challenge on `2026-06-16`, so practitioners still treat final merits as active outside the plaintiff's case (`T0-37 L210`, `[B]`).

## Indian-national specifics

- **Visa Bulletin backlogs (November 2026; December 2026 bulletin not yet published as of `2026-11-19`):**
  - **Final Action Dates (India):** EB-1 `2023-02-01` (~3.8-year gap), EB-2 `2013-11-01` (~13.0-year gap; **356,360** approved Indian principals waiting), EB-3 `2014-01-22` (~12.8-year gap; **ahead of EB-2**; 106,214 waiting) (`T0-37 L80, L238`; confirmed live `2026-11-19`, `[B]`).
  - **Dates for Filing (India):** EB-1 `2024-07-01`, EB-2 `2015-01-15`, EB-3 `2015-01-15` (`T0-37 L80, L178`, `[B]`).
  - **Practical consequence:** An Indian national filing a fresh EB-2 PERM or EB-2 NIW in late 2026 secures priority-date retention, AC21 H-1B extensions, and H-4 EAD eligibility — **not** a near-term green card. Reaching a US green card within a single career horizon realistically requires **EB-1A**, **EB-1B** (outstanding researcher), **EB-1C** (via L-1A manager transfer), or cross-chargeability through a spouse born outside India/China (`T0-37 L80, L121`, `[B]`).
- **Indian consular waits (`2026-10-21` snapshot, H/L/O/P/Q non-immigrant visas):** Kolkata 1 month, Mumbai 2 months, Chennai 2.5 months, New Delhi 3.5 months, Hyderabad 6.5 months (`T0-37 L84`, `[B]`). Factor narrowed dropbox eligibility and social-media screening into any international travel (`T0-37 L84, L176, L240`, `[B]`).
- **Three-year Indian bachelor's degrees:** AACRAO EDGE evaluates standard 3-year Indian bachelor's degrees (BSc/BCA/BCom) as equivalent to 2–3 years of US study, which blocks single-source EB-2 eligibility unless paired with a qualifying master's degree or an H-1B/EB-3 experience equivalency (`T0-37 L241`, `[B]`).
- **Employer reality:** FY2025 saw 406,348 H-1B approvals (69.9% India-born, 62% computer occupations, median pay `$133,000`, `T0-37 L83`, `[B]`), and H-1B FY2026 Q1–Q3 approval held at 97.0% (`T0-37 L232`, `[B]`). US big tech and well-funded AI labs still sponsor H-1B transfers, L-1 transfers, and O-1A (especially for senior/staff engineers clearing OEWS Levels III–IV), while early-stage startups often prefer O-1A or L-1 because of March-only registration, litigation overhang, and the `2026-09-09` extension surcharge on 50+/50% IT consultancies (`T0-37 L116–L119, L169`, `[B]`).

## Remote work and tax pointer

- Physical presence governs US work authorisation: working remotely from inside the United States for a non-US employer on visitor entry is **unauthorised employment**, and H-1B work is authorised solely for the petitioning US employer (`T0-37 L250`, `[B]`).
- Conversely, working remotely from outside the US (for example, from India as an independent contractor or via an Employer of Record) requires **no US visa**; see [india-remote-playbook.md](../india-remote-playbook.md) and [india-remote-tax.md](../../09-compensation/india-remote-tax.md).
- US tax residency follows the IRS Substantial Presence Test or green-card status (check `irs.gov` Publication 519 and a cross-border CPA; pointers, not tax advice, `T0-37 L253`).

## Decision rules by profile

- **Mid-level engineer with a US offer (Levels II–III):** Confirm OEWS wage level on the LCA before relying on FY2028 H-1B registration (Level I odds are modelled near 15%; Level III near 46%). Ask whether the employer can bridge via an offshore entity for 12 months into **L-1B** (with L-2S spousal work rights) or sponsor **O-1A** (`T0-37 L74, L95, L136–L139`).
- **Senior / staff practitioner or researcher without a US offer:** Build an **O-1A** evidence packet (see the [hub criteria checklist](../visa-and-relocation.md#o-1a-the-eight-criteria-for-an-industry-engineer)) to remove lottery friction from employer negotiations, and assess **EB-1A** once you have independent citations, judging, or high-impact system launches — accounting for the 32.2% FY2026 Q3 approval rate and PA-2026-05 (`T0-37 L77–L79, L118–L120`).
- **Recent graduate (US F-1 or Indian university):** US F-1 STEM OPT remains a 3-year runway, but monitor the proposed `$70k/$30k` OPT school fee NPRM (`ICEB-2026-0100`) and target Level II+ roles or cap-exempt research employers (`T0-37 L82, L100, L117`). Graduates outside the US without 1 year at a multinational face low Level I H-1B odds and should compare [Canada](canada.md), [UK/Western Europe](uk-ireland-western-europe.md), or [Singapore/Australia](asia-pacific.md).

## Pitfalls and vendor myths

- **Treating self-reported vendor approval rates as USCIS baselines:** Law firms such as WeGreened advertise 98–99% NIW / 91% EB-1A rates on pre-screened refund cohorts, and Beyond Border quotes "98%" without publishing denominators (`T0-37 L86, L270–L273`, `[C]`, conflict of interest). Compare any retainer against USCIS's FY2026 Q3 32.2% EB-1A and 55.3% NIW baselines (`T0-37 L79`, `[C]`).
- **Assuming the `$100,000` H-1B fee is "permanently struck down":** Prior Phase 0 summaries (`T0-08`) called the fee struck down; in fact, Proclamation 11069 extended it to `2027-09-21`, `Chamber of Commerce v. DHS` upheld it at the district level (D.C. Cir. undecided), and appeals remain live in the First and Ninth Circuits (`T0-37 L75, L296`, `[B]`).
- **Assuming Gold Card skips the India queue:** Form I-140G sits inside existing EB-1 and EB-2 numerical limits and offers no bypass of Indian Visa Bulletin retrogression (`T0-37 L85, L124`, `[B]`).
