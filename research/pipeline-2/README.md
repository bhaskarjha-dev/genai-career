# Research Pipeline 2

Phase 0 researched the domain before this repository existed. Research pipeline 2 (RP2) researches what the repository needs as of November 2026: each session is designed backwards from the pages and data fields it will change, returns graded records in a fixed shape, tests the repository's claims, and is verified before anything is integrated. The owner runs the sessions in a deep-research tool; an agent or the owner integrates them. Decision [D-021](../DECISIONS.md) adopts this design; an independent review on 2026-11-08 shaped its final form (oversized sessions split, one owner per research topic, a stricter record contract).

**Use this page to:** see what is planned and what is done ([tracker](#tracker)); pick the next session ([waves](#waves-and-the-recommended-order)); run one ([how to run a session](#how-to-run-a-session)); and see how results become data and pages ([reduce steps](#reduce-steps-from-sessions-to-data-and-pages)).

## Why a second pipeline

**What Phase 0 was.** Twenty-two sessions, generated by Vivechak v0.1.0 from a project vision in October 2026 ([RESEARCH-PIPELINE.md](../RESEARCH-PIPELINE.md)). They were broad landscape scans ("what is true about role demand, pay, ATS, visas…"), sound for discovery, and they are the evidence behind v0.1.0 to v0.3.0. They could not be designed against outputs that did not exist yet.

**What that left, measured on the data as of 2026-11-08:**

| Area | State of the evidence |
|---|---|
| Role demand | 2 of 29 roles graded B; 18 C, 8 D, 1 model output. Durability and leverage are judgement (D) for all 29 |
| Role pay | 6 of 29 graded B; 9 C, 14 D. India pay: 2 of 24 B, 1 C, 21 D (placeholders for eight roles) |
| Skill tiers | 1,529 role–skill links: 154 measured from job descriptions (10%) and 31 from a posting corpus; 679 rest on session mentions and 665 (43%) are practice-curated |
| Posting measurements | 12 posting titles measured once (JobsPipe, 2026-11-02) |
| Credentials | 20 listed; whole issuer groups absent (frontier labs, GitHub, data platforms other than Databricks, most governance bodies) |
| Countries and visas | 7 corridors; H-1B outside the audit; the status table past expiry |
| Job search | No measured India platforms; no target-employer list; no evaluation of agent or MCP tooling |
| Networking | Most rules practice-curated [P]; how reviewers use LinkedIn or GitHub unmeasured |

Much of the guidance was drafted by agents from the sessions plus web lookups. That is not wrong in itself: the quality of evidence comes from its sources, not from the tool that found them. But many claims were never researched to the depth their importance deserves.

**What is different in November 2026.** The outputs are known: the data files, their schemas and the owner pages (D-019). So every RP2 session states which fields and pages it feeds, carries the repository's claims to test, and returns records that map onto the data. Rankings are not taken from the research agent: it collects graded attributes and gives a provisional top five as a cross-check, and the repository computes orders with published weights, as it already does for roles.

## Design principles

1. **Outcome-first.** Each session names the owner pages and data it feeds ([catalogue.json](catalogue.json), field `feeds`).
2. **Test the repository's claims.** Every prompt lists claims as K1, K2…, rendered from `data/` where possible, and the report gives each a one-word verdict (confirmed, updated, contradicted, stale, unverifiable). Statements an outside agent cannot test (scores, model outputs, what the repository lacks) appear as unnumbered context instead.
3. **Census before ranking.** Sessions gather attributes with grades; scripts compute value orders with stated weights and lenses (Grade M).
4. **Structured return.** A Data Appendix in JSON Lines with fixed record types ([PROTOCOL.md](PROTOCOL.md) §10), using the repository's role and skill IDs, checked by a script before the report is saved.
5. **One shared protocol.** Evidence grades, dates, freshness classes, ethics and the report format live once, in [PROTOCOL.md](PROTOCOL.md), appended to every prompt.
6. **Sized for one deep-research run.** One question family per session: about 15–30 entities, one role, or a small role group. Large catalogues are split (boards, countries, visas, credentials).
7. **One owner per research topic.** Each of the 42 tool categories in `data/tools.json` is re-verified by exactly one session (the script enforces it), and each cross-cutting topic has one owner: ghost jobs, pay transparency and scams (T0-24), the law on AI in hiring (T0-47), AI-training platforms (T0-66). Other sessions cross-check only.
8. **Absences are results.** "Not found after searching X, Y and Z" is a valued finding, and every requested item ends as a record or an absence.
9. **Verify before integrating.** An independent check, preferably by another model family, against thresholds ([VERIFY.md](VERIFY.md)).
10. **Measure where research cannot.** Counting and computing at scale belong to scripts over official and public data ([instruments](#measurement-instruments)), not to an agent's browsing.
11. **Within the law and platform rules.** No personal data about private people; no logging in or bypassing walls to count; automation and contact-data tools are evaluated, not endorsed; legal material stays pointers.
12. **Vivechak-compatible.** Briefs keep Vivechak's eight prompt blocks (DECISION, BRIEF, SCOPE, KNOWN, CALIBRATION, APPROACH, DONE, FORMAT) and its session skeleton.

## What deep research can and cannot do

| Task | Deep research | How RP2 handles it |
|---|---|---|
| Official facts: exam pages, visa rules, statutes, vendor documentation | Good, when it opens the primary page | Grade A only for opened primary pages; verification re-opens them |
| Finding studies, surveys, datasets and their methods | Good | Ledger with access status; snippets capped at B |
| Cataloguing boards, tools, communities, credentials, employers | Good, with gaps | Coverage lists plus seed lists; absences required |
| Sampling 15–30 job descriptions per role | Adequate for aliases, requirement wording and pay bands; too small for share-based tier changes (about ±18 points at n = 30), and biased towards large English-language employers | Sampling method stated; shares used as corroboration only; scripted collection planned ([INS-1](#measurement-instruments)) |
| Counting live postings across platforms | Weak: counts are not comparable or reproducible, and many platforms hide counts behind logins | Same platform and query as the repository's recounts; no logging in; the owner's logged-in counts ([INS-5](#measurement-instruments)) |
| Measuring conversion or what tactics cause | Cannot; it can only find others' measurements | Grades say so; selection effects named |
| Avoiding invented sources | Imperfect | Script checks of structure; independent verification of every load-bearing record |

## Waves and the recommended order

Every session is independent: none needs another's output, so any order works and several can run at once. The waves are a recommendation, by effect on a job search and by what the owner asked for first.

1. **Wave 1, 18 sessions: get hired, plus the two catalogues the owner asked for.** Networking (T0-19 to T0-22), the résumé (T0-23), job search (T0-24 to T0-27), the certification census (T0-28 to T0-34) and the country scan (T0-35, T0-36). Start with the P0 sessions: LinkedIn, referrals, the résumé, both board sessions, employers, the two largest credential groups and both country halves.
2. **Wave 2, 23 sessions: depth for the top targets.** Visa dossiers (T0-37 to T0-42), remote work (T0-43), pay (T0-44, T0-45), interview loops (T0-46), AI-mediated hiring (T0-47) and dossiers for the twelve highest GenAI target roles (T0-48 to T0-59, top four first).
3. **Wave 3, 12 sessions: completeness.** Dossiers for the other 17 roles (three single, five groups), transitions for four new backgrounds, learning resources, skill momentum and emerging titles, the market pulse and onboarding.

**If you run only ten:** T0-19, T0-22, T0-23, T0-24 (or T0-25 if you are job hunting in India), T0-27, T0-46, the dossier for your own target role, and three that are there because the owner asked for them: T0-28, T0-29 and the half of the country scan that holds your targets (T0-35 or T0-36). By the repository's own evidence a certificate is a tie-breaker, so a list chosen purely for getting hired would swap the two credential sessions for pay (T0-44 or T0-45) and remote work (T0-43).

**Timing.** The hyperscaler and developer-tool credential sessions (T0-28, T0-33) are best run after 2026-12-05, once the November and December vendor conferences have announced their changes; `--prompt` warns if you run them earlier.

**Pace.** Run three to five sessions in parallel at most; integration, not research, is the bottleneck. Integrate by reduce step (all seven credential sessions together, for example) rather than one session at a time, and do not let saved sessions wait longer than their freshness class allows.

## How to run a session

```bash
python3 -B scripts/research_pipeline.py --next                         # what to run next
python3 -B scripts/research_pipeline.py --prompt T0-19 > ~/T0-19.md    # assemble the prompt, outside the repository
# Paste ~/T0-19.md into a deep-research tool. Save its answer as ~/T0-19-draft.md.
python3 -B scripts/research_pipeline.py --appendix ~/T0-19-draft.md    # check the report; fix until PROBLEMS: 0
python3 -B scripts/research_pipeline.py --verify ~/T0-19-draft.md > ~/T0-19-verify.md   # independent check (VERIFY.md)
cp ~/T0-19-draft.md research/sessions/T0-19-linkedin-profile-search-content-engagement.md
python3 -B scripts/build_all.py                                         # registers the findings; updates the tracker
```

Then integrate it with [INTEGRATE.md](INTEGRATE.md). Drafts and prompts stay outside the repository (or in the git-ignored `temp/`); only the final session file goes into `research/sessions/`, because saved sessions are never edited.

### Running sessions on several computers

The research itself runs in the browser, so any computer with the repository, Git and Python 3.8 or later can assemble prompts and check drafts; nothing needs installing.

1. **One integration computer, many research computers.** Research computers never commit. They produce checked, verified drafts; the integration computer saves them into `research/sessions/`, runs `build_all.py` and commits. This keeps the generated files (the findings registry, the tracker, `llms.txt`) from conflicting and keeps CI green.
2. **Split by family, not by session.** Give each computer whole families from the tracker (for example networking and the résumé; job search; certifications; countries and visas), so that no session runs twice.
3. **Assemble each prompt on the day you run it:** `--prompt` writes that day's date into the brief.
4. **Hand over** each finished draft through a shared folder, named as the prompt's FORMAT line says (`T0-xx-<slug>.md`), once `--appendix` shows `PROBLEMS: 0` and the verification summary is appended.
5. **Usage limits** of a deep-research product apply per account, not per computer.

On Windows, use Git Bash or WSL, so that `>` writes the prompt file in UTF-8.

**Choosing and using the tool.**

- Use a deep-research mode with live web access and citations, for example Claude's Research, ChatGPT's deep research, Gemini Deep Research or Perplexity's deep research. One session per fresh chat.
- Paste the whole prompt in one message. Do not feed instructions in several turns: the prompt front-loads everything on purpose.
- If the tool asks clarifying questions, reply: "Proceed with your stated assumptions."
- If the output stops early, reply: "Continue from the last complete line." If the Data Appendix is missing or cut, reply: "Now output only the Data Appendix." Add no new instructions.
- The checker tolerates a front matter wrapped in a code fence or indented, Windows line ends and a byte-order mark; anything else it reports.
- **Conflicts of interest.** Run the frontier-lab census (T0-29) in a product whose developer's credentials are not all in scope (for example Perplexity), or split the run so that no lab's credentials are researched with its own product, and verify with another model family either way. The same applies whenever a session studies the tool vendor's own products.
- **Without the script:** a brief can be read on GitHub, but its placeholders (the role cards, tool lists, credential lists and claims) are filled from `data/` and the catalogue only by the script; use the script.
- **With Vivechak:** the prompts follow Vivechak's eight blocks, but the Vivechak server builds its session graph only from the Phase 0 [RESEARCH-PIPELINE.md](../RESEARCH-PIPELINE.md), so RP2 runs outside it. Vivechak's own session validation may still be run on a saved report; this pipeline does not depend on it.

## Reduce steps: from sessions to data and pages

A reduce step runs when the sessions it needs are saved (R3 can run role by role). The tracker shows each step's progress. The integration rules for every record type are in [INTEGRATE.md](INTEGRATE.md) §2.

| Step | Turns | Into |
|---|---|---|
| R1 Credentials | T0-28 to T0-34 | A credential data file with a schema (or an extended ontology), a value-order script, a generated credential catalogue; updated credentials by role and credential ROI |
| R2 Geography | T0-35 to T0-45 | A country data file with a schema, a ranking script, a generated country-ranking page; the visa page's corridors and status table rewritten; the India remote playbook updated |
| R3 Roles | T0-48 to T0-66 | Role scores and their India lens, title aliases, skill links with job-description evidence; ranks, the target order and the hubs regenerate |
| R4 Networking | T0-19 to T0-22 | The networking guides and proof-of-work standards; tools; a dedicated LinkedIn guide if the evidence supports one |
| R5 Résumé | T0-23 | The résumé model and screening facts; the skeletons and the regional table; the résumé generator's inputs |
| R6 Job search | T0-24 to T0-27 | Channels and boards (India measured), the application operating system, the employer landscape; tools; a target-employer list |
| R7 Pay | T0-44, T0-45 | Pay benchmarks, the negotiation playbook, offer evaluation; the pay pillars |
| R8 Interviewing | T0-46, T0-47 | Interview loops, coding rounds, system-design archetypes with model prices, ATS mechanics; practice prompts |
| R9 Tracks and the rest | T0-67 to T0-71 | Researched routes for four new backgrounds, learning resources, a measured surging ring for the skills radar and emerging-title verdicts, the market overview, onboarding |

**R1 method: the credential value order** (proposed; the decision record at R1 fixes it). Each credential gets six criteria, each scored 0–10 with a low–mid–high range and a grade, like the role pillars:

| Criterion | Weight | Why this weight |
|---|:---:|---|
| Hiring signal: share of GenAI postings naming it, by role family and region, measured by the job-description collector (INS-1) | 0.30 | The most direct evidence that employers value it. Where it is unmeasured, the criterion is left out and the other weights re-normalised, never filled from small samples |
| Channel value: partner-programme counts, employer targets and bonuses, client-contract clauses | 0.20 | Where certificates pay off: services firms, partners and GCCs (T0-11) |
| Rigour and verifiability: proctoring, hands-on format, public verification, renewal | 0.15 | How much a reviewer can trust the signal |
| Skill alignment: exam domains against the role family's Must and Important skills (computed) | 0.15 | Whether preparing for it builds what the role needs |
| Currency: launch or update date, retirement risk | 0.10 | Avoids credentials that are about to vanish |
| Cost to earn: fee plus preparation hours (inverted) | 0.10 | Matters, but less than whether it is worth having |

Lenses: one order per role family; one per hiring channel (product companies and labs; services, partners and GCCs; governance); and a **cost-ordered collector's list** across all credentials (value per hour and fee, with cumulative fees and hours) for readers who want to take many. Monte Carlo over the ranges gives rank intervals, as for roles. The credential ROI guide keeps its decision rule (a tie-breaker, three conditions, a stop-loss): the order answers "which one", not "whether".

**R2 method: the country and city ranking** (proposed; fixed by a decision record at R2). Pillars per country, each 0–10 with a range, a grade and a basis: GenAI demand; pay (AI and ML engineers, with purchasing-power parity); net pay after tax and rent at reference salaries (computed by INS-4, with the sessions' figures as a cross-check); access (routes, thresholds, processing, quotas or lotteries, path to settlement), scored separately for an Indian national and for a generic non-EU national; remote reach (cross-border remote share, time-zone overlap); ecosystem (labs, employers, funding); stability (policy volatility). Lenses: highest net pay; fastest legal access for an Indian national; remote from India; long-term settlement; early career. Cities take their country's access and add their own demand, pay and rent. The ranking is a model output (Grade M), and every visa fact stays a pointer to its official source.

## Measurement instruments

Deep research samples; these measure. They are planned builds (ROADMAP §4), specified here so the sessions collect what they need.

- **INS-1 Job-description collector** (planned `scripts/collect_jds.py`, standard library only). Reads postings from employers' own boards through the public job-board endpoints that Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby and similar vendors document, for the employer list from T0-27 (its records carry each employer's applicant-tracking vendor and board URL). Classifies titles with `data/role_titles.json`, extracts skills with the ontology's aliases and posted pay ranges, and writes a dated snapshot with n per title to `data/posting_skill_shares.json`; raw postings stay outside the repository. It respects each vendor's terms and rate limits, never scrapes sites whose terms forbid it, and measures its own keyword recall on a hand-labelled sample (target ≥ 95%, ROADMAP P1-1). It turns the dossiers' samples into repeatable monthly measurement, and it supplies R1's hiring signal. Build it before wave 2 if you can.
- **INS-2 Official registers.** US Department of Labor LCA disclosure files and the USCIS H-1B Employer Data Hub; the UK register of licensed sponsors; the Netherlands IND register of recognised sponsors; Canada's positive LMIA employer lists. A script can count filings by employer, city and AI job title and the filed wage ranges (A for filings, never for pay): sponsorship signals for R2 and R6.
- **INS-3 Posting recount.** The existing T0-01 recount protocol on JobsPipe, Fast AI Jobs, Skillenai and Go Fractional, monthly (class V1), as scheduled in ROADMAP §5. T0-70 is its quarterly, wider counterpart.
- **INS-4 Net-pay calculator** (planned). Effective income tax and employee social contributions at reference salaries for each country in the scan, from official schedules (or OECD Taxing Wages parameters where they cover the salary), including the special regimes for incoming workers. Grade M on A inputs; R2's net-pay pillar.
- **INS-5 Logged-in platform counts.** Some platforms (for example Naukri, Instahyre and LinkedIn) show counts only to signed-in users, and research agents must not log in. The owner runs a fixed list of queries by hand each month, signed in to their own accounts, and records the dated counts in a small data table (Grade B). Never automated against platform terms.

## Limits and risks

- **Research is not measurement.** Agents cannot count live postings reproducibly or measure what causes a hire. RP2 labels such claims honestly and moves counting to instruments; conversion stays as measured by others (vendor datasets, selection effects named).
- **Networking advice is mostly lore.** Expect many C, D and P grades for LinkedIn and X tactics even after research; the platforms' own documentation is the strongest evidence. T0-22's yield-per-hour comparison tests, rather than assumes, that networking is the highest-yield activity.
- **A single "most to least valuable" certification list would mislead.** For most engineering roles a certificate is a tie-breaker whose value depends on the hiring channel (T0-11). R1 therefore gives orders per role family and channel, plus a cost-ordered collector's list, beside the stop rule.
- **Country rankings are personal.** Nationality, qualifications, family and career stage change the answer, and visa rules change monthly. The ranking is a model with lenses; legal facts remain pointers to official sources, not advice.
- **Seeds can be wrong.** Credentials named from memory or other research may not exist under those names, and reported exam codes may differ; the sessions confirm each or record an absence.
- **Research agents can invent sources.** The structure check and independent verification reduce, not remove, the risk; data-changing sessions get the required check.
- **Conflicts of interest.** Model developers researching their own credentials or tools must declare it; verification by another model family offsets it.
- **Integration is the bottleneck.** Fifty-three sessions need careful integration; running faster than integrating wastes their freshness.
- **Law and platform terms.** Contact-data products, scraping and auto-apply tools carry legal and terms-of-service risk; the sessions evaluate them, and the repository does not endorse breaking platform rules.
