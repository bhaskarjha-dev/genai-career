## DECISION

What should a new hire in a GenAI role do in the first 90 days and the first two years to pass probation, deliver visible results and progress, and what does the evidence say about promotion, retention, performance expectations and burnout in AI roles at the research date? This informs decisions {{decisions}} and these outputs of the knowledge base: {{feeds}}. Door: two-way. What would change the advice: published probation or performance rubrics for AI roles; data on promotion speed or attrition in AI roles; evidence on workload and burnout.

## BRIEF

The knowledge base's onboarding and growth guidance rests on Phase 0 research from October 2026: role clarity linked to newcomer adjustment in a meta-analysis, RAND's root causes of AI project failure, a regression-evaluation suite and a measured cost cut as the two quick wins with independent support, no public GenAI probation rubric, promotion pay and timing from compensation datasets, and no research on burnout. The aim is to refresh and extend it: published performance expectations and ladders for AI roles, evidence on probation and early attrition, how AI coding tools changed expectations of engineers, promotion speed, and sustainable pace. Question type: evaluative. Audience: people starting GenAI roles at labs, Big Tech, startups, enterprises, GCCs and services firms, including India.

## SCOPE
