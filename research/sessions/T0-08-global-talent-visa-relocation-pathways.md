**As of 21 October 2026.** This is a research audit, not legal advice. Several routes are changing right now (flagged in *Open Questions & Risks*). Every claim carries an evidence grade and a ledger ID such as [US-01].

## Research Question

For an Indian GenAI/AI engineer **without an academic PhD**, what are the verified evidentiary requirements and approval criteria, and the realistic preparation timelines and costs, for six talent routes in October 2026: US O-1A, UK Global Talent, Canada Global Talent Stream (GTS), EU Blue Card (Germany, France), Singapore ONE Pass/COMPASS, and UAE Golden Visa? Is the claim that O-1A is limited to academics with dozens of citations true? Sub-questions: (1) how do the eight O-1A criteria map to industry evidence; (2) what are the track records, fees and vetting of Legalpad, Unshackled, Fragomen and similar providers; (3) how does GTS's two-week process work; (4) what evidence do UK endorsers need and what is the Exceptional Promise pass rate; (5) what are the 2026 Blue Card floors, language rules and PR timelines in Germany and France; (6) how do the UAE and Singapore compare on earning thresholds, consulting flexibility and tax; (7) how does O-1A differ from employer-tied statuses on cost, timeline and sponsor flexibility.

## Key Findings

**1. The KNOWN claim is false as a matter of USCIS policy.** O-1A requires "sustained national or international acclaim", shown by a major award or at least three of eight evidentiary criteria, then weighed in a separate totality ("final merits") step. The Policy Manual sets no degree, PhD, citation-count or academic-employment prerequisite. Journal rankings, citation totals/h-index, elite-institution employment, invited talks and US-government grants appear only as optional totality factors; the manual says a case does not need them and that citations matter less early in a career. Authorship of even one qualifying article satisfies criterion 6 at the evidence step. Industry occupations are accommodated through "comparable evidence" (for example a major trade-show presentation in place of scholarly articles, or highly valued equity in place of salary for founders). **Grade A** [US-01, US-03]. Caveat: the cached chapter I could read predates the January 2025 revision that added AI/"critical and emerging technology" examples [US-02].

**2. What fails is the quality of proof, not the lack of credentials.** Nine O-1 appeal decisions (May 2025 to June 2026) summarised by a practitioner turn on: evidence that names the company rather than the person (funding or accelerator acceptance offered as "awards"; press that mentions a founder in passing); judging claimed from invitations rather than completed reviews; an equity claim rejected because the company's own fair-market value ($0.00001 a share) contradicted the claimed value and the secondary-market document disclaimed reliance; reference letters that assert rather than explain; and "adoption by one or a few companies" offered as major significance. **Grade A** for the two-step framework [US-01]; **Grade C** for the decision patterns [US-10] (the AAO PDFs on uscis.gov blocked automated retrieval, so they were not read directly).

**3. O-category approvals are ~91% but drifting down, and USCIS has slowed.** USCIS publishes only the combined I-129 "O" class (O-1 plus O-2); no O-1A-specific rate exists. FY2025: 93.9% (29,733 approved, 1,948 denied). FY2026 Q1-Q3: 91.0% (21,321 approved, 2,096 denied), so denials through Q3 already exceed all of FY2025. RFE share was reported near 23% in Q3 FY2026 against about 19% a year earlier. Pending I-129 petitions rose 175.6% between FY2023 Q2 and FY2025 Q4, and one tracker reports 12.5 months to finish 80% of O petitions (July 2026), although a vendor page cites only about 4-6 months for standard processing, so the true wait is unresolved. Premium processing (15 business days; $2,965 since 1 March 2026) is the only option with a published clock. **Grade B** for the approval counts, rates and RFE share (vendor pages citing USCIS I-129 data, re-checked 22 October 2026; USCIS's own tables were not opened) and for the premium fee [US-07, US-08]; **Grade C** for the 175.6% pending-petition rise and the 12.5-month figure [US-09].

**4. US cost benchmark.** Government fees for an employer with more than 25 employees: $1,055 (I-129, O class) + $600 (Asylum Program Fee) = $1,655, or $4,620 with premium processing; a small employer pays $530 + $300 = $830, or $3,795 with premium. Specialist attorney fees are quoted at roughly $5k-15k; an advisory opinion at $250-500; all-in budgets at $10k-25k; the consular MRV fee is $205. **Grade B** (government fees) / **Grade C** (attorney and all-in figures) [US-06, US-07, US-15].

**5. The brief's provider list needs correction.** Legalpad was acquired by Deel in August 2022 and its visa services now sit inside Deel's immigration organisation. Unshackled Ventures is a pre-seed venture fund for immigrant founders (since 2014) that points founders to an immigration partner; it is not a petition-preparation firm. Fragomen processes O-1s mainly for institutional and corporate sponsors (for example the University of Florida's pipeline: 1-2 weeks internal review plus 2-3 weeks drafting) and publishes no individual fee schedule. Specialist price lists exist (Manifest Law from $6,999 up to $12,500 plans; about $8k at Beyond Border) but every approval-rate claim found (for example Deel's "98%") is self-reported. **Grade B** (corporate facts) / **Grade C** (claims) [US-11 to US-16].

**6. O-1A is a bridge, not a green card.** O-1 permits dual intent by regulation, but permanent residence needs a separate petition. In the October 2026 Visa Bulletin the EB-1 India final-action date is 1 February 2023 (about 3.7 years behind) and EB-2 India reopened at 1 November 2013 after being unavailable in September. The reported EB-1A approval rate fell from 74.7% in Q1 FY2025 (66.9% for FY2025 as a whole) to 32.2% in Q3 FY2026 (40.7% for FY2026 Q1-Q3). Consular risk is real: practitioners report a "spate" of INA 214(b) refusals of USCIS-approved O-1s (June 2026), and petition-based appointment waits in India run roughly 1 to 5.5 months by post. **Grade B/C** [US-05, US-08, US-17, US-19, US-20].

**7. UK Global Talent is the only no-employer route, but no Exceptional Promise pass rate is published.** Stage 1 endorsement by Tech Nation (one mandatory plus two optional criteria; three dated letters; up to ten evidence items) then Stage 2 visa. Fees: £561 + £205 = £766, plus the Immigration Health Surcharge (IHS) of £1,035 a year. Visa decision within 3 weeks from outside the UK. ILR after 3 years (Exceptional Talent) or 5 years (Exceptional Promise), subject to UK earnings in the field, B1 English under the August 2026 guidance and the Life in the UK test. One law-firm page (law stated 24 September 2026) reports that Statement of Changes HC 1691 raises the English requirement for settlement to B2 for applications from 26 March 2027 on many routes including Global Talent, so plan for B2; the same page says the 10-year "earned settlement" baseline is not yet in law and the government had not published its consultation response. All-field endorsement success was about 72% (April 2020 to April 2023, secondary) and 54-65% for digital technology (historical, secondary); no Promise-specific figure was found. **Grade A** (process, fees, 3-vs-5-year ILR rule and B1 requirement, re-read 22 Oct 2026) / **Grade B/C** (earned-settlement status; B2 change) / **Grade C** (pass rates) [UK-01, UK-02, UK-04, UK-06, UK-08].

**8. Canada GTS is employer-driven and wage-floored; "two weeks" is an aim.** Category B covers NOC 21231, 21232, 21211, 21230 and other tech occupations at the prevailing wage (the highest of the Job Bank regional median or the employer's own pay range for similar staff). LMIA fee C$1,000 per position; a Labour Market Benefits Plan with annual progress reviews. IRCC "aims" for two-week permits for complete online applications from outside Canada. Toronto-region median for NOC 21231 is C$56.49/hour (about C$117.5k a year); British Columbia is C$62.50/hour (about C$130k). September 2026 changes: a formal "employer" test (staffing agencies cannot be employers) and a 29% cut to the Category A referral-partner list. Canada's announced H-1B pathway had not launched as of September 2026. **Grade A** for programme rules, fee and benefits plan (ESDC page re-read 22 Oct 2026) and the two-week aim [CA-01, CA-02]; **Grade B** for the Job Bank wage figures (excerpts of official pages), the referral-list cut and the Budget 2025 announcement [CA-03 to CA-05]; **Grade B/C** for the status of the H-1B pathway [CA-06]. The 10-business-day LMIA figure is not on the ESDC page and rests on secondary sources [CA-07].

**9. Germany is the lowest-ambiguity route.** 2026 floors: €50,700 (general) and €45,934.20 (shortage occupations including ICT, graduates within three years, and ICT specialists without a degree); contract of at least 6 months; ICT specialists without a degree need 3 years' experience within the last 7; Federal Employment Agency consent applies below €50,700; permanent settlement after 27 months (A1 German) or 21 months (B1). Employer fast-track (§81a AufenthG) costs €411 with about 2-4 weeks pre-approval, then up to 3 weeks to an embassy appointment and up to 3 weeks to a decision. **Grade A** for the thresholds, the ICT-without-degree test, the 6-month contract, the consent threshold, 4-year validity and the €100 card fee (Berlin immigration office page re-read 22 Oct 2026) [DE-02]; **Grade B** for the pension-ceiling percentages, the §81a timings and fee, and the 21/27-month settlement periods, which rest on excerpts of official pages [DE-01, DE-03 to DE-05].

**10. France costs more and PR needs French.** EU Blue Card salary floor is €59,373 (1.5 × the €39,582 reference salary, in force since 21 August 2025, up from €53,836.50); contract of at least 6 months per summaries of Decree 2026-308 (the France-Visas page re-read on 22 Oct 2026 still says at least one year; unresolved); a 3-year higher-education degree or 5 years' experience; no French at entry. EU long-term residence after 5 years of EU residence (the last 2 in France for Blue Card holders) requires B1 French and a civic exam since 1 January 2026. One site's claim of a 1.2× shortage-occupation threshold is unverified, and the ministerial list of shortage occupations had not been published when last reported. **Grade A** for the €59,373 floor and the degree/experience rule as stated on France-Visas (re-read 22 Oct 2026) [FR-01]; **Grade B** for the reference-salary history, Decree 2026-308 and the B1 French rule [FR-02 to FR-04]; Grade C for the 1.2× claim [FR-06].

**11. Singapore: ONE Pass is a high-earner pass; an AI & Tech track arrives on 28 January 2027.** Today: S$30,000 fixed monthly salary for 12 consecutive months from one employer that is an "established company" (market capitalisation of at least US$500m or revenue of at least US$200m), or a Singapore offer meeting the same tests. The achievements route covers sports, arts and culture, and academia and research only. From 28 January 2027: at least S$22,500 fixed plus vested equity to reach S$30,000, at least 5 cumulative years in the last 10 as founder, C-suite or senior technical role, and an employer meeting valuation/revenue/assets-under-management thresholds (or, for start-ups, US$30m raised). The Employment Pass alternative: S$5,600 now, S$6,000 for new applications from 1 January 2027, plus 40 COMPASS points (exempt at S$22,500 fixed). ONE Pass holders numbered 8,500 at December 2025, and their median monthly salary at application was S$36,000 (25th percentile S$31,900). **Grade A** [SG-01 to SG-03, SG-06, SG-07]; **Grade B** for fees and processing time [SG-04].

**12. UAE: salary-gated rather than talent-gated, with no personal income tax on salary.** Golden Visa "skilled professional": employed in the UAE, MOHRE occupational level 1 or 2, bachelor's degree, at least AED 30,000 a month (Dubai is reported to require basic salary excluding allowances and longer payment history). Specialist and "exceptional talent" routes rely on an attested PhD in priority fields or a government nomination. The Federal Authority (ICP) publicly denied rumours of "lifetime" Golden Visas in July 2025. Employment income is untaxed; 9% corporate tax applies to licensed business or freelance income above AED 375,000. **Grade A** (skilled-professional criteria; ADDED page re-read 22 Oct 2026) / **Grade B** (tax, denial, Dubai tightening) [AE-01 to AE-05].

**Confidence by route (analysis).** US policy framework: high. US outcome statistics: medium. UK process, fees and ILR: high; UK pass rates: low. Canada GTS: high. Germany: high. France: medium (floor verified; the sources conflict on minimum contract length). Singapore ONE Pass/EP: high. UAE: medium.

## Recommendation

This is a conditional decision framework, not a prediction of any individual outcome; confirm with licensed counsel in the relevant country.

**Bottom line.**
(1) Treat O-1A as a credible but evidence-intensive route that is **not** academic-only: plan 12-18 months of external-validation work before filing and a budget of roughly US$10k-25k all-in, and treat the status as a temporary bridge unless EB-1/EB-2 timing improves.
(2) For nearer-term, lower-ambiguity relocation use an employer-offer route (Germany Blue Card; Canada GTS if a Canadian employer is ready) or, if you want no employer tie, UK Global Talent.
(3) Treat Singapore ONE Pass and the UAE Golden Visa as compensation-gated: use them only if the salary test is already met (or, for Singapore, wait for the AI & Tech track after 28 January 2027).

## Detailed Findings

### 1. United States: O-1A

#### 1.1 Standard and two-step test [US-01, US-03]
O-1A covers the sciences, education, business and athletics. "Extraordinary ability" means being among the small percentage at the very top of the field. The petition needs a major internationally recognised award or at least three of eight criteria. The officer first checks whether the evidence meets the regulatory wording of three criteria (no judgement about acclaim at that step), then weighs everything together to decide whether the beneficiary has sustained acclaim at the very top of the field. Meeting three criteria "does not necessarily" establish eligibility. The burden is a preponderance of the evidence (Matter of Chawathe, cited in the manual). Work must continue in the beneficiary's "area of extraordinary ability", which USCIS reads broadly to include occupations sharing skills and knowledge (it names an academic moving into a private company as an example). Grade A.

#### 1.2 The eight criteria mapped to non-PhD industry evidence
The "evidence" column is my mapping (analysis) built from the criteria text [US-01] and practitioner summaries; the last column draws on [US-10] (Grade C).

**Which three?** On paper the cheapest combination for an industry engineer is 7 + 8 + 4 (role at a distinguished employer, a high-pay offer, completed judging). Criteria 5 and 3 do most of the work in the final-merits step because they show acclaim outside your employer; criterion 6 is a low-barrier option for anyone with even one qualifying publication; criteria 1 and 2 are rarely buildable on demand (practitioner view [US-10]). This is analysis, not a sourced approval-probability.

#### 1.3 Totality factors and comparable evidence [US-01, US-02, US-04]
- Officers may weigh any relevant evidence in the totality step, including: publications in highly ranked journals, high citation totals or h-index, employment at leading institutions, unsolicited invitations to speak at recognised conferences, and named roles on competitively funded US-government research grants. Footnotes state that none is required and that citation counts matter less early in a career.
- Comparable evidence is allowed criterion by criterion once you explain why a listed criterion does not readily apply to your occupation; you must still meet three separate criteria. Examples in the manual: a major trade-show presentation instead of scholarly articles; highly valued startup equity instead of salary.
- The January 2025 update (PA-2025-02) added examples for "critical and emerging technologies" (AI named in the background), a letter from an interested US-government agency as totality evidence, clearer petitioning by an entity you own, and rules for when an extension is limited to one year. The executive order that prompted it was rescinded on 20 January 2025, but the manual updates remained in effect as of 4 February 2025 [US-04]. I found no later rollback but did not retrieve the live post-2025 chapter. One vendor claims open-source contributions and podcasts are "expressly included" [US-23]; I could not confirm that wording.

#### 1.4 Petition mechanics and sponsor flexibility [US-03, US-04, US-05, US-11]
- Who may file: a U.S. employer, a U.S. agent, or a foreign employer through a U.S. agent; a separate legal entity you own may petition, but you cannot petition on your own as an individual.
- Required: Form I-129; evidence for three criteria (or a major award); a written advisory opinion from a peer group or expert (if no peer group exists, USCIS decides on the other evidence); the contract or a summary of the oral agreement; an itinerary or description of events. File no more than one year before the need and ideally at least 45 days before.
- Validity: initial grant up to three years; extensions are described as one-year increments on the USCIS page, while AILA reports up to three years where the event or activity changes. Work is limited to the petitioned engagement; a new employer needs a new I-129, and material changes need an amendment. A university guide notes a 60-day grace period after termination and no payment for outside activity. O-3 dependants cannot work (study is allowed).
- Dual intent: 8 CFR 214.2(o)(13) and the State Department's Foreign Affairs Manual say a filed or approved immigrant petition cannot be a basis to deny O-1 classification. A university page cautions otherwise, but the regulation text governs [US-05, US-11].

### 2. United Kingdom: Global Talent (digital technology)

#### 2.1 Process, fees, timing [UK-01, UK-02, UK-05] Grade A
- Two stages: Stage 1 endorsement (one GOV.UK form since 4 Aug 2025, passed to Tech Nation [UK-07], Grade C); Stage 2 visa within 3 months of the endorsement letter.
- Fees: £561 (endorsement) + £205 (visa) = £766; dependants £766 each; IHS usually £1,035 a year (£776 for under-18s per the fee summary [UK-05]). Stale pages still show £524 or £456; use the GOV.UK figures.
- Decisions: Stage 2 usually within 3 weeks from outside the UK (8 weeks inside). Endorsement takes about 5-8 weeks per secondary sources [UK-11] (Grade C). If refused, a review request must be filed within 28 calendar days.
- No job offer and no English test at entry. Grants run 1-5 years; extension requires UK earnings in the endorsed field; ILR requires B1 English and Life in the UK.

#### 2.2 The rubric: Exceptional Talent versus Exceptional Promise [UK-04, UK-09, UK-10] Grade B/C
The GOV.UK applicant guidance page for digital technology was not retrievable; the criteria below come from a law-firm reproduction of the guidance. Wording and numbering vary slightly between sources.

| | Exceptional Talent | Exceptional Promise |
|---|---|---|
| Mandatory (1) | Recognised by others in the last 5 years as a **leading talent** in digital technology | Recognised in the last 5 years as having the **potential** to be a leading talent |
| Optional (any 2 of 4) | Innovation: proven track record as a founder or senior executive of a product-led company, or as an employee in a new digital field or concept; contributions outside day-to-day work (for example mentoring, collaborations, large industry initiatives); significant technical, commercial or entrepreneurial contributions as founder, senior executive, board member or employee of a product-led company; academic contributions or research published or endorsed by an expert | Same four areas with a lower bar: at least one example of innovation as a founder or employee in a new field; significant contributions as a founder or employee (no senior-executive requirement) |
| Typical career stage | 5+ years, already leading | Under 5 years, early in career; cannot later switch to Talent [UK-09] |
| Settlement | 3 years | 5 years [UK-02] |

"Product-led digital technology company" is described as one whose primary revenue comes from a proprietary digital product, service, platform or hardware [UK-10] (Grade C). **Analysis:** engineers whose experience is mainly at services or outsourcing firms may find the optional criteria harder to evidence.

**Evidence checklist for a GenAI engineer (analysis built on the rubric).**
- *Mandatory:* speaking invitations, awards, press naming you, independent adoption of your open-source or product work, judging, hiring-bar signals in letters.
- *Optional:* product launches with usage metrics and your role; mentoring, community, hackathon or standards work; revenue or scale impact at a product company with a CTO letter; publications or preprints endorsed by an expert.
- *Format rules (Home Office guidance):* 3 dated letters from 3 different well-established organisations acknowledged as experts in digital technology; a CV; up to 10 evidence items; no objects, DVDs, CDs, digital files or web links (use a scanned page with the link visible); evidence of any digital technology businesses in the last 5 years [UK-02] (Grade A). Law-firm summaries say each writer must have known your work for at least 12 months [UK-04].
- *Common refusal reasoning reported:* letters from managers or colleagues alone are insufficient to show leading talent [UK-12] (Grade C).

### 3. Canada: Global Talent Stream

#### 3.1 Mechanics [CA-01, CA-02] Grade A
- GTS is an LMIA stream run by ESDC; the employer applies, the worker cannot. Category A needs a designated referral partner plus a "unique and specialized" position (wage floor C$38.46/hour or C$80,000 for the first two positions per calendar year, C$72.11/hour or C$150,000 beyond that; advanced degree and/or 5 years' experience). Category B needs no referral: the job must be on the Global Talent Occupations List (updated December 2022). Relevant NOC 2021 entries include 21211 data scientists, 21230 computer systems developers and programmers, 21231 software engineers and designers, 21232 software developers and programmers, 21220 cybersecurity specialists and 20012 computer and information systems managers, all at prevailing wage.
- Prevailing wage = the highest of the Job Bank regional median hourly wage or the pay range for current employees with the same job and skills (plus the Category A floors). Only guaranteed wages count (no bonuses, tips, overtime or profit sharing). Employers must review wages yearly.
- Labour Market Benefits Plan (LMBP): Category B employers commit to skills and training investment as the mandatory benefit plus at least two complementary benefits; ESDC runs annual progress reviews, and failure to make reasonable efforts likely means negative GTS decisions for two years.
- Fee: C$1,000 per position, non-refundable.
- Work permit: IRCC "aims" to process eligible applications within two weeks if the worker applies online from outside Canada with a complete application and holds a positive GTS LMIA; spouses and children applying at the same time are included [CA-02, CA-08]. Reported service standards (ESDC about 10 business days for the LMIA; two weeks for 80% of eligible permit applications) are secondary-sourced [CA-07] (Grade B/C); the ESDC requirements page, re-read on 22 Oct 2026, states no business-day service standard.

### 5. Singapore

#### 5.1 ONE Pass (as of 14 Sep 2026) [SG-01, SG-02] Grade A
- **Salary route:** at least S$30,000 fixed monthly salary for the 12 consecutive months before applying, from **one** employer (salaries from two employers cannot be added), at an established company (market cap at least US$500m or annual revenue at least US$200m, with global office figures considered), or a Singapore role at such a company starting within 6 months. Existing pass holders need one year in Singapore.
- **Achievements route:** sports, arts and culture, academia and research (agencies such as MCCY, MOE, NRF and A*STAR review holistically). Technology is not listed on this route today.
- **AI & Tech track (from 28 Jan 2027):** at least S$30,000 a month for 12 consecutive months, with fixed salary at least S$22,500 and the remainder possibly vested ESOP/ESOW; at least 5 cumulative years in the last 10 as founder, C-suite or a technical role (for example senior software engineer); employment in a tech company, tech division or tech VC firm with valuation or market cap of at least US$500m, revenue of at least US$200m, assets under management of at least US$500m, or (start-ups only) at least US$30m raised.
- **Flexibility:** exempt from COMPASS and job-advertising rules; may start, operate and work for multiple companies at once; no need to reapply when changing jobs; family passes available; spouse may apply for a Letter of Consent; annual income and activity declaration due by 31 January.
- **Validity and renewal:** up to 5 years, renewable if you averaged S$30,000 fixed over 5 years in Singapore or run a Singapore company employing at least 5 locals who each earn at least the EP minimum qualifying salary.
- **Uptake:** about 3,600 holders (Dec 2023), 6,300 (Dec 2024) and 8,500 (Dec 2025); about 70% work in financial and insurance services, information and communication, and professional services [SG-06] (MOM reply to PQ 2699, 5 Aug 2026; Grade A, re-checked 22 Oct 2026). A second MOM reply (PQ 1882, 8 Sep 2026) gives holders' monthly salaries at the point of application, as at December 2025: 25th percentile S$31,900, median S$36,000, 75th percentile S$44,500 [SG-07] (Grade A). Analysis: the S$30,000 floor therefore sits below the 25th percentile of actual holders.
- **Cost and speed:** S$105 application + S$225 issuance; most cases within about four weeks [SG-04] (Grade B; immigration-firm summary).

#### 5.2 Employment Pass and COMPASS [SG-03] Grade A
- Stage 1 qualifying salary (all sectors except financial services): S$5,600 now, rising with age to S$10,700 at 45 or older; from 1 Jan 2027 S$6,000, up to S$11,500 (financial services S$6,200/S$11,800 now; S$6,600/S$12,700 in 2027).
- Stage 2 COMPASS needs 40 points: C1 salary percentile (20/10/0), C2 qualifications (20 for top-tier institutions, 10 for other degree-equivalents), C3 diversity (firms with fewer than 25 PMETs score 10 by default; otherwise 20 if the candidate's nationality is under 5% of the firm's PMETs, 10 for 5% to under 25%, 0 at 25% or more), C4 support for local employment (10 by default for firms under 25 PMETs; otherwise 20/10/0 by local-employment percentile, with at least 10 for firms with 70% or more local PMETs), C5 Shortage Occupation List bonus (20, or 10 if the candidate's nationality share is one-third or more), C6 strategic economic priorities (10). Exempt if fixed monthly salary is at least S$22,500 (also exempt: overseas intra-corporate transferees and roles of one month or less). Page last updated 28 April 2026; re-read 22 Oct 2026.
- **Analysis:** because C3 scores by the candidate's nationality share inside the hiring firm, an Indian candidate scores 0 on C3 where Indian nationals are 25% or more of the firm's PMETs; I did not find data on how often this binds.

#### 5.3 Tax [SG-05, SG-01] Grade A/B
Non-residents are taxed on employment income at the higher of 15% or the progressive resident rates; employment of 60 days or less in a year is exempt; 183 days in a calendar year generally makes you a resident (with a two-year concession). Resident rates are progressive (0% to 24% in secondary summaries). MOM notes ONE Pass holders may need to pay income tax.

*Evidence-ledger template:* Criterion | Item | Names me? | Independent source? | Date | Document location | Status.

#### 8.2 UK Global Talent
Weeks 0-2: choose Talent or Promise (Promise cannot be upgraded later); line up three independent referees; choose at most 10 evidence items. Weeks 2-10: assemble a scanned, link-visible evidence pack and submit the single GOV.UK Stage 1 form. Then 5-8 weeks for the decision, 28 days to request a review if refused, Stage 2 within 3 months of the letter, visa within about 3 weeks abroad.

#### 8.3 Canada, Germany, Singapore, UAE
Canada: the employer identifies a Category B NOC, sets pay at or above the Job Bank median, drafts the LMBP and files the LMIA; the worker then files online from outside Canada with biometrics. Germany: confirm degree recognition or the 3-of-7-year ICT test, agree the §81a fast-track with the employer, book the embassy appointment, then plan PR (pension contributions; B1 German for the 21-month route). Singapore: confirm fixed-salary history and employer eligibility; decide between filing a ONE Pass now, waiting for the AI & Tech track on 28 January 2027, or using an Employment Pass. UAE: obtain an offer with at least AED 30,000 basic and the right MOHRE level; collect the salary certificate and bank statements.

**Expert objections and what the evidence says.**
1. *"A 91% approval rate is meaningless because of selection bias."* Agreed: the O class mixes O-1A, O-1B, O-2 and extensions and excludes cases screened out by attorneys [US-10]; I could not estimate a conditional probability for a typical non-PhD engineer.
2. *"Final merits is where industry engineers lose."* The policy is two-step by design [US-01] and the AAO patterns support it [US-10, Grade C]; no frequency data exist.
3. *"Critical role and high salary are commodity criteria."* The manual treats them as ordinary criteria, but AAO reasoning shows title or pay alone is not enough; no data show how often officers accept them [US-01, US-10].
4. *"O-1A has no green-card path."* Correct; see Key Finding 6.
5. *"Tech Nation's pass rate for Promise is unknowable."* True from public data; the best proxies are all-field and digital-technology averages (Grade C) [UK-06].
6. *"Canada's two-week claim is marketing."* The official wording is "aim"; only a target is stated [CA-02], and one practitioner source says the current permit target is slower than two weeks [CA-07].
7. *"Germany's route depends on classification."* The ICT-without-degree exception depends on ISCO-08 group 133 or 25 and on the 3-of-7-year test [DE-02]; misclassification would defeat it.
8. *"UAE and Singapore passes reward pay, not talent."* Supported: the verified tests are salary thresholds, with achievements routes limited to specified fields and nomination [SG-01, AE-01, AE-03].
