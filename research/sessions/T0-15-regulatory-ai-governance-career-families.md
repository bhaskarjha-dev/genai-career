# Regulatory AI-governance career families (as of October 2026)

> Research ran 2026-10-21 to 2026-10-23 (two Axial pages were re-read on 23 Oct) and this file was written 2026-10-23. Every figure is "as retrieved" on those dates. Evidence grades A to D are defined in section 9, and tags such as [S23] point to rows of the evidence ledger there. No downstream decision IDs were supplied in the brief, so `informs_decisions` is written in words.

**Answer in brief**

1. **One real dedicated role, three mostly absorbed ones.** A senior, largely non-coding "AI governance programme lead or manager" is a genuine dedicated role (6 of the 13 job descriptions I could read). "AI compliance specialist" and "algorithmic auditor" are mostly duties absorbed by privacy, legal, security, model-risk and audit teams, or specialisations inside assurance firms. "AI safety engineer" is two small markets: frontier-lab safety (about 600 technical FTEs worldwide) and an enterprise "responsible-AI or AI-governance engineer" niche seen mainly at IT-services firms.
2. **Volumes are low thousands a year, not a new mass profession.** About 2,000 US postings in January to September 2026 (one recruiter-analytics source), 119 live UK postings naming the EU AI Act (about 0.7% of AI-citing postings, my ratio), and roughly 7 AI builders per governance hire across 8 EU countries. LinkedIn, Indeed and Naukri counts could not be obtained.
3. **A "surge" in technical compliance engineers is not supported.** The high-risk duties that need conformity pipelines now start on 2 Dec 2027 (Annex III) and 2 Aug 2028 (Annex I); conformity for most Annex III uses is self-assessment; the 2026 duties fall on providers of general-purpose models and generative systems (the engaged population is tens to a few hundred organisations; I found no count of all firms bound by Art. 50(2)); none of the 13 sampled job descriptions names C2PA or Guardrails AI.
4. **The brief's milestones are half right.** GPAI duties began on 2 Aug 2025; August 2026 added Art. 50 and AI Office fining powers (first information requests went out on 29 Aug 2026); 2 Dec 2026 only ends a grace period for older generative systems. C2PA, conformity assessments and bias-mitigation test suites are not mandated by name for GPAI providers.
5. **AIGP is a cheap differentiator, not a gate.** It is mentioned in about 9% of US AI-governance postings (CISSP 10.4%, CIPP 8.2%), none of the 13 job descriptions requires it, and no salary premium is established.
6. **Confidence is medium:** high for legal dates and tool versions, low to medium for posting volumes and credential effects.

## 3. Key Findings

Each finding carries an evidence grade (A to D, see section 9). Detail, tables and caveats are in section 6.

### F1. The brief's EU milestone sequence is only half right (Grade A for dates; B for the Art. 50(2) grace)

In the Commission's consolidated text as at 27 Jul 2026, which reflects the Digital Omnibus on AI [S01]:

- GPAI duties (Chapter V) have applied since 2 Aug 2025. The Regulation applies generally from 2 Aug 2026, which is also when fines under Art. 101 begin. The AI Office's first formal requests for information went out on 29 Aug 2026, including a set to more than 30 firms about training-content summaries and copyright [S01][S23][S24].
- Art. 50 transparency duties apply from 2 Aug 2026 [S17]. The date 2 Dec 2026 is only the end of the Art. 111(4) grace for providers whose generative systems were placed on the market before 2 Aug 2026 [S09][S10][S15].
- High-risk duties start on 2 Dec 2027 for Annex III and 2 Aug 2028 for Annex I. New Art. 5 bans on non-consensual-intimate-imagery and CSAM generators start on 2 Dec 2026. Legacy GPAI models have until 2 Aug 2027 [S01][S09].

So "August 2026 GPAI transparency" merges the 2025 GPAI regime with the 2026 Art. 50 duties and enforcement powers, "December 2026 marking" is a legacy grace, and "2027 high-risk" is right only for Annex III and only in December. Law-firm sources put the Omnibus's publication in the Official Journal on 24 Jul 2026 and its entry into force on 27 Jul 2026 [S14]; its regulation number, (EU) 2026/1744, comes from another law-firm source and was not checked against EUR-Lex [S16]. **So what:** the near-term legal pull falls on model and generative-system providers and on AI Office responses, not on enterprise conformity teams.

### F2. What GPAI providers must actually deliver, and what the brief wrongly assumes (Grade A for the Act and C2PA texts; B for the Code)

- **Mandated for every GPAI provider (Art. 53(1)):** (a) technical documentation (Annex XI) kept current and supplied to the AI Office on request, (b) information for downstream providers (Annex XII), (c) an EU copyright policy, (d) a public training-content summary on the AI Office template. Systemic-risk models add Art. 55(1): model evaluation with documented adversarial testing, systemic-risk assessment and mitigation, serious-incident tracking and reporting, and cybersecurity [S03][S04]. Adhering to the GPAI Code of Practice (22 listed signatories) is the main route to show compliance [S03][S21].
- **Not mandated by name:** conformity assessments (a high-risk-system procedure under Art. 43 [S06]); bias-mitigation test suites (Annex IV asks high-risk providers to document metrics including "potentially discriminatory impacts" [S08], which is not a GPAI duty); C2PA.
- **Marking (Art. 50(2)):** it binds providers of AI *systems*, including GPAI systems, that generate synthetic content. They must mark outputs in a machine-readable format with solutions that are effective, interoperable, robust and reliable "as far as this is technically feasible" [S02]. The voluntary transparency Code (final 10 Jun 2026, about 190 signatories) asks for at least two layers of machine-readable marking and does not name C2PA [S18][S19][S20].
- **What C2PA is:** a signed provenance-manifest standard, not a watermark. Its own deployment guidance says watermarks can be removed or spoofed and fingerprint matching is probabilistic, and the specification says it gives no protection against complete removal of manifests [S121][S123].

**So what:** "C2PA cryptographic watermarking is mandated for GPAI providers" is wrong on the actor (systems, not only models), on the technique (the Act is technique-neutral) and on what C2PA is.

### F3. Conformity assessment is mostly self-assessment, so no notified-body labour market exists before 2028 (Grade A for Art. 43; B for standards and authorities)

Under Art. 43, Annex III points 2 to 8 use internal control (Annex VI) with no notified body; Annex III point 1 (biometrics) may use internal control only where harmonised standards or common specifications were applied, otherwise a notified body; Annex I products follow their sectoral procedure, and sectoral notified bodies must apply for AI designation by 28 Jan 2028 [S06]. No harmonised standard had been cited in the Official Journal as of 28 Apr 2026, prEN 18286 (AI quality management) was targeted for late 2026, and ISO/IEC 42001 sits outside the harmonisation process and gives no Art. 40 presumption of conformity [S25]. Only 9 Member States had clearly designated both authorities by 17 Jun 2026 (12 partial, 6 unclear), and I found no notified-body count [S26]. **So what:** most high-risk compliance is first-party work done by the provider's own staff, and an independent "conformity assessor" profession is not mandated for most uses.

### F4. Posting volumes are in the low thousands per year and a small slice of AI hiring (Grade C throughout)

- **US:** 1,997 AI-governance postings in January to September 2026, about 2,700 a year annualised (my arithmetic) [S64][S65]. Axial quotes about 71 new postings a week (range 11 to 195), but 1,997 over 39 weeks is about 51 a week, so its own figures disagree, and the same n appears on four Axial pages with different dates and windows [S64][S65][S66][S67].
- **UK:** 119 live postings mention "EU AI Act" against 17,685 live AI-citing postings, about 0.7% (my ratio; the two counts may not share a definition) [S68][S69].
- **EU:** 3,519 AI-related postings across 8 countries with about 7 builders per governor (Sweden 16:1, France 11:1, Ireland 3.5:1); fewer than 3 in 10 governance postings mention the AI Act [S70].
- **ZipRecruiter** shows "1000+" for Responsible AI, AI Ethics and AI Risk, yet displays 20 results a page over 3 pages, and one Deloitte requisition appears 16 times (one per city) on the AI Risk page I read. I treat "1000+" as a display claim, not a count [S71][S72][S73].
- **India:** no platform count obtained; a small aggregator shows 0 [S76].

**So what:** thousands a year in the US, about a hundred live UK postings naming the EU AI Act, a few hundred governance postings across eight EU countries, and unknown in India. This partly meets the brief's "quantified with platform data" criterion; LinkedIn and Indeed are missing (section 7).

### F5. Ownership is fragmented: dedicated at programme-lead level, absorbed at specialist level (Grade B; medium confidence)

- **Who owns it:** IAPP (n above 670) finds privacy 22%, legal and compliance 22%, IT 17%, data governance 10%, ethics and compliance 6% and security 5% as primary owner, and half of AI-governance staff sit in ethics, compliance, privacy or legal teams [S96]. The Stanford AI Index 2026 captions show information security most often primary (21% in 2024 and 2025) and risk/compliance rising from 13% to 19% [S99]. Two surveys with different respondent bases name different top owners (privacy and legal in IAPP's privacy-professional sample, information security in the Stanford series), which is what fragmented ownership looks like; IAPP's base probably inflates the privacy share.
- **Regulators agree:** EIOPA says an "AI officer" is optional, adds no new requirements and names compliance, audit, DPO and actuarial functions [S27]. The NAIC bulletin (29 jurisdictions) asks insurers for a cross-functional governance structure rather than a new role [S45][S46]. SR 26-2 folds predictive AI into model-risk management and excludes generative and agentic AI [S42][S44]. In a hospital survey, 33% cite unclear ownership across IT, quality and compliance [S101].
- **Dedicated roles do exist:** 41 of 50 large banks had dedicated AI-governance staff at end-2024, but only 247 responsible-AI staff across all 50 (about 4.9 each) [S98]; only 10 of 671 IAPP respondents needed no extra staff [S96].
- **"AI ethicist":** whether the title vanished cannot be shown either way. Evidence is 2023 layoffs without headcounts [S113], 2024 Indian listings with AI-ethics titles [S93] and a noisy 2026 ZipRecruiter page [S72].

**So what:** plan for a small number of senior programme owners and for upskilling existing privacy, security, risk and audit staff.

### F6. Technical "compliance engineer" roles exist but are thin, and the skill stack is ordinary MLOps rather than watermarking (Grade C for postings; A for the Act's deliverables)

I read 13 job descriptions, two only in part (a purposive sample surfaced by title searches, not a random one): 6 class G, 2 H, 4 T and 1 other [S79]-[S92]. The four class-T postings sit at IT-services firms (Cognizant, Infosys), a Microsoft-stack staffing client (Beacon Hill) and one employer-hidden listing; the two hybrids are at Stryker (medtech) and Eaton (industrial). They ask for Python, evaluation pipelines (hallucination, groundedness, subgroup analysis), runtime guardrails and red-teaming, logging and audit trails, observability, model cards and risk logs, cloud MLOps, and EU AI Act, NIST AI RMF and ISO 42001 literacy. Named tools: Fairlearn and AIF360 in 1 of 13 (Infosys); Giskard, NeMo Guardrails and Arize as "preferred" in 1 (Cognizant); IBM OpenPages in 1; Microsoft Purview, Fabric and Azure AI Foundry in 1; **C2PA in 0; Guardrails AI in 0** [S87][S88][S90][S91]. In the larger Axial sample Python appears in 27.1% of postings and observability or monitoring in 42.3% [S67]. The Act's technical deliverables map onto familiar work: Art. 12 event logs [S07], Annex IV documentation and test metrics [S08], Art. 55 adversarial testing [S04], Art. 50(2) marking [S02]. **So what:** technical compliance engineering is an extension of ML-platform, MLOps and security engineering; the marking and provenance stack is built inside provider ML and product teams.

### F7. "Algorithmic auditor" is a specialisation inside assurance firms, not a high-volume career (Grade A for the NYC evidence; B for the rest)

- **NYC Local Law 144:** a 2024 study of 391 employers found 18 had posted an audit report and 13 a transparency notice [S48]. The state Comptroller's December 2025 audit found the city enforcer reviewed 32 sites and flagged 1 likely violation where the auditors found 17, and only 3 of 12 test complaint calls reached the right office [S47].
- **EU Digital Services Act:** 23 public audit reports (21 very large platforms, 2 search engines) in the 2025 cycle, written by assurance firms; KPMG names Technology Assurance, TechLaw and Privacy and Digital TMT as supporting teams [S112].
- **AI Act:** internal control for Annex III points 2 to 8 [S06]. **ISO/IEC 42001:** certification bodies are only now being accredited (ISO/IEC 42006 published Jul 2025; BSI's ANAB accreditation Mar 2026) [S28][S29]. **ISACA AAIA** requires an existing CISA, CIA or CPA [S109].

**So what:** mandated audits have produced few audit jobs; where they exist they are held by Big Four, certification bodies and internal audit.

### F8. "AI Safety Engineer" names two small markets plus one unrelated use (Grade B and C)

- **Frontier safety:** Art. 55 duties and the Code's Safety & Security chapter fall on providers of systemic-risk models (presumed above 10^25 FLOP [S05]). The Commission lists 22 GPAI Code signatories (xAI signed only the Safety & Security chapter), and the AI Office's first Safety & Security information requests went out on 29 Aug 2026 [S21][S23]. One 2025 community estimate counts about 600 technical and 500 non-technical FTEs across 67 and 49 organisations [S111].
- **Enterprise responsible-AI or governance engineer:** the thin class-T niche of finding 6.
- **Negative result:** I found no job-board page showing postings titled "AI Safety Engineer" at regulated enterprises (searches returned career-guide blogs). Automotive "functional safety for AI" (Bosch, ISO/PAS 8800) is a separate use of the word [S92].

**So what:** no single high-volume career sits behind the title; the commercial-compliance version is small and mostly engineering-led.

### F9. AIGP is a cheap differentiator with no demonstrated hiring gate or pay premium (Grade C for mention rates; B for the survey)

AIGP appears in 8.9% of Axial's US postings (table value; 8.7 to 9% in the text), next to CISSP 10.4%, CIPP 8.2%, CISM 8.1%, CIPM 6.8%, CRISC 6.4% and CISA 5.9%; a mention is not a requirement [S67]. None of the 13 job descriptions names AIGP, and only one (Eaton, Pune) names any certification, preferring CISSP, CIPP or CISA [S86]. Of the 20 visible listings on ZipRecruiter's IAPP page, none requires or prefers AIGP [S74]. IAPP's salary survey shows medians of about $151.8K (AI governance only), $123K (privacy only) and $169.7K (both), but IAPP says certification is "not proof of cause and effect" and owns the credential [S107][S108]. No holder count or AIGP-specific premium was found, and a training vendor's "13%/27% higher salary" claims are unsourced [S110]. The exam costs $649 for IAPP members and $799 otherwise, with no prerequisites [S106]. **So what:** useful as a low-cost signal on top of a privacy, security or audit foundation, not as a primary investment.

### F10. Of the brief's four "high-paying" sectors, only healthcare is supported (Grade C)

Axial's sector medians use the top of each posted range, not a typical offer: life sciences $272K (56 postings), healthcare $249K (108), technology $242K (304), manufacturing $237K (54), professional services $219K (897). Financial services, government and defence, and automotive are not reported [S66]. Volume is led by professional services (32 to 36% of postings) and financial services (13 to 15%) [S64][S65]. Some professional-services volume may be firms hiring to deliver governance to clients, which would make end-user demand smaller than the counts suggest (my inference; not tested). The page's own text and table also disagree (the text says manufacturing tops the table at $272,000; the table shows life sciences at $272K and manufacturing at $237K), and I used the table. **So what:** treat healthcare and life sciences as the best-evidenced premium and the other three as unevidenced.

### F11. US demand is driven by sector regulators and contracts, not by state statutes or executive orders (Grade B)

The 11 Dec 2025 executive order set up a DOJ AI Litigation Task Force [S35]. Colorado's SB 24-205 never took effect: xAI sued on 9 Apr 2026, DOJ moved to intervene on 24 Apr and an order barred enforcement on 27 Apr; it was replaced by SB26-189, a notice, disclosure and human-review regime from 1 Jan 2027 that drops impact assessments and the risk-management programme [S31][S33][S34]. Congress had passed no preemption as of 22 Jun 2026 [S37]. NIST's AI RMF stays voluntary and "is being revised" [S39]; COSAIS control overlays are in development [S40]; OMB M-25-21 requires agency Chief AI Officers and AI governance boards [S41]. Sector drivers persist: the NAIC bulletin in 29 jurisdictions [S45], SR 26-2 for banks [S42] and NYC Local Law 144 [S47]. **So what:** the US pulls AI-governance staff through insurance, banking and procurement more than through AI statutes.

### F12. DPDP's effect on GenAI architecture is anticipatory until 13 May 2027, and India's binding AI rule is the provenance amendment (Grade B; documented gap)

The DPDP Rules were notified on 13 Nov 2025; consent-manager rules start 13 Nov 2026 and the main obligations 13 May 2027. MeitY's proposal to compress the timeline was not adopted, the 5 Oct 2026 Removal of Difficulties Order is clarificatory, and the Data Protection Board is not fully operational [S50][S51][S52][S53]. For GenAI systems the text implies itemised notice and consent, "encryption, obfuscation, masking or virtual tokens" safeguards, processing logs kept at least one year, and for Significant Data Fiduciaries an annual DPIA and audit plus algorithmic due diligence [S56]. There is no AI-specific rule for training data: the s.3(c)(ii) public-data carve-out is untested for scraping [S60][S61]; India has no standalone AI law [S59]; and I found no official Indian guidance on LLM training, retention or unlearning. The nearest binding India-specific rule is the IT Rules amendment on synthetic content, effective 20 Feb 2026, with permanent metadata or provenance markers and 3-hour takedowns [S57]. India's visible AI-governance postings sit at global capability centres and IT-services firms (Alstom, Eaton, EXL, Cognizant, Infosys) [S84][S85][S86][S90][S91]. **So what:** expect privacy-engineering and DPO demand to lead, with AI-specific roles following the 2027 enforcement date.

## 4. Recommendation

**Plan for three things, not four titles.** (1) A small, senior, mostly non-coding "AI governance programme" role; (2) a thin technical "responsible-AI or governance engineer" niche that grows out of ML-platform, MLOps and security engineering; (3) assurance and audit specialisations inside existing audit, certification and model-risk careers. Expect most compliance work to stay absorbed by privacy, security, risk and legal teams through at least 2027. Do not plan on a high-volume standalone AI-compliance-specialist, algorithmic-auditor or AI-ethicist labour market. Revisit when the triggers in 4.5 fire.

### 4.2 For someone choosing a path

Ordered by strength of evidence:

1. **Programme lead or manager (G).** Enter from GRC, privacy, audit or security with about five years (Axial's median minimum experience is about 5 years; ERGO asks for 5+, Alstom 8 to 10) [S67][S81][S84]. Build EU AI Act, NIST AI RMF and ISO/IEC 42001 literacy, an AI inventory or registry, risk-assessment method and committee operations. Target consultancies, banks and insurers, large manufacturers and global capability centres. Axial's US median is $169,000, but its salary page says its figures are the top of each posted range, so a typical offer is lower [S64][S66].
2. **Governance or responsible-AI engineer (T or H).** Enter from ML, MLOps, platform or security engineering. Build a portfolio mapped to the Act's deliverables: Annex IV-style model documentation, Art. 12-style structured logging, subgroup evaluation with Fairlearn, an LLM red-team or evaluation harness (Giskard v3, NeMo Guardrails or Guardrails AI), and a provenance-signing demo with c2pa-python. Target IT-services firms, medtech and Microsoft-stack enterprises; expect few openings.
3. **Assurance specialisation (A).** Stay in audit, model-risk or validation and add AI-specific scope: AAIA (requires CISA, CIA or CPA) or an ISO/IEC 42001 auditor route if you aim at certification bodies or Big Four DSA and ISO work [S109][S112][S29].
4. **Frontier safety (R).** Research-grade, about 600 technical FTEs worldwide; outside commercial-compliance careers [S111].

Avoid positioning as a standalone "AI ethicist". Use "responsible AI" or "AI governance" with a functional anchor (privacy, risk, security or engineering).

**Credentials, in order:** an established domain credential first (CIPP or CIPM, or CISSP, CISA or CRISC), because each is mentioned about as often as AIGP or more [S67]; AIGP second and optional ($649 or $799) as a differentiator [S106]; AAIA only if you already hold CISA, CIA or CPA [S109]; an ISO/IEC 42001 auditor or implementer course only if you target certification-body work (no hiring-weight data found). No credential has an evidenced salary premium.

### 4.3 For organisations deciding how to staff

1. Name one accountable programme lead (or confirm the existing privacy, risk or security owner) and a cross-functional committee. Do not build a separate AI-compliance department yet.
2. Make evidence generation a by-product of delivery: Art. 12-style logs, evaluation reports, system documentation and incident records live in the ML platform and are staffed from MLOps, platform and security engineering [S07][S08].
3. **If you provide GPAI models or generative systems,** staff now for Art. 53 and 55 deliverables, for AI Office information requests (Matheson's advice is to reuse data-protection inspection processes) and for Art. 50(2) marking. Systems placed on the market before 2 Aug 2026 have until 2 Dec 2026; newer systems have no grace [S03][S04][S23][S15]. Give marking and provenance a named owner in product ML.
4. **If you provide Annex III high-risk systems,** plan for 2 Dec 2027 but gate large spending on harmonised standards (prEN 18286) and on your sector's guidance; the date has already moved once [S01][S25].
5. **Banks and insurers:** let model risk own predictive-AI governance (SR 26-2, NAIC bulletin) and assign explicit owners for generative and agentic AI, which SR 26-2 leaves outside its scope [S42][S44][S46].
6. **India:** sequence privacy-engineering and DPO capacity for 13 May 2027, and treat provenance marking of synthetic content (in force since 20 Feb 2026) as the nearer AI-specific duty [S53][S57].

### 4.4 Tooling stance

- Build on maintained components: Fairlearn 0.15.0 (12 Oct 2026), c2pa-python 0.38.0 (29 Sep 2026, pre-1.0 so pin versions), Guardrails AI 0.11.0 (14 Aug 2026, with breaking changes), NeMo Guardrails, Giskard v3 (pre-release, no tabular testing) [S114][S119][S117][S118][S126][S125].
- Treat AIF360 (last PyPI release 8 Apr 2024) and holisticai (3 Mar 2025) as stale unless repository activity shows otherwise (I could not check GitHub); vendor or fork them if you depend on them [S115][S116].

**Reading the table**

- The legal pull in 2025 and 2026 falls on model and generative-system providers. The Commission counts about 190 signatories of the transparency Code and 22 of the GPAI Code, and its first requests reached more than 30 firms on training-content summaries and copyright [S20][S21][S23]. Those counts show who has engaged, not everyone who is bound: Art. 50(2) applies to any provider of a system that generates synthetic content, and I found no count of such providers. The pull is on product and ML teams at model and generative-system providers, not on enterprise conformity teams.
- The pull on enterprise providers of high-risk systems starts on 2 Dec 2027 and 2 Aug 2028, and it has already moved once (from 2 Aug 2026 and 2 Aug 2027) [S01].
- Enforcement tools are in place: Art. 101 fines for GPAI providers of up to EUR 15m or 3%, documentation requests (Art. 91), evaluations (Art. 92) and information requests (Art. 75(1), where only requests made by decision carry fines). Matheson reports no interviews or premises inspections yet, and advises reusing procedures built for data-protection inspections [S23]. A Danish legal portal's English rendering of a Commission press item adds that the Scientific Panel was convened on summer incidents involving AI agents [S24].
- Authorities and standards are not ready: 9 of 27 Member States had clearly designated both authorities by 17 Jun 2026, no harmonised standard had been cited in the Official Journal by 28 Apr 2026, and ISO/IEC 42001 gives no presumption of conformity [S25][S26].

### 6.8 "AI Safety Engineer": two small markets and one unrelated use

- **Negative result.** I found no job-board page showing postings titled "AI Safety Engineer" at regulated enterprises; searches returned career-guide blogs.

**Reading.** No single high-volume career sits behind the title. The compliance-driven version is small and engineering-led, and the frontier version is a research-grade market of about a thousand people.
