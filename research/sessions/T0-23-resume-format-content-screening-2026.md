## Research Question

**Decision.** Which résumé format, section order, length, design and content give a GenAI candidate, from new graduate to leader, the best chance of being read and shortlisted in 2026 by (a) a parser, (b) an AI ranking layer and (c) a human reviewer with little time, in the US, Canada, UK, EU, India, Gulf states, Singapore, Japan and Australia; and what should differ between role families. The question is evaluative and causal: which features change callbacks, and by how much. Readers are GenAI job seekers worldwide, with extra depth for India-based readers.

**Sub-questions.**

1. Which résumé features have experimental or audit evidence on callbacks or hires, and how large are the effects?
2. How long do reviewers spend on a résumé, and where does each reading-time figure come from?
3. What do applicant-tracking vendors document about parsing, ranking and automatic rejection (K1 to K3, K21)?
4. What do audits of AI ranking and studies of hidden text show about bias, self-preference and detection?
5. How are AI-written résumés and cover letters treated by screening software and by recruiters?
6. What do official and practitioner sources say about photo, date of birth, nationality and visa, salary and notice, and length in each region?
7. What differs between the 12 role families?
8. Which builders, checkers and parse checks are safe to use (K12 to K22)?

**Scope as applied.** LinkedIn profiles, interview preparation and the law on automated screening are out of scope; the law appears only where it changes résumé advice. Where the brief's wording was ambiguous, I resolved it as follows: "ATS" means an employer's applicant-tracking system, not a checker website; "tailoring" is judged by what the cited study defines it to be; a source's own statements about its product are graded A and its claims of value C with COI. Evidence on screening software, AI-written résumés and surveys is limited to 2024-01-01 to 2026-11-11; experiments on résumé features may be of any date and are flagged where old.

**Method.** I ran 79 web searches in the working session on 2026-11-13 and 2026-11-14, in English and, for regional rules, in German, French, Dutch, Japanese, Swedish and Danish; an earlier experiments strand ran six more on 2026-11-13. The search tool returned titles and URLs only, so every fact in this report comes from a page that was fetched. The ledger lists 127 rows: 112 web pages read through the fetch tool, one own measurement (M1, below), 4 pages seen only as a snippet or metadata (Extract), 4 blocked pages (robots rules, redirect loop or a URL outside the session's allowlist) and 6 pages that could not be fetched (Gap). I did not work around any block. Pages were fetched in batches against fixed URL lists, and I re-checked the load-bearing figures myself: Huntr's tailored and untailored rates, the NBER wage effect, the Greenhouse parse page, the hidden-prompt study and the ResumeGo figures. Sample sizes are given wherever a page gave them.

## Recommendation

The rules below are conditional ("if … then …"). Each one names the evidence and its grade, the way the rule can fail, and what finding would change it. Where the evidence is only [P] (practice) or D (judgement), the rule says so. Most rules protect against the mechanics that vendors document (parsing, extracted fields, form-answer rejection) because those are the best-evidenced; almost no experiment tests format, length, bullets or tailoring directly.

| # | Rule | Evidence and grade | Failure mode | What would change it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | **Format and file type.** If you apply through an employer's form or ATS, then upload a single-column PDF exported as text (from Word, Google Docs, LaTeX or Typst), or a .docx where the portal asks for one; keep it under 2.5 MB; put your name and contact details in the body, not in a header, footer or text box. Then read the file back with two extractors from different families (for example pdftotext and PyMuPDF) and a copy-paste test, and compare the order. | Greenhouse's parse checklist [S25] (A). M1: one-column order matched across six extractors; two-column order differed between extractor families [S26] (M, n = 1 file per layout). Vendor-run layout test [S27] (C, COI). | A plain single-column file has less visual impact. Two open-source extractors can both pass while a vendor's own parser fails, because the vendors' parsers are not the open-source ones. | Vendor documentation or a controlled test showing equal parsing of two-column layouts across the main ATS products. |
| R2 | **Length by seniority and region.** If you are a new graduate or have under about five years of experience, use one page; from mid-level up use two. Leaders and staff-level candidates may use a third page only where the region tolerates it (the Gulf, per practitioners). Cut experience older than 15 years unless it is the evidence the role needs. | No causal evidence either way (KF9). Official maxima: two pages in Canada [S57] and Australia [S61], one to two in Sweden [S82], one to two A4 pages for the Japanese work-history statement [S62] (A). One page for juniors is [P]. Vendor simulation favours two pages at every level, 1.4 times for entry level [S4] (C, COI). | A one-page graduate résumé may leave out the evidence a ranking layer looks for; a two-page résumé padded with weak bullets dilutes the strong ones. | An independent experiment that varies page count while holding content fixed. |
| R3 | **Section order.** If you are early in your career, put education and projects after a short introduction; if you have substantial experience, lead with experience. Put publications early only for research roles. Use the skeletons in Detailed Findings (b). | UK National Careers Service: early-career candidates put education after the introduction, experienced candidates lead with work history [S59] (A). Sweden prescribes no fixed order [S82] (A). Section order for GenAI roles is [P]. | A region with a fixed form (Japan's 履歴書) overrides the skeleton. | An experiment on section order, or a vendor page describing how sections are weighted. |
| R4 | **Design.** If your résumé goes through a portal, use a plain typeface, standard headings (Experience, Education, Skills, Projects, Publications), no icons, graphics, photo (outside Japan), text boxes or layout tables. A one-file vendor test read a sidebar before work history and turned icons into stray characters [S84] (C, COI). | Plain design can look generic; design and creative roles need a portfolio link. | Vendor documentation showing that designed templates parse cleanly. |

No experiment on quantified bullets found. A same-file test saw six of eight checker scores rise by about six points when measurable results were added [S1] (C, COI), which measures checkers, not outcomes. | Invented or unverifiable numbers; confidentiality limits; a metric without a baseline misleads. | An experiment that varies quantification and measures callbacks. |
| R6 | **Keyword mirroring and tailoring depth.** If the posting lists requirements, then give each one a verifiable evidence line in your most recent relevant role, use the posting's job title in your headline only where it is true, and spend 10 to 20 minutes on the summary, the skills order and the top bullets. Do not paste keyword blocks. | AI layers compare extracted titles, skills, years, dates and employers with recruiter criteria [S28, S29, S30] (A). Huntr's association between tool-tailored applications and interviews [S16, S38] (C, COI). The 10 to 20 minute budget has no source (D). | You may tailor to wording the ranker does not weigh; inflated claims are exposed by an AI interview (21% of 720 audited candidates claimed a skill rated "not familiar") [S46] (B). | An independent experiment on tailoring or keyword mirroring. |
| R7 | **Summary or headline.** If you have more than about two years of experience, open with two or three lines that give the role family, the stack and the scale of your work. A new graduate may omit it. Leave out objective statements. | Sweden's employment service says a short summary can help employers see fit [S82] (A); the Australian Human Rights Commission calls a career profile optional and caps it at five points [S61] (A). The effect of a summary is [P]; no experiment found. | A generic summary wastes the top of the page. | An experiment on summaries. |
| R8 | **Cover letters.** If the application invites or requires a cover letter, write a short, specific one yourself. If it is optional, skip it unless it adds something the résumé cannot hold. | Both DeepMind postings read list the cover letter as optional [S71, S72] (A). On one platform the premium for customised applications disappeared after LLMs arrived [S43] (B). | A region or employer that expects a letter (not researched for most regions) may read its absence badly. | A field experiment on cover letters after LLMs. |
| R9 | **GenAI-specific content.** If you claim GenAI skills, show artefacts: a deployed or reproducible system, how you evaluated it, what failed, public code, papers or write-ups. List a tool only beside evidence. | Practitioners ask for evals, projects with trade-offs and production work [S73, S74] (C, COI). Listing AI skills raised stated interview-invitation probability by roughly 8 to 15 points, and certificates added little over self-declared skills [S45] (B). Skill inflation is detectable [S46] (B). | Private repositories, confidentiality limits, links that stop working, and evaluations that were tuned to the test. | An audit or field experiment on GenAI résumés. |
| R10 | **AI-assisted writing and disclosure.** If you use an AI writing tool, write the first draft yourself, use the tool to tighten and proofread, check every claim, and follow each employer's stated policy. Anthropic's candidate guidance (COI) lets candidates refine a résumé with Claude, asks them to write the first draft, forbids inventing experience and asks for openness about AI use. | Writing help raised hires in a randomised experiment [S42] (A). Anthropic's guidance, dated 2025-07-10 [S88] (A, COI). Some reviewers say they would reject AI-written material (19.6% of 600 US hiring managers) [S47] (C), while LLM screeners prefer LLM-written text [S44] (B). | The two pressures conflict and you cannot see which reviewer you get; recruiter detection claims (73% say they can tell; 33.5% against 25% by chance) are unreliable and sit at C. | A study of real hiring outcomes for AI-polished and human-written résumés. |
| R11 | **Hidden text and auto-apply.** Do not use white text, tiny text, keyword blocks or hidden prompts. Extract your own file's text and compare it with what you see, in case a template carries hidden text. Read a platform's own terms before using an auto-apply tool. | All six extractors returned a hidden marker [S26] (M). About 1% of roughly 197,000 résumés on one platform carried hidden prompts, and detectors are deployed [S7, S36] (B). Recruiters may see the text and disqualify [S8] (C). LinkedIn's help pages on automation were blocked [S9, S10]. | If some production systems reward hidden text, which is unmeasured, honest candidates lose a little. A template can carry concealed text without the applicant knowing. | A measured production effect, or the text of platform policies on automation. |
| R12 | **Regional fields.** Follow the regional table in Detailed Findings (c). Omit photo and date of birth in the US, Canada, UK, Singapore and Australia; include a photo and date of birth on Japan's 履歴書; in India and the Gulf follow the posting or the recruiter, and where unsure send the version with fewer protected attributes. State work authorisation in one line where the application asks. Give a notice period where practitioners expect it (India, the Gulf). Keep salary expectations off the résumé. | Official guidance for the US, Canada, UK, Singapore, Australia and Japan (KF12) (A); practitioner sources for India, the Gulf and Germany (C), and the two India sources disagree on photo. Audits show human screening reacts to names [S52] (B). | Practitioner norms may be out of date (the Gulf News page is dated 2018-09-15); omitting a locally expected field may cost interviews; official guidance for several regions was not reached. | Official guidance for Germany, India and the Gulf; evidence on how photos change callbacks. |
| R13 | **Role-family differences.** Use the role-family table in Detailed Findings (b) and put your family's proof artefact first: shipped systems and evals for applied roles, publications for research roles, deployment stories for forward-deployed roles, programme and audit outcomes for governance roles. | Thin: one posting per family at most [S71, S72, S89], one survey for governance [S90], practitioner pages for evals [S74] (A for the postings; B for the survey; C for the pages). Most cells are [P]. | One posting does not describe a family; titles overlap across families. | A sample of postings per family and any outcome data. |
| R14 | **Dates and gaps.** Keep month-and-year dates. ### (f) Own measurement M1: six PDF text extractors on four synthetic PDFs

Source row: [S26]; grade M. I ran this test on 2026-11-14 to check K5 and K20 and to see how far extractors agree. It is a demonstration, not a survey: **one file per layout**, synthetic content, two PDF producers only (Chromium print-to-PDF through Playwright, and ReportLab). Tool versions: pdftotext and pdffonts 24.02.0 (Poppler); PyMuPDF 2.1; pypdf 6.17.0; pdfminer.six 20260107; pdfplumber 0.11.10; ReportLab 5.0.1; Playwright 1.63.0.

**Files.** (1) single column: a heading, five "skills" lines and six "experience" lines, then a hyperlink; (2) two columns built with flexbox (skills left, experience right); (3) the single-column file plus a white 8-point marker string, "ZXQ-HIDDEN-7731"; (4) a ReportLab file that draws the left and right text row by row, using the standard Helvetica font. **Reading order** is the order in which the labels L1 to L5 (left column) and R1 to R6 (right column) come out of each extractor.

| File | pdffonts (type, embedded, Unicode map) | pdftotext | pdftotext -layout | PyMuPDF | pdfminer.six | pypdf | pdfplumber | Grade |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Single column | CID TrueType, yes, yes | L1 to L5, then R1 to R6 | same | same | same | same | same | M |
| Two columns | CID TrueType, yes, yes | Row by row: L1 R1 L2 R2 … | Row by row | Column by column: L1 to L5, then R1 to R6 | Row by row | Column by column | Row by row | M |
| Rows drawn in interleaved order | Type 1, not embedded, no Unicode map | Row by row | Row by row | Row by row | Column by column | Row by row | Row by row | M |
| Hidden white marker | CID TrueType, yes, yes | Marker returned | Marker returned | Marker returned | Marker returned | Marker returned | Marker returned | M |

**Link target.** For the single-column file PyMuPDF's link list returned https://github.com/example, while the pdftotext text contained the visible words "GitHub profile" and not the URL.

**What the results support.**

1. **Hidden text (K5):** every extractor returned the white marker, so text that a human cannot see is visible to machines. Whether a production system keeps or flags it is not tested here.
2. **Reading order (K2, K20):** a simple single-column file reads identically everywhere. A column layout does not: four of the six read the two columns row by row and two read column by column, and for the interleaved-drawn file five read row by row and one column by column. A pair of extractors from different families (for example pdftotext and PyMuPDF) exposes the difference; two extractors from one family would not.
3. **Unicode-map rule (K20):** the standard Helvetica file shows "no" in the Unicode-map column and still extracted cleanly in all six tools. The rule "a Unicode map on every row" therefore raises a false alarm for non-embedded standard fonts; read it as "on every embedded font".
4. **Links (R15):** only PyMuPDF recovered the target; show the URL as visible text.
5. **Not tested:** Type 3 fonts, scanned or image-only PDFs, DOCX files, PDFs produced by Word, Google Docs, Canva or LaTeX, and any vendor parser. The extractors are open-source libraries, not the parsers inside an ATS.

**Generator script** (as run; saves the four PDFs):

```python
import sys
from playwright.sync_api import sync_playwright
from reportlab.pdfgen import canvas
out = sys.argv[1]
L = "".join(f"<p>L{i} skill item office fi</p>" for i in range(1, 6))
R = "".join(f"<p>&bull; R{i} experience line</p>" for i in range(1, 7))
link = '<p><a href="https://github.com/example">GitHub profile</a></p>'
head = "<h1>Jane Example</h1><p>AI Engineer</p>"
single = f"<html><body style='font-family:Arial'>{head}<h2>SKILLS</h2>{L}<h2>EXPERIENCE</h2>{R}{link}</body></html>"
two = f"<html><body style='font-family:Arial'>{head}<div style='display:flex'><div style='width:28%'><h2>SKILLS</h2>{L}</div><div style='width:72%'><h2>EXPERIENCE</h2>{R}{link}</div></div></body></html>"
hidden = single.replace(link, link + "<p style='color:#fff;font-size:8pt'>ZXQ-HIDDEN-7731</p>")
with sync_playwright() as p:
    b = p.chromium.launch(); pg = b.new_page()
    for name, html in (("single", single), ("twocol", two), ("hidden", hidden)):
        pg.set_content(html); pg.pdf(path=f"{out}/{name}.pdf", format="A4", print_background=True)
    b.close()
c = canvas.Canvas(f"{out}/interleaved.pdf")
y = 780
for i in range(1, 7):
    if i <= 5: c.drawString(50, y, f"L{i} skill item")
    c.drawString(220, y, f"R{i} experience line"); y -= 20
c.save()
```

**Extractor script** (as run; prints fonts, reading order, hidden-marker result and the link check):

```python
import re, subprocess, sys
import fitz, pdfplumber
from pypdf import PdfReader
from pdfminer.high_level import extract_text
d = sys.argv[1]
def ptt(f, *a): return subprocess.run(["pdftotext", *a, f, "-"], capture_output=True, text=True).stdout
ext = {
 "pdftotext": lambda f: ptt(f),
 "pdftotext-layout": lambda f: ptt(f, "-layout"),
 "pymupdf": lambda f: "".join(p.get_text() for p in fitz.open(f)),
 "pdfminer": lambda f: extract_text(f),
 "pypdf": lambda f: "\n".join(p.extract_text() for p in PdfReader(f).pages),
 "pdfplumber": lambda f: "\n".join((p.extract_text() or "") for p in pdfplumber.open(f).pages),
}
for name in ("single", "twocol", "interleaved", "hidden"):
    f = f"{d}/{name}.pdf"
    fonts = subprocess.run(["pdffonts", f], capture_output=True, text=True).stdout.splitlines()[2:]
    fl = sorted({(" ".join(l.split()[1:-6]), l.split()[-5], l.split()[-3]) for l in fonts})
    print(f"== {name}: fonts(type,emb,unicode-map)={fl}")
    for k, fn in ext.items():
        t = fn(f)
        if name == "hidden": print(f"   {k}: marker={'ZXQ-HIDDEN-7731' in t}")
        else: print(f"   {k}: order={' '.join(re.findall(r'\b[LR][1-6]\b', t))}")
    if name == "single":
        print("   links(pymupdf)=", [l.get('uri') for l in fitz.open(f)[0].get_links()], "| url-in-text(pdftotext)=", "github.com" in ptt(f))
```
