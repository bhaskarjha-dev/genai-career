# T0-30: Certification Census 3: Course Platforms, Universities, Indian Programmes, Credential Bodies and Recognition Programmes

This session is a dated census of GenAI-relevant certificates, certifications and recognition programmes from five issuer types: course platforms, university online programmes, Indian institute and industry programmes, credential bodies marketed to Indian job seekers, and vendor-run recognition programmes. Sources are numbered S1 onwards in the ledger at the end of the report and are cited as [S#]. Grades follow EGP-1.0: **A** is a primary source I opened (an issuer's own page is A for existence and terms only; its claims of value are C with COI); **B** is an established secondary source or a sizeable dataset (preprints sit here); **C** is third-party analysis, a vendor claim or a single corroborating source; **D** is memory or judgement; **E** is unverifiable and appears only in absences; **M** is my own calculation, graded by its weakest input. Access status is **Read** (the page was opened through a fetch tool that returns a summary of the page, not its raw text), **Extract** (a search snippet or title only), **Blocked** (the tool refused the address and I did not try to get round it) or **Gap** (searched for, not found). Retrieval took place on 2026-11-13 and 2026-11-14 (dates in the researcher's calendar, Asia/Kolkata), after the brief's anchor of 2026-11-11, because the session was interrupted by usage limits; every figure is stated as of its retrieval date unless the cell gives another. In tables, "n/s" means the page I read did not show the item; "n/a" means it does not apply.

## Research Question

**Decision question (D-005).** Which GenAI-relevant certifications do course platforms (Coursera, edX, Udacity, DataCamp), university online programmes, Indian institute and industry programmes, credential bodies marketed to Indian job seekers, and vendor recognition programmes offer as of 2026-11-13; what does each involve and cost; how much hiring signal and channel value does each carry; for which role families; and how do they compare? The answer feeds `data/skills_ontology.json`, `career/04-certifications/credential-roi.md` and `career/03-skills-and-tech/learning-roadmaps.md`. The door is two-way.

**Sub-questions.**
1. Census: what is the exact official name, format, proctoring, fee, validity, prerequisites and status of each credential, kept apart as proctored exams, course-completion certificates, degrees with credit, and recognition programmes?
2. Seeds: is each seed in the brief confirmed with its exact name, or recorded as an absence?
3. Hiring signal: how many GenAI postings in a sample I draw myself name each credential, for the US and India separately?
4. Channel value: do partner-programme rules, employer statements or public-sector clauses count individual certifications from these issuers?
5. Rigour: hands-on or multiple choice, proctoring, public verification, exam dumps.
6. Skill alignment, cost to earn and currency (launches, renamings, retirements since 2025-01-01).
7. Probes: employer recognition; accreditation and ownership of each credential body; prices, financing, refunds and mis-selling actions in India.
8. K1: does the knowledge base's claim about the one randomised study hold?

**Readings I chose where the brief is open.** "Hiring signal" means the share of GenAI postings that name the credential, plus any experimental evidence on employer response. "Channel value" means rules or statements that make an individual credential count towards a partner tier, a client contract or an employee target. A credential "serves" a role family only where an opened page (exam guide, domain list or course list) covers that family's core skills; mappings made from a title alone are recorded but not counted towards the five-credential threshold for a provisional order. No weighted scores are computed.

**Scope as applied.** The brief's evidence window is 2025-01-01 to 2026-11-11. I used older sources only for existence, terms or history and flag each: the UGC notice (2023-12-12), USAII documents (2023-02), the AI CERTs exam policy (dated 2024-01-08), the 2U/edX report (2024-12-06), the GSDC launch release (2024-12-03), the Athey and Palikot preprint (first submitted 2024-04-30; revised 2025-11-05) and a Google Cloud partner post (2020-07-15). Credentials from other issuer groups (for example Microsoft AI-102, AWS, Google Cloud and NVIDIA exams) appear only as overlaps.

**Method.**
- *Issuer pages.* I opened the issuer's own page for each credential where the tool allowed it (60 page fetches on issuers, regulators and studies, plus 2 re-reads of ARTiBA exam pages). Coursera facts come from a delegated research pass whose page reads are listed as S1 to S24; its query log was not retained, so Coursera searches are not enumerated.
- *Searches.* 52 web searches in the main session, covering the credential bodies, accreditation (IAS), consumer and regulator actions, universities, Indian programmes, recognition programmes, platform ownership, job boards, and partner and employer channels.
- *Hiring sample (FALSIFY move).* I drew a convenience sample of GenAI postings with search-engine `site:` queries on Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby, Workable, SmartRecruiters, amazon.jobs, jobs.apple.com, foundit, hirist, cutshort and similar boards, using queries such as "Generative AI Engineer", "GenAI Engineer Bengaluru", "Solutions Architect generative AI", "AI governance manager", "AI security red team" and "ML platform engineer", for the US and for India, on 2026-11-13. 100 posting-page fetches were attempted and 30 returned usable text (US n=15 and India n=14 are counted; one Dublin posting is excluded from both counts). Another 27 attempts were refused because a usage limit had been reached, so a planned second wave (AI security, evaluation, infrastructure and further India roles) was not read; the rest were listing pages, script shells, closed or missing pages, or addresses the tool would not fetch. A fixed prompt asked the fetch tool to quote any sentence naming a certification, certificate, credential or badge, or any of Coursera, edX, Udacity, DataCamp, NPTEL, NASSCOM, USAII, ARTiBA, DASCA, IABAC, Blockchain Council, GSDC, AI CERTs, MVP, Google Developer Expert, AWS Hero or AWS Community Builder. I did not read every posting in full myself: the extraction ran through the tool's summariser, so recall is unverified. The postings are listed in section 7.1 so that a maintainer can repeat the audit.
- *Other falsification searches.* Resume-audit experiments on certificates; regulator and court actions naming upGrad, Great Learning, Simplilearn, Emeritus, USAII, ARTiBA, DASCA, IABAC, Blockchain Council or GSDC; tenders naming USAII, ARTiBA or NASSCOM "certified resource" clauses; employer statements from IT-services firms.
- *Blocked or failed.* usaii.org, dasca.org, certnexus.com, mvp.microsoft.com, the AWS Community Builders page on aws.amazon.com, professional.mit.edu, futureskillsprime.com, nptel.ac.in sub-pages, the Coursera India Plus page and help centre, the National Consumer Helpline dashboard (login wall) and constructed Trustpilot addresses were refused or unreadable. They are listed in the ledger as Blocked and in Absences.

**Confidence.** Medium for issuer facts (fees, formats, names), which rest on pages I opened; low for hiring signal, channel value and any ordering, because the sample is small, partner rules were opened for Microsoft and AWS only, and several bodies' own sites could not be read.

**What would change these rules.** (1) A reproducible audit showing any credential in more than 10% of GenAI postings for a market segment, with a sample large enough to exclude chance. (2) Partner-programme rules that begin to count platform, university or body credentials. (3) A public accreditor listing for any body in the group (IAS or another signatory). (4) A regulator or court action naming a body in the group. (5) Launches, renamings or retirements, such as the Microsoft retirements in KF11. (6) A published paper behind the China resume experiment [S26].

## Detailed Findings

### 7.1 Hiring-signal sample: method and postings

**Method.** Convenience sample, drawn on 2026-11-13 through web search with `site:` queries on applicant-tracking boards (Greenhouse, Lever, Workable, amazon.jobs, company career pages) and on aggregators (foundit, employbl, Built In Bengaluru, jobs.anitab.org, jobs.stripes.co, Jobgether), with GenAI role titles (engineer, applied AI engineer, solutions architect, governance director). Pages that were closed, listing pages, JavaScript shells or refused were not counted: US fetch failures were SmartAsset (wrong page), Censys and Rise8 (listing pages), Turing, Ro, Influur, Glean, three Apple pages (not found), CFGI, Auxia, Hive, Employ, a second Scale AI posting and four further Jobgether pages (site errors or addresses the tool would not fetch); India failures were two hirist pages (script shell), Atomicwork (not GenAI), enterpriseminds, Thoughtworks, Nomiso, Weekday, Cognizant, Capgemini (position filled), Apple India, Prophecy, Enterpret and further boards. In addition, 27 fetch attempts (Databricks and eight further India pages at 14:31 Asia/Kolkata on 2026-11-13, and a second wave of 18 US and India pages at 22:03) were refused because a usage limit had been reached. That wave covered AI security, evaluation, inference and infrastructure roles and further India services-firm roles, so those role types are absent from the sample. A closed posting whose text remained visible was kept as text evidence and flagged "closed". Fixed extraction prompt: quote any sentence naming a certification, certificate, credential or badge, or any of Coursera, edX, Udacity, DataCamp, NPTEL, NASSCOM, USAII, ARTiBA, DASCA, IABAC, Blockchain Council, GSDC, AI CERTs, MVP, Google Developer Expert, AWS Hero or AWS Community Builder. The tool summarises each page, so a sentence could be missed; recall is unverified.

**Biases.** Search-engine convenience sample; applicant-tracking and aggregator pages over-represented; Workday-based career portals (TCS, Infosys, Wipro) mostly unreadable; LinkedIn, Indeed and Naukri unreadable; frontier laboratories over-represented in the US set; governance roles searched deliberately, to test where certificates appear; closed postings included.

### 7.11 K1 and the experimental evidence

**K1 (confirmed).** The study is Athey and Palikot, "The Value of Non-Traditional Credentials in the Labor Market", arXiv 2405.00247 (first submitted 2024-04-30; version 2 dated 2025-11-05; a preprint, so grade B) [S25].
- Design: a randomised nudge to share certificates on LinkedIn among more than 800,000 online certificate earners in developing countries who have no college degree (a delegated read of the full text gives about 880,000 learners and a Coursera collaboration); employment was tracked for 40,000 learners.
- Results: new employment +6% (+1.0 percentage point); certificate-related jobs +9% (+1.2 points); the lowest employability tercile +12%, the top tercile negligible; complier effect 11 percentage points; counterfactual resume score +8.5 on a 1 to 100 scale; sharing rose by about 2.9 percentage points; no callbacks were measured.
- Fit with the claim: it covers Coursera certificates, learners without degrees and developing countries, and does not cover cloud badges, degree-holders or GenAI roles. I cannot see which studies the knowledge base cites, so I tested the claim's description of this study.

**Other experiments, which show "the one randomised study" is no longer a safe count.**
- AEA RCT Registry trial 17143: a China resume field experiment with 1,500 postings reports that advanced occupational certificates drew 42% more callbacks than basic ones; the entry was registered 2025-10 and the fieldwork ran from 2018-10 to 2019-02; no paper was found. Grade C (registry text) [S26].
- Ithaka S+R report of 2026-05-07: a choice experiment with 543 US hiring professionals, fielded 2025-12-10 to 2025-12-22; an online bachelor's degree lowered selection by 2.5 percentage points (4.9% relative); experience and job-relevant software skills mattered more. It concerns degrees, not certificates, and I saw no AI-specific finding in the part read (the first 100,000 characters). Grade B [S27].
- None of the three tests GenAI roles, university certificates in AI, or any credential body.

## Open Questions & Risks

**Open questions.**
1. Would a larger, systematic audit show any credential in this group above 10% of GenAI postings for a segment? About 100 readable postings per segment would bound a zero count near 3% (rule of three; M), and the audit should include AI security, evaluation and infrastructure roles, which this sample lacks.
2. Is GSDC's "ISO/IEC 17024 Accredited · PCB-168" claim true? The accreditor's directory or the body would settle it.
3. Is the Coursera Plus price US$399 or US$239 a year, and what is the INR price for India as of a date after 2026-11-13?
4. Do the AWS Generative AI Competency and Google Cloud specialisation rules count any non-vendor credential?
5. Do university certificates and institute programmes carry weight with Indian employers? Nothing in this session measures it.
6. Has Microsoft retired or replaced AI-102 itself, given the listed retirement of the Azure AI Engineer Associate (2026-06-30)? Another session owns that exam.

**Risks to the records.**
- Fees, start dates, enrolments and ACE credit change often; the credential records carry a 90-day freshness class (V2).
- The fetch tool summarises pages, so a figure could be mis-summarised; maintainers should check load-bearing figures (fees, validity, pass marks) against the cited pages.
- Hiring counts rely on a summariser's extraction, and recall is unverified.
- Coursera enrolments and ratings are Coursera-reported (COI); Coursera facts come from a delegated pass.
- The Udacity "AI Engineering with Claude" row touches Anthropic, the developer of the model that wrote this report; it is marked COI.
- Two usage-limit interruptions shortened the posting sample and the university and Indian-programme searches.

## Discovered Concerns

1. **A Coursera page promotes a discount on an exam whose credential has a listed retirement.** The Microsoft AI & ML Engineering page (2026-11-13) promotes 50% off AI-102, and Microsoft's partner page lists the Azure AI Engineer Associate with a retirement date of 2026-06-30 [S5], [S84]. I did not open the AI-102 exam page, so whether the exam itself was retired is unknown.
2. **Naming traps.** The Coursera "Enterprise AI and Data Engineering with Databricks" specialisation is issued by Pragmatic AI Labs, not Databricks [S14]. Google's "Generative AI Leader" exists as a Coursera professional certificate and, per DataCamp's list, as a Google Cloud exam; they are different credentials and must stay apart [S15], [S30]. Coursera's "Agentic AI" is a single course, not a certificate programme [S11].
3. **Unverifiable accreditation display.** GSDC shows "ISO/IEC 17024 Accredited · PCB-168" without naming the accreditor, and IAS shows no holder for that number [S58], [S60], [S61]. I make no allegation; the claim is unverified.
4. **Coursera hours.** Headline hours and listed hours differ on several pages (KF10), which can mislead a reader planning study time [S2]–[S8].
5. **Degree claim.** A news item reports a Udacity "accredited" AI Product Management MBA for under US$5,000, accredited by Woolf. UGC's notice on ed-tech and foreign-institution online degrees means an Indian reader should check recognition before relying on it [S34], [S78].
6. **Cross-group credentials surfaced.** AWS Certified Generative AI Developer - Professional and the FDE Validated and FDE Advanced levels [S85]; the Claude Certified Architect - Foundations exam named on a Udacity page (Anthropic; COI) [S32]; the SAS exams that the SAS Machine Learning Engineer certificate prepares for [S16]. These belong to other sessions.
7. **UGC alert provenance.** The 2026 press report of a UGC alert gives no notice date or number; the 2023-12-12 notice is the primary text [S78], [S79].
8. **Ownership transparency.** ARTiBA states no legal entity or headquarters and uses third-party operators for invoicing; its fee bundles learning resources with the exam [S52], [S54].
9. **Possible post-merger changes.** The Coursera and Udemy merger closed 2026-05-11; only the first part of the 10-Q was read, so changes to certificates, prices or Plus after the merger could be missed [S20].
