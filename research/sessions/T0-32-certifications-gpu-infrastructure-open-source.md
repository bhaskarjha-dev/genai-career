# T0-32: Certification Census 5: GPU, Infrastructure, Networking and Open-Source Foundations

This report is a dated census of the generative-AI-relevant certifications from NVIDIA, AMD, Intel, Cisco, the Linux Foundation and CNCF (including the Agentic AI Foundation and the PyTorch Foundation), Red Hat and Anyscale (Ray), with a hiring-signal sample of 47 job postings. The research date is 2026-11-13: every page was retrieved on that date, two days after the brief's anchor of 2026-11-11, and every fact is stated as of that date. `[S#]` points to a row of the Sources & Evidence Ledger. Grades follow the protocol: A is a primary page I opened (an issuer's exam page or an employer's own posting); B is an established secondary source or a sizeable dataset; C is third-party analysis, a single source or an issuer's claim of value (marked COI); D is memory, judgement or a single anecdote; E means nothing found; M is my own calculation, which inherits its weakest input; P is practice-curated. K1 to K3 are the knowledge base's claims, tested under Repository Deltas. Page dates are page-metadata dates (created or updated), not launch dates. Fees are in USD as printed by the issuer: no page read lists an INR price. The brief's evidence window starts on 2025-01-01; sources dated before it are flagged in the ledger and used only as history, partner rules or tender evidence, which is why the front-matter evidence_window starts earlier (it gives the earliest and latest source dates, as the protocol asks).

## Research Question

**Decision question.** As of 2026-11-13, which GenAI-relevant certifications do these eight issuers offer, what does each involve and cost, how much hiring signal and channel value does each carry, for which role families, and how do they compare, so that a job seeker can decide which to take, in what order, at what cost and when to stop (decision D-005)?

**Sub-questions.**

1. Census: what exists, with exact names, codes, formats, proctoring, fees, validity, prerequisites and status?
2. Seeds: is each of the 13 seeds confirmed, renamed, or absent?
3. Knowledge-base claims: are K1 (NCA-GENL active), K2 (NCP-GENL active) and K3 (CKA, status unrecorded) supported?
4. Hiring signal: how many postings in a sample I draw myself name each credential, for the US and India separately?
5. Channel value: which partner-programme rules, employer targets or tender clauses count individual certifications?
6. Rigour: hands-on or multiple choice; proctoring; public verification; exam security and dumps.
7. Skill alignment: which role families do the exam domains serve?
8. Cost to earn, currency (launches, renamings, retirements) and a provisional order within the group.
9. Falsification: does any certification appear in more than 10% of GenAI postings for a market segment?

**Scope as applied.**

- Every credential on the issuers' own listings that relates to GenAI, ML, data for AI, AI infrastructure, agentic AI or AI security, at every level, including beta and announced credentials. For NVIDIA I took the whole hub (13 exam codes), because the hub is the issuer's own list, and flagged the weaker links (OpenUSD, accelerated data science, rack and cabling).
- NVIDIA Deep Learning Institute (DLI) certificates are kept apart from exams. Kubernetes credentials are included because the brief names them (CKA, CKAD, CKS, KCNA), with KCSA added as the security sibling.
- A credential named in the brief that I could not find gets an absence record, not a credential record (one case: an "AI Power & Cooling" exam named only on an NVIDIA offer page).
- The brief's anchor is 2026-11-11; pages were retrieved on 2026-11-13. Anything that changed in between is folded in, and the date of every fact is its retrieval date unless stated.
- India depth: I looked for INR prices on every issuer page, for Indian IT-services certification targets, and for Indian public-sector tender clauses, and drew an India sample of postings.

**Method.**

- *Issuer facts (grade A).* 78 ledger rows, of which five were not opened (S24, S25, S60, S74 and S75). Exam pages were opened through a fetch tool that returns a model-written summary, so I arithmetic-checked every list of domain weights (all sum to 100 except NCP-AAI, whose ten printed weights sum to 98 on two reads), re-read the contested pages, and kept conflicts as conflicts.
- *Hiring sample (grade M).* 47 postings whose full description text loaded: US 26 and India 21, drawn on 2026-11-13 from employer applicant-tracking pages (Greenhouse, Lever), job sites (foundit, Cutshort, Wellfound, Instahyre) and two employer career sites, through 48 board-restricted and open-web searches by role, board and city. Strata: S1 GenAI application and ML roles (20 postings), S2 AI-infrastructure and inference roles (20), S3 channel employers such as integrators, OEMs and infrastructure vendors (7). At most three postings per employer per stratum. The sample is search-led, not random, and was not chosen for certification mentions. A posting counts only if its text loaded; 21 further candidates (JavaScript shells, bot checks, rate limits, redirects, landing pages) were not counted and no attempt was made to get round any access control. Intervals are exact 95% Clopper-Pearson intervals; they describe sampling error only, not selection bias.
- *Falsification searches.* (i) The sample itself: a certification string in more than 10% of postings would have refuted P3. (ii) Searches for a published mention rate for NVIDIA certifications: none found. (iii) Three searches for an NVIDIA announcement that NCP-AAI or NCP-GENL is open for booking: none found. (iv) A search for a partner rule that counts NVIDIA-certified individuals: none stated on the public page. (v) Searches for exam dumps, to test how far the rigour of each exam can be trusted (titles only; nothing opened or used).
- *Not done.* No posting probes for Cisco AI, PyTorch, Ray or Intel certification strings beyond the sample; no partner portals opened; no exam taken or booked; no page re-read after 2026-11-13 (a re-read of the NVIDIA hub on 2026-11-15 was refused by the fetch tool's allowlist).

## Recommendation

Certifications in this group are tie-breakers, not entry tickets: none appears in more than 10% of the GenAI postings I could read in the application and infrastructure segments (KF10). Their value depends on the hiring channel, and the only channel with documented rules is the partner channel (KF12). The decision rules below are judgement built on the evidence cited (grade D for the rules themselves; the evidence behind each is graded in the Key Findings). Each option carries its failure mode.

| # | Decision rule | Evidence | Failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | **Application-layer GenAI and ML roles.** Build a portfolio first. Take a certificate only as a cheap syllabus: NCA-AMAD or NCA-GENL (USD 125 each). | 0 of 40 postings in the application and infrastructure strata name any in-scope certification (upper bound 8.8%); NCA-GENL confirmed active, with five weighted domains [S2, S9, S67]. | Two to four weeks go on a multiple-choice exam that no sampled employer names, and portfolio work is displaced; an associate-level certificate may read as filler. |
| R2 | **AI-infrastructure, platform and inference roles at channel employers** (integrators, OEMs, infrastructure vendors). Take the CKA first (USD 445), then NCA-AIIO (USD 125); take NCP-AII or NCP-AIO (USD 400 or 500) only when the target employer's postings or partner programme name NVIDIA certification. | CKA is the only credential in this group that combines a hands-on exam, a name in two postings (preferred at Submer; required with CKAD and CKS in an NVIDIA posting) and a partner rule (KCSP); both certification hits in the sample preferred an "NVIDIA certified professional" without naming an exam [S33, S68-S70]. | CKA is not GenAI-specific, so it can be spent on roles that never ask for it; two postings name it (one outside the sample), which is thin evidence; CKA dumps are posted on general platforms (grade D), which can erode the signal; 24-month validity creates a renewal decision whose fee is not stated. |

| R4 | **Free credentials as low-cost experiments.** AMD ROCm Certified Associate (labelled free) if you target AMD GPU roles; Ray Foundations Accreditation (free). | AMD Associate is live; Ray accreditation is listed free [S43, S58]. | No sampled posting names either (ROCm is named as a skill in 0 of 47 postings), so the signal is unproven; AMD publishes no duration or passing score, and its Professional and Expert timelines conflict. |

**What would change these rules.**

1. A reproducible audit showing one certification in more than 10% of GenAI postings for a market segment. Channel employers (stratum S3, n=7 here) are the likeliest place to find one.
2. A change to partner-programme rules, for example an NVIDIA Partner Network requirement for certified individuals (visible only in the partner portal, which I did not open), or a change to the KCSP engineer rule.
3. NVIDIA removing the "(Coming soon)" label, publishing passing scores or retake rules, or listing the "AI Power & Cooling" exam named on its offer page.
4. AMD publishing the Associate fee, duration and passing score, or launching the Professional level.
5. Any launch, renaming or retirement among the 33 records (facts expire after 90 days; announced credentials after 14 days).
6. An INR price or an India availability notice from any issuer.

### 6. Rigour and verifiability

- **Proctoring.** NVIDIA remote exams are proctored (hub FAQ); eight exam pages say so themselves and five do not [S1-S15]. Linux Foundation exams are online and proctored, with live monitoring and recorded sessions described in a 2023-05-01 post [S26-S30, S41]. The AMD programme agreement says proctoring may be remote [S44]. Cisco's DCAI exam is proctored [S54]. Intel's MLOps exam is scheduled through Pearson VUE [S61]. Red Hat and Anyscale pages do not state proctoring.
- **Public verification.** Credly badge pages exist for Red Hat EX267 [S49] and Cisco DCAI [S54]; the AMD agreement names Credly [S44]. The Linux Foundation offers a Verify Certification page and its CKA page does not mention Credly [S26]. NVIDIA pages name a digital badge with an optional certificate [S2-S15]; the one account of the platform behind it (Credly) is a single third-party post, grade D [S23].
- **Exam security.** The Linux Foundation publishes a misconduct policy with sanctions, live monitoring and recorded sessions, and gives no incident counts [S41]. No incident counts were found for any other issuer (Absences).
- **Dumps.** A search for CKA dumps returned 17 links, all on general hosting or community platforms (Zenodo 12, HackMD, Substack, GitLab, a university e-portfolio, a help forum), five of them titled as dumps or practice questions. A search for NCP-AAI returned mainly preparation vendors (practice-test listings, training companies) and one dump-branded site. Titles only: nothing was opened or used (grade D) [S74]. A hands-on format therefore does not by itself stop leaked answers from circulating.
- **What a format can show.** A Cloud Security Alliance note (2026-09-25, grade C) argues that a multiple-choice exam cannot show production implementation, and applies the point to the MCPA [S36]. The same logic applies to any multiple-choice associate exam in this group; no measurement of how employers read these exams was found.

### 10. Provisional orders

Method (judgement, grade D; no weighted scores). I weighed five considerations in this order and applied them as judgement, not as a formula: (1) fit with the family's work, judged from the published domain weights, core before adjacent; (2) availability, so an announced or beta credential sits below the active credentials of the same fit and is flagged; (3) evidence that an employer or partner programme counts the credential or its category, meaning a posting that names it, a posting that names its category without a code (for example NVIDIA-certified professional) or a published partner rule, with postings counting for more because they speak to hiring; (4) a hands-on component before multiple choice; (5) lower exam fee. Salary effect and prestige are not inputs, because no evidence on either was found. The brief asks for an order only where at least five credentials serve a family, which holds for four families. The orders rank what exists; the Recommendation rules say whether to take any, and the two differ in one place: R2 puts the USD 125 NCA-AIIO ahead of the USD 400 to 500 NVIDIA professional exams until an employer names them.

## Data Appendix

The records below are the machine-readable form of this report: 33 credential records, 64 measure records and 26 absence records, one JSON object per line. Conventions:

- The `grade` of a credential record covers the issuer facts in that record (fee, format, validity, status and so on). The `families` and `channels` fields are my judgement (grade D) even where the record's grade is A.
- `null` means not established; the reason is in `note`. `proctored` is true, false or null (null means the page read does not say). `items` is a number or an object with `lo` and `hi`. `fee` is an amount with a currency; `fee_local` is null on every record because no INR or other local price was found.
- `ttl` is a freshness class: V0 (14 days) on 4 records; V1 (31 days) on 54 records; V2 (90 days) on 33 records; V3 (180 days) on 5 records; V4 (365 days) on 1 record. It is omitted on absence records. `as_of` is the retrieval date, 2026-11-13, except on 5 measure records that carry the date a published figure gives.
- `src` lists ledger identifiers (S1 to S78). Measure records carry grade M (my own calculation); intervals are exact 95% Clopper-Pearson intervals.
- Credential identifiers are lowercase with underscores.
