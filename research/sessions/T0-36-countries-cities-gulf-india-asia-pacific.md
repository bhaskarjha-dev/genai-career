# T0-36: Countries and Cities for GenAI Careers 2: the Gulf, India and Asia-Pacific

This session gathers dated, graded measures for a country and city ranking of GenAI careers across 14 countries and 23 cities in the Gulf, India and Asia-Pacific. Ledger rows are numbered S1 to S102, and [S#] after a claim names its source. Grades follow the protocol: A is a primary source I opened; B an established secondary source or platform dataset; C attributed third-party analysis or a vendor page; D memory or judgement; M my own calculation, which inherits the weakest input grade. The brief's temporal anchor is 2026-11-11; retrieval ran on 2026-11-14, so a few Levels.fyi pages carry an update date of 2026-11-12. Currency conversions use ECB-derived cross rates per US dollar (S43) and are indicative to about ±2%; no purchasing-power conversion is made. Tax figures are illustrative calculations, not advice. Where a source URL was not kept from a search snippet, the ledger says so, and the row is graded as an extract.

## Research Question

**Decision question.** Which countries and cities in the Gulf, India and Asia-Pacific give a GenAI professional the best mix of job volume, net pay after tax and rent, legal access for foreign workers, remote reach and ecosystem depth, and how does the order change for an Indian national, a generic non-EU national, an early-career candidate and a reader who wants to settle?

**Sub-questions.** (1) Demand and talent measures; (2) headline pay and software-engineer baselines; (3) net pay at a reference salary, with special regimes; (4) rent and cost-of-living indices, and disposable income; (5) access headline per country; (6) remote reach and time-zone overlap with India; (7) ecosystem depth; (8) 2025–2026 policy changes; (9) language; (10) provisional top five per lens; plus verdicts on K1 to K3.

**Scope as applied.** All 14 countries and 23 cities in the brief were searched; where a city had no data, an absence record says so. Four readings were chosen where the brief was ambiguous:

1. Level-specific Levels.fyi pages were not retrieved, so the "senior AI-engineer median" is approximated by the 75th percentile of the all-level ML Engineer distribution (reference R2), and the all-level median serves as the typical-level reference (R1). Both are labelled as proxies.
2. "Net pay" is income tax plus mandatory employee social contributions. Employer-paid items (Australian superannuation, Gulf end-of-service gratuity, Singapore CPF for citizens) and voluntary savings are excluded.
3. A uniform US$100,000 gross case isolates the tax regime from local pay levels.
4. "City level" means a Levels.fyi city page or an aimlroles.com city count; Numbeo-derived pages supply rents.

**Method.** About 85 searches and page fetches on 2026-11-14 across: Levels.fyi (16 location pages), aimlroles.com (employer ATS feeds), JobsPipe, JobRise, the Stanford AI Index 2026 economy chapter (primary PDF), Singapore's Ministry of Manpower, Abu Dhabi's Department of Economic Development, Immigration New Zealand, Malaysia's MIDA and MDEC, PwC Worldwide Tax Summaries, and Numbeo-derived cost pages. A Python script computed tax from the schedules in the Detailed Findings. Sample sizes are given where a source states them (AmbitionBox n = 22,571 and 5,956; Indeed Australia n = 110; Glassdoor Riyadh n = 9); Levels.fyi pages did not display n.

**Falsification searches (five).** (i) K3's "India highest" against JobsPipe's full country table and Indeed Hiring Lab: Singapore is higher. (ii) Numbeo against asking rents in Dubai: one agency says Numbeo understates by 25–40%. (iii) Cross-platform posting counts: ratios between platforms vary from 1.4× to 3.7×. (iv) Levels.fyi sample stability: UAE ML Engineer medians read AED 144,020 on 2026-10-16 and AED 242,002 on 2026-11-14. (v) Japan's "PR in 1–3 years" claim against reports of tightened PR guidelines in February 2026.

## Recommendation

1. **Anchor pay expectations on the right population.** An India-based reader should treat Levels.fyi medians (₹44 lakh for Bengaluru ML engineers) as the pay of employers that report to Levels.fyi, such as big tech, labs and well-funded start-ups, and ₹11–13 lakh as the broad market [S3] [S4]. *Failure mode:* negotiating towards a Levels.fyi figure at an employer outside its sample.
2. **For the highest net pay with a clear legal route, target Singapore on an Employment Pass.** The EP floor is S$5,600 a month (S$6,000 from 2027-01-01), and at the Levels.fyi median tax is about 8.8%, leaving about US$116k [S44] [S15] [S42]. Use ONE Pass only if fixed pay reaches S$30,000 a month, or from 2027-01-28 on the AI and Tech track if the employer qualifies [S1]. *Failure mode:* EP and COMPASS rule changes, an employer-bound pass, a discretionary PR process, and rent near US$32k a year.
3. **Treat the Gulf as a tax play, not a pay play.** Zero personal income tax is documented for Saudi Arabia, Qatar and Bahrain [S38]–[S40], but median pay is lower and noisier, and Dubai rent absorbs much of the gain (US$37.8k kept at the UAE median against US$83.7k in Singapore) [S17] [S75]. Aim for sovereign-backed or lab-grade packages near the UAE 75th percentile (AED 360,049). *Failure mode:* no permanent-residence route for ordinary workers, residence tied to the employer, and thin pay data.
4. **For settlement, rank Australia and New Zealand first, then Japan, South Korea and Hong Kong.** Australia has points-tested and employer-sponsored PR and a 3,000-place MATES scheme for Indians [S70]–[S73]; New Zealand opened two skilled residence pathways on 2026-08-24 [S50]. *Failure mode:* Australian tax of about 28% at the median, Core Skills processing of 7–10 months, wage multiples in New Zealand, tightened Japanese PR guidelines, and Korean language and tax-regime sunsets.
5. **Do not plan on remote hiring into these countries from India.** Remote shares on employer sites are low (1.6% Singapore, 4.4% India) and unmeasured elsewhere. Overlap is best with the UAE (7.5 hours) and Singapore (6.5 hours). *Failure mode:* contractor or employer-of-record classification, tax residency and exchange-control issues that this session did not research.
6. **Early-career readers should start with India's volume cities (Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune), then Singapore and Japan.** JobsPipe shows entry-level shares of AI roles of 21.2% (India), 25.7% (Singapore) and 27.2% (Japan) [S6]. *Failure mode:* other India samples show far fewer junior roles (4.3% of live tech postings; one entry-level title among 158 Data and AI listings) [S13] [S102], and Singapore's EP floor can exceed junior offers.
7. **Re-check dated rule changes before acting:** Singapore 2027-01-01 and 2027-01-28; Malaysia 2027-01-01; Korea 2026-12-31; Japan's PR transition to 2027-03.

**What would change this advice:** posting or pay data with disclosed n for Qatar, Bahrain, Hong Kong and New Zealand; changes to the thresholds above; a change in Gulf or Korean tax treatment; and hiring shifts by large AI employers (for example further lab build-outs in Singapore).

## Open Questions & Risks

**Strongest objections an expert would raise, with the evidence found.**

1. *Levels.fyi medians are self-selected (big tech, funded labs), so country ranks reflect who reports.* For: K2's 3.4–3.9× gap; Australia's total compensation of A$178k against posted base of A$130–135k; UAE readings from AED 144k to AED 242k; Hong Kong and New Zealand percentile lists collapse to a few reports. Against: Robert Walters' 2026 Japan survey (ML engineer base of ¥8–15m in one East Japan table) brackets Levels.fyi's Tokyo median and 75th percentile (¥8.1m and ¥14.7m), and no source found contradicts the Singapore or Seoul figures. Net: use Levels.fyi for relative ranking among well-funded employers, not for offer benchmarks at other employers.
2. *Gulf net pay is understated because employers add housing, schooling, flights and end-of-service gratuity.* For: allowances and gratuity are excluded here. Against: the UAE Levels.fyi base median (AED 228,001) is 94% of total compensation, so equity and bonus are small; allowances were not measured. Net: the Gulf figures may be low by an unmeasured amount.
3. *Posting counts are not demand.* For: JobRise's October counts are 1.4–3.7× aimlroles', and JobRise's own Singapore snapshot (760 open listings) is below its October AI and ML count (1,249). Against: within one source the ordering (India, Singapore, China, Australia) is stable across aimlroles and JobRise. Net: use ratios within one source only.
4. *Numbeo rents are unreliable, especially in Dubai.* For: an agency blog says Numbeo understates Dubai asking rents by 25–40%; other Singapore captures read higher than S$3,419; the Sydney, Mumbai and Shanghai rents are 2025 data. Against: the after-rent order is robust: with Dubai rent 40% higher the UAE median keeps about US$26.6k, and with Singapore rent 30% higher Singapore keeps about US$74.1k. Net: the ranking survives the error; the absolute figures do not.
5. *The 75th percentile is not "senior".* For: all-level distributions mix levels. Against: no level-specific page was retrieved; R2 is labelled a proxy. Net: treat R2 as an upper-typical case.
6. *Recalled tax schedules for Taiwan, Malaysia, New Zealand and China may be wrong.* For: grade D. Against: a five-point error in China's effective rate would move Beijing's net by about US$4.9k, enough to reorder it against Korea and the UAE but not against Singapore or Australia. Net: China's third place in lens 1 is the most exposed.
7. *ONE Pass and the Golden Visa are out of reach for most readers.* For: S$30,000 a month is S$360,000 a year, above Singapore's Levels.fyi 90th percentile (SGD 279,389), and AED 30,000 a month is about the UAE 75th percentile for total pay (AED 360,049). Against: none found. Net: the Employment Pass and other routes serve most readers.
8. *Conflict of interest.* Anthropic is the author's developer and is named in the ecosystem rows. Those rows rest on press reports and the company's own post and are marked COI.

**Other risks.**

- The brief's temporal anchor (2026-11-11) differs from the retrieval date (2026-11-14); some Levels.fyi pages carry an update of 2026-11-12.
- Pay medians (V3) go stale after 180 days; thresholds (V4) change on the dates in section 8.
- Exchange rates are an undated republication of ECB rates plus a May 2026 Taiwan close; a 2% error moves net pay by about 2%.
- The ledger has rows without URLs (S31, S42, S43, S44, S101) because snippets were not kept; maintainers can re-find them by title.
- Tax calculations omit reliefs, bonuses' timing, residency tests, double-taxation treaties and social-security agreements, and do not model Indian nationals' tax status after leaving India.
- Open: how far Levels.fyi's Gulf data include sovereign-backed employers' packages; whether Korea's flat 19% regime is extended beyond 2026-12-31.

## Discovered Concerns

1. The knowledge base's relocation corridors for this region (ONE Pass and the UAE Golden Visa only) sit at the top of the pay distribution: S$360,000 a year against a Singapore ML Engineer 90th percentile of SGD 279,389 [S1] [S15], and AED 360,000 a year against a UAE 75th percentile of AED 360,049 [S2] [S17]. Employment Pass, Malaysia EP, Hong Kong GEP and TTPS, Australia 482 and MATES, Japan HSP and Korea K-Tech Pass reach more readers.
2. Title population matters: the Levels.fyi "AI Engineer" median is 55% of the "ML Engineer" median in Singapore and 46–48% in Bengaluru [S15] [S16] [S3] [S31]. The knowledge base should store pay by title.
3. Saudi Arabia's 2025 private AI investment (US$2.03bn) exceeds Singapore's (US$1.82bn), while the UAE is absent from the AI Index top-15 chart despite large sovereign programmes; the Quid method probably misses state-backed investment [S9].
4. aimlroles.com counts include postings at university sites (Singapore city labels are campus names), which may overstate employer demand [S8].
5. JobRise's pages are internally inconsistent on Singapore (760 open listings against 1,249 AI and ML titled listings) [S10] [S12].
6. South Korea's flat 19% foreigner tax regime covers employment starting by 2026-12-31 and the K-Tech Pass offers a 50% reduction for 10 years; neither is in the net-pay tables, so Korea's net pay for qualifying engineers could be higher [S96] [S42].
7. Japan's PR guidelines were reported as tightened in February 2026, which weakens the "PR in 1–3 years" claim for the settlement lens [S97].
8. Gulf News and vendor pages report Saudi, Qatar and UAE residency details that I could not match to official portals [S58].
