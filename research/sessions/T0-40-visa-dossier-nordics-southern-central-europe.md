## Research Question

**Decision question.** For a GenAI professional who wants to work in Sweden, Denmark, Finland, Norway, Spain, Portugal, Poland, Estonia, Austria and Italy, which legal routes to work exist at 2026-11-13, what does each require, cost and take, how likely is an employer to use it for an AI role, and what leads to permanent residence? It informs D-007 and `career/11-tracks/visa-and-relocation.md`.

**Sub-questions.** (1) Salary thresholds and effective dates; (2) changes since 2025-01-01; (3) fees and processing; (4) paths to permanent residence and citizenship; (5) employer reality; (6) Indian nationals; (7) remote work while resident; (8) the best two or three routes per country and a line for each of three readers.

**Scope as applied.** All ten countries were searched in two passes. Salary thresholds, 2025–2026 rule changes, fees, official processing indications and permanent-residence rules are covered from agency pages for Sweden, Denmark and Finland and in part for the other seven countries; the India-side items (mobility instruments, social security agreements, apostille, police clearance, the EU–India trade agreement) are covered from official pages. Visa-centre operators and waits, degree recognition, employer statistics by occupation, founder, researcher and intra-company routes, and tax pointers for most countries remain Absences. Per-country reader recommendations rest on the thresholds and procedures found and are provisional where no official page was reached.

**Method.** First pass (2026-11-13): 19 searches in standard mode and 4 successful page fetches (2 further fetches were rejected because the URLs had not appeared in earlier results). Second pass (2026-11-13 and 2026-11-14): 35 searches and 83 fetch attempts, of which 58 returned page content (some URLs were fetched twice); the rest returned an error (URL not surfaced by a search, site error, robots exclusion or URL length) or a blocked page. Sub-agents were not used because their calls hit a usage limit. Languages: Swedish, Danish, Finnish, Norwegian, Spanish, Portuguese, Italian, Polish, German, English. The fetch tool returns an extract of each page, so figures were requested as exact sentences from the opened page. Domain-filtered searches worked for Sweden, Denmark and Finland; the filtered Norwegian search returned no UDI pages and an unfiltered query was needed. Ledger: 128 sources: 50 at grade A, 58 at B and 20 at C (vendor, aggregator or unattributed guide).

**Falsification searches.** (a) My recalled Danish threshold (DKK 465,000) was tested against the agency's history table: it is the 2023 level, not 2026 [S14]; the agency's pay-limit page confirms DKK 552,000 [S84]. (b) A Finnish Blue Card page snippet showing EUR 3,827 [S20] was tested against the agency's income page, which states EUR 3,937 for 2026 [S19]; the snippet is the 2025 figure. (c) Two vendors disagreed on Finland's path to permanent residence (about 2 years [S78] versus about 6 years [S23]); the agency page shows 6 years with 2 years' work history, or 4 years through income, degree or language, and no two-year route [S89]. (d) Two vendors disagreed on Portugal's D8 income floor (EUR 3,680 [S44] versus EUR 3,480 [S45]); no official figure was found. (e) The first-pass claim that Sweden ended the permanent residence permit as a main rule was tested against the agency's news item, which limits the removal to asylum-related permits [S82]. (f) The first-pass Single Permit claim (17 states, from a press report) was tested against the Commission's package of 2026-07-08, which has no such item [S105], and then against the Commission's press release of 2026-07-15, which lists the 17 states [S128]. (g) Vendor figures for Italy (about EUR 36,300 [S96]) and Estonia (EUR 2,092 [S61]) were tested against official pages and matched [S120][S98]; a vendor euro figure for Spain's teleworker visa [S33][S34] is not on the official page [S94].

## Recommendation

Decision rules for a reader who has not acted. Confirm every figure on its official page within 14 days of acting (D-007). The rules point to official sources, are not legal advice and give no estimate of approval odds.

1. **Test the salary against the decision date, not the offer date.** Sweden's floor moved with the SCB median on 2026-06-16 and applies at decision; Denmark's limit is re-set each 1 January and Finland's Blue Card figure was EUR 3,827 in 2025 and EUR 3,937 in 2026; Spain updated on 2026-06-01; Estonia's table runs from 2026-03-05 to March 2027. Failure mode: an offer that clears a threshold when signed fails when the case is decided.
2. **With an offer and a salary above the Blue Card threshold, ask the employer to file the Blue Card where it exists:** Sweden SEK 53,625 a month, Finland EUR 3,937 a month, Spain EUR 41,356.36 a year, Poland PLN 13,355.34 a month, Austria EUR 55,678 a year, Italy about EUR 36,300 a year (approximate; outside the entry quotas). In Sweden it sits outside the 90 percent rule and runs up to four years. Failure mode: the Blue Card thresholds are among the highest of the national routes and the card ties the holder to an employer (Austria: 24 months, with a 30-day wait to change employer); Italy's two official time statements differ (30 and 90 days) and delays are reported.
3. **Otherwise use the national employer-sponsored permit:** Sweden SEK 34,470 a month plus collective-agreement pay (fee SEK 2,200); Denmark DKK 552,000 a year (fee DKK 6,810; 1 month, up to 3), or the fast-track if the employer is SIRI-certified (1 month, up to 2); Norway NOK 599,200 for a Master's-level job without a collective agreement; Finland EUR 3,937 (Specialist; fee EUR 750 online) or EUR 1,600 (employed person, possibly with a labour-market test); Estonia from EUR 2,092 a month with the Unemployment Insurance Fund's permission. Failure mode: Denmark's Positive List has no AI, data-scientist or machine-learning title, Sweden's 75 percent group is not listed on the agency page and Finland's labour-market test was not checked for AI roles.
4. **Without an offer, the only job-search route verified here is Austria's 6-month search visa for very highly qualified people** [S68] (the card behind it needs at least 70 points [S101]); Portugal's qualified job-search visa exists in law [S36] but one media source reports it as suspended [S41]. Do not plan around any other job-seeker route from this dossier. Failure mode: Austria's points table was not retrieved and the search-visa terms rest on a snippet.
5. **Recent graduates:** Sweden lists holders of student or researcher permits as exempt from the 90 percent rule on a first work-permit application [S12]; Finland's post-degree permit needs net income of EUR 1,210, 1,090 or 1,030 a month depending on region [S19]; Spain's reduced Blue Card threshold of EUR 33,085.09 applies where the qualification was obtained within three years before applying [S127]. Failure mode: the Swedish and Finnish conditions were read only in part, and the Spanish reduced figure still needs a degree of at least three years at level 6 [S93].
6. **Remote-work visas are a weak fit for local employment.** Spain's teleworker visa allows employment only with companies outside Spain and caps work for Spanish companies at 20 percent of professional activity [S94]; Italy's visa and Portugal's D8 floor rest on vendor sources (C). Failure mode: a Spanish employer is excluded for employees, and the Italian and Portuguese terms were not confirmed on official pages.
7. **For an Indian national, check whether the destination has a listed mobility instrument or social security agreement.** Instruments are listed for Austria, Denmark, Finland, Italy and Portugal [S108]; social security agreements for Austria, Denmark, Finland, Norway, Portugal and Sweden [S109]. Do not assume either replaces a national permit. Failure mode: the instrument texts were not read, so what each gives an engineer is unknown (A2).

**What would change these rules:** the Swedish extension change for applications registered from 2026-12-02; the Danish collective-agreement scheme from 2027-01-01 and the pay-limit re-set each 1 January; any Swedish rule on permanent residence for work-permit holders; Portuguese ordinances defining qualified activity; the Talent Pool application date and platform; the Single Permit infringement outcomes; the effective date of Estonia's 2026 amendments; the Polish draft exemption; signature and ratification of the EU–India agreement.
