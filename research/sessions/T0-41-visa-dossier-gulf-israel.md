This dossier tests which legal routes let a generative-AI professional work in the United Arab Emirates, Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Bahrain and Israel, with a lens for Indian nationals. Citations such as [S12] point to rows of the Sources & Evidence Ledger near the end of the file. Grades follow the protocol: A is an official page, procedure or statute that I opened; B is a law-firm, media or NGO summary; C is indirect evidence; D is recalled from memory; E is a search that found nothing; M is my own arithmetic from graded inputs. The brief's temporal anchor is 2026-11-11; sources were retrieved from 2026-11-13 to 2026-11-18 and the file was assembled on 2026-11-18, so every rule is stated as read on its retrieval date and every record carries an `as_of` date and a freshness class. ## Research Question

**Decision question.** For a GenAI professional who wants to work in the Gulf states and Israel, which legal routes to work exist at the research date, what does each require, cost and take, how likely is an employer to use it for an AI role, and what leads to permanent residence? The answer informs decision D-007.

**Sub-questions.** (1) What is the route census per country, with threshold, fee, time, dependants and settlement path? (2) What is the employer reality: levies, quotas, statistics, employer statements? (3) What differs for Indian nationals: caps, bilateral schemes, attestation, police clearance, degree recognition, consular waits? (4) May a resident work remotely for a foreign employer? (5) Which rules are pending, contested or changed since 2025-01-01? (6) Which two or three routes fit a GenAI professional, and what should each of three readers do?

**Scope as applied.** Five countries. I read "GenAI professional" as a skilled foreign technology worker (software or machine-learning engineer, researcher, technical leader), because no official route names generative-AI roles; the dossier records where AI, coding or ICT is or is not named. Founder routes and remote-work visas are covered briefly. Tax is limited to one-line pointers; asylum, family-only routes and routes closed to skilled workers are out of scope. Where the brief is ambiguous I chose the reading most useful to a job seeker and did not ask questions.

**Method.**
- **Sources.** 87 sources are recorded in the ledger: official portals and procedures (u.ae, ICP, GDRFA Dubai, ADRO, MoHRE, HRSD and Qiwa, pr.gov.sa and my.gov.sa, Qatar's Ministry of Interior and real-estate authority, LMRA, NPRA, bahrain.bh, Israel's Population and Immigration Authority in Hebrew and English, India's MEA, the HCCH), plus law-firm, media and NGO summaries labelled B.
- **Queries.** English searches restricted to official domains to surface the official URL; Arabic searches on 2026-11-17 for the UAE Golden Visa salary wording, the Saudi Premium Residency criteria and Qatar work-permit fees (the two Abu Dhabi pages and the Qatar statute opened; the pr.gov.sa, Ministry of Interior and Hukoomi pages did not); Hebrew searches and Hebrew PDFs for Israeli procedures; absence searches by country for job-seeker, digital-nomad, remote-work, graduate, intra-company and points-based routes (2026-11-15 and 2026-11-17); employer searches for MBZUAI, KAUST, HUMAIN, HBKU, QCRI, Georgetown University in Qatar and Bahraini AI employers, plus the LMRA statistics page.
- **Reading.** Pages were opened with a fetch tool that returns a summary. Key pages were read twice (ICP guide, u.ae coders programme, procedure 5.3.0043, the Israeli statistics) with consistent figures. Pages that failed are listed under Absences.
- **Disconfirming (falsification) searches.** I searched for an official UAE AI-specific residence visa (found only a visit-visa announcement, a coders programme without criteria and older ICP wording); for the claimed AED 100,000 "nomination" Golden Visa for Indians; for a BHD 300 monthly levy in Bahrain; for a flat SAR 800 Saudi levy; for confirmation that Saudi Arabia abolished kafala; for a 2026 start of the basic-pay reading of the Golden Visa threshold; and for job-seeker, digital-nomad and remote-work routes in Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Bahrain and Israel. Section 7 of the detailed findings records the results.
- **Sample sizes.** Not applicable to rule lookup. Processing times are stated service standards, not measured medians. Official usage statistics exist only for Israel (2018 to 2019).

## Recommendation

Decision rules for a job seeker, each with the way it can fail. Re-check every figure on its official page within 14 days of acting (D-007).

| R1 | With an offer, let the employer file the standard permit (UAE MoHRE, Saudi Qiwa, Bahrain LMRA, Israeli B/1 through an Israeli employer) and ask in writing for the fees, levies and timeline; in Saudi Arabia also ask how the Qiwa skill classification and Nitaqat apply to the role. For Qatar the official fee and time pages could not be read, so ask the employer and check the Hukoomi and Ministry of Interior pages yourself. | The employer's figures come from a vendor or from memory and differ from the official page (for example the Saudi levy has two tiers). |
| R2 | With a UAE contract, apply for the Green Visa if pay is at least AED 15,000 a month, the role is level 1 to 3 and you hold a bachelor's degree; apply for the Golden Visa if pay is at least AED 30,000 and the role is level 1 or 2. Before relying on the package, ask the authority or the employer's PRO whether basic or total pay is tested and whether a pay history or tenure is expected. | The authority counts basic pay only or expects pay history, so a package built on allowances fails the Golden Visa test; the role is classified below level 2. |
| R3 | Without an offer, the UAE job-exploration visit visa is the only documented entry. Confirm which authority's rule applies (ICP says "or", GDRFA Dubai says "and"), budget the refundable AED 1,000 deposit and the fees, and do not assume a right to work. | The 60 to 120 days lapse without an offer; overstay costs AED 50 a day (ICP); the pages conflict on eligibility. |
| R4 | Do not pay for a residency product to obtain work rights unless official text states them. The Saudi Unlimited Premium Residency (SAR 800,000, B) and Qatar's property residency (QAR 730,000) are described without stated work rights. | A large payment buys residence without a work right. |
| R5 | In Israel, pursue B/1 only with an Israeli employer able to pay at least twice the average wage (NIS 27,132 a month from 2026-01-01 per a law firm); a new graduate has a graduate route only with an Israeli degree in a high-tech field. | An offer below the threshold is refused; the 63-month cap leaves no residence path; the spouse work arrangement under procedure 5.3.0043 was stated to end 2026-03-01 unless extended. |
| R6 | If you are India-based, start document attestation before the offer is final: follow the destination embassy's instruction (UAE: MEA attestation, then UAE Embassy attestation), keep original degree documents, and ask whether an apostille is accepted. Degree holders are not ECR-stamped (page dated 2016-05-16). | A wrong attestation chain: the HCCH table lists neither the UAE nor Qatar, and India's accession carries an objection mark whose objecting states are not shown. |
| R7 | Treat these as unconfirmed and re-check them first: the AI-specialist visit visa, the coders Golden Visa, the basic-pay reading, the Abu Dhabi Green Visa nomination note, Qatar's five-year permit, the Saudi Special Talent criteria and High-Skilled thresholds, kafala reform, the Israeli spouse arrangement and the Overseas Mobility Bill. | Acting on a vendor or press summary of a rule that was announced but not launched. |

**What would change these rules.** An official statement of the Golden Visa pay basis; published criteria for the coders Golden Visa; the Special Talent criteria on pr.gov.sa; the Saudi High-Skilled wage threshold and points; an official Qatari fee and time page; notice of an extension of the Israeli spouse arrangement; the January uprating of Israel's average wage; the measures that the ILO framework for Saudi Arabia is to identify by the end of 2027; passage of the Overseas Mobility Bill; the 2027 step in Bahrain's fee schedule.

## Discovered Concerns

1. The repository's visa entry (K1) covers the UAE Golden Visa only; the other four countries and the UAE's other routes have no entry.
2. Vendor and press content circulates claims that official sources contradict or do not support (section 7). Any pipeline that ingests such content should test it against an official page first.
3. Many official pages are undated (the HRSD levy, the LMRA transfer and Golden work-permit pages, the MoHRE and ADRO pages), so the freshness classes cannot be applied without a retrieval-date rule.
4. India's emigration-check page is dated 2016-05-16; any repository statement built on it may be stale.
5. The HCCH table does not list the UAE or Qatar. A repository statement that an apostille suffices for Gulf use would need checking against each embassy.
6. Israeli procedure numbering and dates are inconsistent across documents: the PIBA list shows 5.3.0041 published 18.8.2024 while a tenth edition dated 6.9.26 exists, and 5.3.0043 published 21.3.2022 while edition 4 is dated 18.3.2024; the header of the 2018 high-tech procedure reads 5.3.0043 while its body cites 5.3.0041.
7. The fetch tool returns summaries, not raw page text. A pipeline step that saves raw text would let grade A rest on text a reviewer can check.
