## Research Question

**Decision question.** How can a GenAI professional in India earn a foreign employer's pay without relocating, and through which employers, structures and rails, at what cost and risk?

**Reading applied.** "Cross-border" means a foreign-headquartered employer and an India-resident worker. Two cases are separated because the playbook compares them: (a) paid at foreign bands through an EOR or contractor structure; (b) paid at Indian bands by the employer's Indian entity ("Remote – India"). "GenAI roles" means AI/ML engineer (LLM), applied AI, forward-deployed AI engineer and AI-evaluation contractor titles.

**Sub-questions.** The ten coverage items in the brief, plus a refresh of K1–K18.

**Scope as applied.** Evidence window 2025-01-01 to 2026-11-11, with an older Levels.fyi snapshot (2024-05-18) kept only as a trend marker. Detailed tax computation and relocation visas are out of scope; rules appear as pointers.

**Method.** About 55 searches (standard mode) and nine successful page opens: JobsPipe, Fast AI Jobs, Deel pricing, Wise India receiving fees, Wise India terms of use, a Jobgether India listing, PostHog's compensation and hiring handbook pages, and the RBI home page (reached when a PDF request redirected). Sample sizes: Fast AI Jobs remote AI-engineer listing n=45 (my tally, M); Jobgether India filter n=416; WFH.team n=6,676; Seeker n=180,429 remote postings; JobsPipe counts unpublished. Falsification searches:
- price conflicts for Remote and Oyster, which surfaced Remote's $699 monthly rate and Oyster's $599 reading;
- Levels.fyi top employers, to test K1's "ceiling";
- whether an EOR removes permanent-establishment (PE) risk, which Deel's own guide denies for dependent-agent PE;
- India-open frontier-lab remote roles, which found local offices and geographic gating instead;
- the FEMA 9-versus-15-month conflict, resolved by RBI notification extracts and several law-firm summaries;
- competitor claims that Wise lacked PA-CB status, tested against Wise's own terms of use.

## Recommendation

Decision rules for an India-based GenAI professional seeking foreign-employer pay without relocating:

1. **Screen on location text, not the "remote" tag.** Keep postings that name India or "worldwide" and whose timezone window includes IST. Open the employer's own application page and read the country dropdown and handbook before applying: some remote-only employers exclude India outright (PostHog limits hiring to GMT+2 to GMT−8). Treat aggregator "Worldwide" and "anywhere" labels as unverified (KF2, KF3).
2. **Classify the structure before negotiating.** Local-entity employment: compare the offer with the Bengaluru P90 of ₹1.07 crore. EOR: compare with the client's total budget, not salary alone. Contractor: quote in USD per hour.
3. **Use the break-even floors (M).** About $39/h at 1,500 billable hours matches the Bengaluru senior median; about $76/h matches P90. Vendor quotes for senior India LLM work sit at $42–100/h, so a rate above $76/h needs senior, evidenced work. Above about $44/h full-time you also leave the presumptive-tax ceiling behind.
4. **If the client offers EOR or CoR,** ask that the fee ($599–699 per month for EOR; $325 or more for CoR) comes from the client's budget, not your rate.
5. **Rails.** Use a rail that issues e-FIRC or FIRA and holds a final PA-CB authorisation (check the RBI register, not the vendor's page). Keep each receipt at or below ₹25 lakh (about $26.7k); use a bank wire for larger invoices. Avoid PayPal for large invoices. Expect Wise to require proof of an export business and to convert to INR on receipt.
6. **Register and file first.** GSTIN and the annual LUT before the first export invoice. Realise and repatriate export proceeds within 9 months (the first proviso allows 12 months, which summaries tie to INR-invoiced or settled exports); confirm in the regulation text how the period is counted for services.
7. **Contract.** Insist on a written assignment stating "worldwide, perpetual", a schedule of pre-existing materials, milestone payments and a liability cap; do not rely on a non-compete either way.
8. **Fraud.** Expect and pass live identity checks; refuse any overpayment refund, off-platform payment or unpaid test beyond a small paid task; treat AI-training platforms as supplementary income only.
9. **Relocation.** Do not plan on a remote role converting to sponsored relocation; apply separately.

**What would change these rules:** a published GenAI-specific share of cross-border remote postings; any change in EOR pricing or Indian EOR law; any amendment to the FEMA export regulations, PA-CB rules or Income-tax Act 2025 rules; the RBI foreign-exchange measures listed on its home page on 2026-11-12 (not opened).

### DF9. Payment and contract fraud: checklist and platform record

1. Overpayment or "refund the difference" requests, where the original payment or cheque later fails. The FTC says no honest employer sends a cheque to deposit and then asks you to send part of it on [S128][S129].
2. Requests to move chat or payment off-platform, which voids platform protection (Upwork's own warnings, as summarised by C-grade guides) [S69].
3. Unpaid test projects that are the real deliverable.
4. Upfront "ID", "compliance" or "equipment" fees.
5. Brand-impersonating clients with no verifiable spend.
6. Chargebacks or disputes after delivery. Wise's terms make the receiver liable for the full amount plus fees if a payment is reversed or charged back, and allow recovery through a negative balance [S112]; marketplaces can reverse paid amounts (C).
7. Phishing for PAN, bank or identity details; voice-recording requests.
8. Fake escrow or "trusted payment" services controlled by the client.
9. Task or "pay-to-get-paid" schemes. Secondary reports cite FTC data: US job-scam losses rose from $90m (2020) to $501m (2024), and Q4 2025 alone was $150.4m from 25,002 reports (C; the FTC Data Spotlight was not opened) [S69].
10. Contracts without written IP, milestones or payment terms.
11. Verify the employer's domain and ATS page independently of the recruiter message.
12. Employer-side identity checks are rising: DOJ laptop-farm prosecutions and a forecast of one in four fake candidate profiles by 2028 [S70][S71][S72]. Treat verification as normal, not as a red flag.

**Platform and provider record.** Misclassification suits against AI-training firms [S55]; a proposed class action against Mercor for about 30,000 professionals [S56]; reports of an April 2026 Mercor breach (C) [S58]; Rippling v. Deel: the court let core trade-secret and RICO claims proceed on 2026-02-23 and, on 2026-09-25/26, refused to strike a key witness and sent Deel's most serious counterclaims to arbitration [S73].
