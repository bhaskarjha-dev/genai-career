## Research Question

**Decision question.** What do GenAI roles pay in India, by city, level, employer type and role; how are Indian offers structured; and how should an India-based candidate negotiate? The answer informs D-003 and D-006 and four knowledge-base outputs (pay benchmarks, negotiation playbook, India remote playbook, role scores).

**Sub-questions.** (1) Pay by role, level and city. (2) Pay by employer type. (3) Offer structure: CTC components, bonuses, ESOPs, provident fund, gratuity, in-hand pay. (4) Increments and switch hikes. (5) Negotiation norms: notice, counter-offers, reneging, salary-history questions. (6) Senior and leadership pay, and local versus remote. (7) Data quality of each source. Six knowledge-base claims (K1–K6) were tested.

**Reading chosen.** Where Levels.fyi has no title page for a GenAI role, the nearest adjacent title is reported and labelled as a proxy; roles with nothing are listed as unmeasured, never filled from memory. Every figure states its population (all employers on a platform, a named employer, a survey panel) and what it includes (total compensation, CTC, fixed, base). Cities in scope were Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune, Delhi NCR (Gurgaon, Noida, New Delhi), Chennai and Mumbai. Other hubs were not measured.

**Method.** The author ran 17 searches (16 standard, one extended) and 27 successful page fetches; a further 15 fetch attempts were refused as off-allowlist URLs, and one fetch that failed on a usage limit was retried successfully. Thirteen research sweeps (12 by Haiku subagents, one resumed Sonnet sweep for Levels.fyi core pages) ran 8–31 tool calls each. A first run of ten parallel sweeps was cut off by a usage limit before it saved anything; the work was repeated in smaller sweeps, which reduced coverage (see Absences). Load-bearing sources for K1–K6 and the main surveys (Levels.fyi, Aon, Indeed, Flexiple, Business Standard, TeamLease via IANS, WiseMonk, the PIB press note, the Supreme Court judgment) were re-opened by the author. Levels.fyi figures were cross-checked across URL variants (slug and language) and dates.

**Falsification searches.** (a) Sources saying AI roles carry no pay premium in India: none found. (b) Sources saying increments were flat or falling: The Wire (2025-10-08) and the OMAM survey (2025-11-19) were opened; neither shows a collapse. (c) Viral pay claims: nine traced (KF15). (d) K5 traced through four queries (three in extended mode) and four page checks (three by a subagent, one by the author) without success. (e) Levels.fyi variants compared for stability.

## Recommendation

Decision rules for an India-based GenAI job seeker. Each rule names its failure mode.

1. **Choose the benchmark by employer population before quoting a number.** For Big Tech, global product firms and large GCCs, use the employer's own Levels.fyi page at your level (Bengaluru, as of 2026-11-13: Google L4 ₹77.2 lakh, L5 ₹127.4 lakh). For Indian product firms use the employer page if one exists (Razorpay senior ₹40.3 lakh, as of 2026-11-11). For IT services and mid-market firms use platform bands (Indeed India ML Engineer ₹11.4 lakh average base, 2026-09-08; Flexiple Bengaluru ML Engineer ₹19.9 lakh median CTC, August 2026). *Failure mode:* Levels.fyi is self-reported, skews to product, GCC and Big Tech employers, and includes stock; a mid-market employer may anchor you low even if your level is high.
2. **Convert every offer into fixed cash, variable target, stock and benefits before comparing.** Ask for the written structure (basic, HRA, special allowance, employer PF, gratuity, variable, joining bonus, clawback). The Labour Codes' wage definition (in force since 2025-11-21) can lower in-hand pay at the same CTC. Use official calculators for in-hand pay; tax is out of scope here. *Failure mode:* structures differ by employer and can be restated after acceptance; central and state rules were still being finalised in 2026, and published illustrations rest on stated assumptions.
3. **Negotiate level and designation before base.** Level moves total comp far more than the AI label: Google Bengaluru L4 to L5 is ₹77.2 to ₹127.4 lakh (as of 2026-11-13). Treat "AI premium" figures as ceilings and ask which band the role maps to. *Failure mode:* IT-services bands are rigid, and a higher level raises the performance bar.
4. **Value ESOPs at zero until liquidity is documented.** Ask for the vesting schedule, cliff, post-exit exercise window, buy-back history and the valuation reference. Documented 2025–2026 events include a PhonePe buy-back of up to ₹800 crore (2025-09-12) and an Unacademy ₹50 crore buy-back priced below its last round, per its founder (2026-02-27). *Failure mode:* buy-backs can be small, selective or below the last round, tax falls due at exercise, and no sourced norm for startup vesting or exit windows was found.
5. **Raise notice buy-out and joining-bonus reimbursement before you sign, and read any bond or clawback clause.** *Failure mode:* the evidence for buy-out practice is a vendor blog (D); the only court anchor is a public-sector bond case, so a private employer's clause may be tested differently.
6. **Expect the current-CTC and expected-CTC questions.** Asking is legal; give an evidence-based expected range and keep documents consistent with what you state. *Failure mode:* consequences of misstatement are documented only by anecdotes and vendor blogs; a bill proposing a ban on mandatory past-salary requirements is not law.
7. **For staff-plus roles, expect the ceiling to depend on employer type.** As of 2026-11-13, medians above ₹1.2 crore appear at Google and Amazon (employer pages) and, on a USD leaderboard whose region filter is unresolved, at Uber, LinkedIn and similar firms; across all Bengaluru senior engineers P90 is ₹1.06 crore. Do not use vendor CAIO bands or rate cards as a market. *Failure mode:* the staff leaderboard shows USD and ranks only top-paying companies.
8. **Use posted ranges as a sanity check only.** One posting is one data point and the bands are wide (₹3–23 lakh in one; postings dated 2026-10-05 to 2026-11-05). *Failure mode:* small firms dominate the postings that disclose pay.

**What would change these rules:** employer-level n and title pages for research and applied scientists on Levels.fyi; a measured switch-hike study from Naukri, Xpheno or similar; final Labour Code rules and the EPFO ceiling decision; surveys of negotiation success in India; private-sector court rulings on joining bonuses and non-competes.

### Open questions

1. Levels.fyi INR pages for research scientist, applied scientist, mid-level, staff and principal roles, and for Noida and Chennai senior engineers, would turn the unmeasured rows in section 1c into records.
2. A measured switch-hike study (Naukri, Xpheno or a platform's offer data) would replace the recruiter ceiling in 4c.
3. Variable-pay realisation and fixed-to-variable splits by employer type are unmeasured.
4. Startup ESOP vesting, exit windows and buy-back pricing against the last round are documented only for three buy-backs.
5. Final central and state rules under the Labour Codes, and any decision on the EPFO wage ceiling after 2026-02-06, are pending or unverified.
6. K5 needs its original measurement, or the knowledge base should retire the claim.
7. India-specific data on counter-offers, reneging and notice buy-outs does not exist in the sources found.

### Risks

- **Freshness.** Pay medians and survey results are V3 (180 days), so figures dated 2026-09-08 to 2026-11-13 are valid to about 2027-03 to 2027-05. Pending items (the EPFO and ESIC ceiling decision, state Labour Code rules, the Shakti Samman Bill) fall in class V0 and should be re-checked within 14 days; the salary-history practice record carries V0 for that reason, and the ceiling values themselves are V4.
- **Reading method.** Pages were read through WebFetch extractions and most were read by research subagents running a smaller model; the author re-opened the load-bearing sources only. The ledger marks which reads were the author's.
- **Single-snapshot pages.** Levels.fyi pages move between URL variants and days (for example AI Engineer median ₹18.3–20.5 lakh); every Levels.fyi figure here is a dated snapshot.
- **Coverage.** The first run of ten parallel sweeps was lost to a usage limit and rerun at smaller scale, so employer, role and city coverage is narrower than the brief asked (see Absences).
- **Currency.** All amounts are INR. USD figures appear only where a source shows them, and the ₹95 per USD rate used in two calculations is an assumption.

## Discovered Concerns

Material findings outside the brief's scope, for the maintainers.

1. **Levels.fyi label and scope inconsistencies.** Per-level tables are labelled "average" while FAQs say "median"; widgets are not level- or location-filtered; a `.md` variant of the ML Engineer page carries the header "Location: United States" while showing INR figures and n=97. The knowledge base's K1 sample of 96 may match a different snapshot or an India-level page. Record the exact URL and snapshot date with every Levels.fyi figure. [S1, S2, S3]
2. **Staff leaderboard variance.** Two reads of the same leaderboard path (stamps 2026-10-19 and 2026-11-12) disagree on Google ($226,809 against $223,016) and on which employers appear; one read says no India filter was applied. [S11]
3. **Flexiple internal conflict.** The FAQ and table medians disagree and the n of 422 looks high for the platform. [S16]
4. **1 Finance premium.** The premium is not monotonic in experience (20%, 33%, 29%, 38%), so the article's framing of a widening premium is not supported by its own table. [S21]
5. **Deloitte page dates.** The page carries a header of 2025-07-17 and a body date of 2026-04-06. [S32]
6. **TeamLease rounding.** The headline says "up to Rs 1.08 crore"; the body says ₹108.6 lakh and uses "median" and "up to" in one sentence. [S22]
7. **GCC increment attribution.** The 10.4% GCC increment is attributed to EY's release and, in one agent's extraction of an Outlook Business article, to Michael Page. The attribution of that article's sector figures was not resolved, so they are not used. [S34, S36]
8. **Labour Code charges.** Q3 FY26 charges for the large IT-services firms appear as ₹4,000 crore, ₹4,645 crore and ₹5,400 crore in different headlines; only the ₹5,400 crore wire headline was captured, and none was reconciled. [S82]
9. **Labour Code details.** DLA Piper notes that the Industrial Relations Code and the Social Security Code appear to conflict on gratuity for fixed-term staff; DLA Piper and K&S describe the 50% rule differently; the Code text was not opened. [S46, S48]
10. **Pine Labs and Meesho dates and shares.** The Pine Labs page is dated 2026-05-13 and carries an "updated 2026-06-25" stamp; the Meesho article gives 67.6% and 68% for the same tranche. Meesho's ₹200 crore buy-back dates from 2024 and is outside the window. [S59, S60]
11. **Wipro article ranges.** The Wipro article cites TCS 2–8% and Infosys 5–8% hikes with no source; they are grade D and not used as anchors. [S39]
12. **Non-compete wording.** The commentary on the 2025 judgment treats restraints during employment as largely outside section 27 and is silent on post-employment restraints, so a blanket statement that all non-competes are void in India is untested here. [S52, S53]
13. **Vendor-dominated AI pay headlines.** Edtech, recruiter and vendor sources supply most "AI engineers earn 2×" or "₹ crore" claims (Scaler, Taggd, Second Talent, TheHireHub.AI); each has a commercial interest. [S19, S24, S25, S75]
