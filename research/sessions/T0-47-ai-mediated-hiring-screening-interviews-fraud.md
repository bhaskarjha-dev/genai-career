## Research Question

**Decision question.** How does AI mediate hiring from application screening to AI interviewers, employers' rules on candidates' own AI use, interview cheating tools and identity-fraud checks, and what should an honest GenAI candidate (worldwide, with depth for India) do about each? The answer informs D-009, `career/06-job-search/ats-mechanics.md`, `career/08-interviewing/interview-loops.md` and `data/tools.json`.

**Sub-questions.** Q1 what each major screening product scores and whether it can auto-reject; Q2 status of *Mobley*, *Kistler* and other cases; Q3 status of the laws in scope; Q4 AI interviewers; Q5 employers' candidate-AI policies; Q6 cheating tools; Q7 identity fraud and countermeasures, including India; Q8 candidate rights and remedies.

**Scope as applied.** Candidate-side reading; interview-loop formats and résumé formatting left to other sessions; laws treated as pointers with status and date. Where the brief's date (2026-11-11) and the session clock (2026-11-13) differ, I kept the brief's date as the anchor and flagged post-window items.

**Method.** About 90 web searches and 10 successful page fetches (5 further fetch attempts were refused or blocked) across vendor documentation, court orders and tracker pages, regulator and legislature pages, official advisories (FBI), employer pages, trade reporting and vendor surveys. Pages read in full: the *Mobley* collective-certification order (S1), the NYC DCWP page (S10), the Workday HiredScore release note and Thomson Reuters audit disclosure (S25, S26), Morgan Stanley's Eightfold bias audit (S30) and FBI alert I-101326-PSA (S56), all primary; and a law-firm state-law survey (S12), HR Dive's report on *Harper v. Sirius XM* (S6) and a litigation tracker (S2), all secondary. Everything else is Extract. Falsification searches: (i) vendor pages for any AI-score auto-reject (found one: Kula); (ii) the origin of the "75%" figure (the trail ends at a 2012 Preptel claim); (iii) surveys on auto-rejection (they conflict, 6–19% against 65%); (iv) whether K1's "only knockout rules" holds (it does not quite). Corroboration of fraud scale: FBI, DOJ reporting, Microsoft/Unit 42, Gartner and AuthBridge.

## Recommendation

Decision rules for an honest GenAI candidate. Each carries its basis, grade and a failure mode.

| Rule | Do this | Basis | Grade | Failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | Answer knockout and prescreening questions precisely and truthfully; treat them as the only documented hard gate. Where a question is ambiguous, ask the recruiter in writing and keep a screenshot. | KF1 | B | A misconfigured rule can reject on a reading you did not intend; you cannot see the rules. |
| R2 | Treat visibility under volume as the practical gate: apply early and selectively; make titles, employers, dates and skills explicit in standard sections so extraction and ranking layers can read them; never stuff keywords or invent skills. | KF2, KF4; about 254 applicants per Greenhouse job and 11,000 applications a minute on LinkedIn [S69] | P | No measured effect size; ranking layers differ; a low rank can still occur. |
| R3 | Where an employer offers an AI opt-out, choose deliberately. In Greenhouse an opt-out routes the application to manual review and deletes any AI score. | KF2, KF8 | untested | No evidence on whether manual routes are read faster or slower; keep a record of your choice. |
| R4 | In NYC, Illinois, Ontario, California, the EU and (from 2027) Colorado, ask in writing for notice, a description of the AI use, an alternative or accommodation, and deletion; keep copies; use the official complaint routes in D7. | KF7, KF8 | B | NYC does not require an alternative; several laws give no private right of action; enforcement is thin; federal preemption is pending. |
| R5 | For AI interviewers, expect a recorded, rubric-scored structured interview: read the consent text, request an accommodation or human alternative before starting, test audio, and answer with specific evidence. | KF9 | B / P | Transcription errors (accent, noise) and one-take formats; ask for human review if a score looks wrong. |
| R6 | Follow each employer's AI rule by round. Default to no AI in live interviews and unproctored take-homes unless the employer writes otherwise. Where AI is allowed or required (Meta pilot, Canva, HackerRank Chakra), practise with the employer's tool and be ready to explain how you steer and verify it. Ask in writing and keep the answer. | KF10 | B | Rules differ by team and change without notice. |
| R7 | Do not use undisclosed live copilots or overlay tools in assessed settings; use AI for preparation, mock interviews and review. | KF10, KF11; K9, K10 | B / C | You forgo a possible edge; I found no measured gain, and the stated consequence of use is disqualification. |
| R8 | Expect identity checks (government ID, selfie, camera on). Complete them only through a link reached from a known channel; match application name, email and phone to your ID; avoid VPN or proxy during the check; ask what is stored and for how long. You may decline, but expect friction. | KF13; K8 | B | Phishing look-alikes; false failures from name mismatch or device anomalies: ask for a manual alternative. |
| R9 | India-based candidates: assemble BGV evidence before an offer (Aadhaar/PAN, degree certificates and mark sheets, relieving and experience letters, payslips, EPFO/UAN history) and make résumé titles and dates match the letters. | KF13 | C | Vendor data errors; ask HR for the report and a re-verification. |
| R10 | Treat recruiter-supplied code and "tests" as untrusted: verify the recruiter's company domain and ATS link, and run tasks in a disposable VM or container. | KF14; K11 | B | Set-up time; use the platform's browser IDE where one is offered. |
| R11 | Never accept paid requests to interview, appear on camera, verify identity, host a laptop or share personal details or account logins for someone else; report approaches to your local FBI field office and at ic3.gov. | KF12, KF14 | A | None for the honest candidate. |
| R12 | Keep a dated log of AI help used in applications and take-homes; disclose it where the employer asks; do not present AI-generated claims you cannot defend in conversation. | Gartner: 39% of candidates used AI in applications (4Q24, n=3,290) [S58]; Resume Genius: 49% of hiring managers treat heavily AI-generated materials as a red flag [S66] | P | Over-disclosure may be penalised where the employer is silent; follow the employer's stated rule. |

**What would change these rules.** A ruling on the FCRA theory in *Kistler*; the class-certification and decertification rulings in *Mobley* (hearing 2027-03-09); vendor documentation that makes score-based rejection a default; Colorado AG final rules and the *xAI v. Weiser* motion; any further delay to EU Annex III duties; new FBI or DOJ advisories on candidate verification; employer policy changes on AI in coding rounds; DPDP commencement on 2027-05-13.

Two cautions. The Eightfold "scoring rate" is measured against each job's median, so about a third of applicants score above it by construction; it is not an acceptance rate. The impact ratios compare those rates across sex and race or ethnicity groups and say nothing about whether a recruiter reads candidates below the line.

**Adoption evidence (all weak; K3 remains a gap).** Morgan Stanley's audit shows about 1.33 million applicant records scored by Eightfold in a 12-month window (S30, M). The *Mobley* order records Workday's own estimate that 1.1 billion applications were rejected using its software in the period at issue, which the court treated as a rough, qualified figure and which is not a count of AI rejections (S1). Greenhouse reports about 175,000 live jobs and about 254 applicants per job (volume, not AI adoption) (S69). SHRM found 39% of organisations had adopted AI in HR and 27% in recruiting (S67). HBS/Accenture found more than 90% of RMS-using employers filter or rank first (2021) (S64). An earlier SHRM release (date not shown in the extract) reported 64% of HR professionals saying their tools automatically filter out unqualified applicants and 19% of adopters having had tools overlook qualified people (S67): it is a self-report and may mean knockout rules.
