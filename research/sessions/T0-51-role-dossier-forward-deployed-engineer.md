## Research Question

**Decision question.** Should the knowledge base keep Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) as a high-ranking GenAI target role (rank 4 of 24; supplied scores: demand 7.5, pay 6.5, durability 6.5, leverage 8.5), and which skill tiers, title aliases, pay benchmarks and entry guidance should it publish for job seekers in the US, Europe and India, from early career to staff level? The decisions are D-002, D-004 and D-015 (identifiers as given in the brief; their wording was not supplied). The door is two-way: each rule below can be reversed when better evidence arrives.

**Sub-questions.**
1. What is an FDE, and which titles and variants carry the label (K1, K2)?
2. How large is demand, how is it changing, and what is the employer, seniority and remote mix (K3, K5)?
3. What do postings ask for: skills, years, degrees, travel, clearance, sponsorship (K10, K11, K13)?
4. What does the role pay in the US, Europe and India, including equity (K4, K6)?
5. How do hiring loops work, and what is the AI-assistance policy (K9)?
6. Which proof of work, credentials and entry routes carry weight (K12, K13, K14)?
7. How does a career progress, and how durable is the label (K7, K8)?
8. How do geography and sponsorship constrain candidates, including LCA filings?
9. What belongs on a résumé or profile, and what should be left off (K15)?
10. Which misconceptions and risks matter, and what is the strongest case against the role?

**Scope as applied.** *Core* means an FDE or forward-deployed software engineer title whose text asks for building and running software in a customer's environment. *Variant* means an alias, a leadership title or an unusual spelling of the same work. *Excluded* means pre-sales, sales engineering, customer success or strategy-only roles that borrow the label. Regions are the US, the UK and continental Europe, India, and remote. Seniority runs from new graduate to staff, with manager listings noted. Employer-internal data, hiring outcomes and Chinese-language markets are out of scope.

**Method.**
1. *Demand frame.* The Fast AI Jobs role page was read on 2026-11-13 (page updated 2026-11-11) [S1] and its filter pages through search extracts [S2]. Growth statistics were traced to their carriers: Lightcast via Fortune [S7], Indeed via LeadDev [S6] and Indeed India via Indian press [S8].
2. *Posting sample.* 42 records naming 27 employer labels (24 named employers and 3 unnamed clients). There are 28 text-based records: 6 employer pages opened in full (grade A), 3 full reads of third-party hosts (a venture-firm job board, a Weekday-hosted page and a Wellfound listing), 11 employer-hosted pages seen as search-result text (3 of them also on a mirror) and 8 aggregator extracts. There are also 14 listing-level records taken from the newest Fast AI Jobs rows, whose requirement text was not read. Skill shares use the 28 text-based records only.
3. *Classification.* Each candidate was labelled core, variant or excluded. Two candidates were excluded and 25 more were dropped because they were closed, stale, unreadable or unverified.
4. *Skill shares.* A skill counts as required when the posting names it outside a preferred or plus list. Preferred-only mentions are counted separately and at half weight for tiering. At n=28 a share near 40% carries about ±18 percentage points, and excerpt-based flags are lower bounds.
5. *Tier rules.* Bands on the weighted share: Must at 35% or more (technical or role-defining skills only), Important 20% to 34.9%, Should 10% to 19.9%, Optional below 10% or segment-specific. A knowledge-base (KB) tier is held when the share lies within five points of a band edge, and any move is capped at one tier per cycle because the shares are lower bounds.
6. *Pay.* Posted bands, LCA offered wages, Levels.fyi and other datasets are reported in their own currency and period, without conversion.
7. *Other items.* Interview, credentials, proof of work and progression were sought on employer pages first, then in third-party material, with grades per source.
8. *Process limit.* Six parallel research slices were planned for sample extension. They could not run because the research agents reached a usage limit on three attempts, so one researcher did all of the work and breadth is lower than planned.

**Sampling biases.**
- Search ranking and fetch success chose the pages. Pages that load without scripts (Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby-hosted, careers sites) are over-represented; script-rendered pages (Palantir's blog and getting-hired pages, USCIS) were not readable.
- The frame favours US AI-native startups, frontier labs and Palantir. Big Tech and enterprises are under-represented: no live Microsoft, Google, AWS or Salesforce FDE posting was found.
- The India sample leans on mirrors, and two Weekday-hosted listings name no employer.
- Liveness was verified only where a page loaded with an apply control or a closure notice; most records were not independently re-checked.
- Posting text states wishes, not hiring practice, and survivorship favours postings that stay open.
- Nine of 28 text-based records were read as full pages; 19 rest on excerpts, so skill shares are lower bounds.
- Where the fetch tool returned a field extraction, the fragments recorded as verbatim are as returned by that tool and were not re-checked against raw page text.
- The `must` field of records read before 2026-11-14 holds condensed keywords, not verbatim quotations.

**Falsification searches (documented).**

## Recommendation

**Decision rules.**

1. **Role ranking (D-002).** Keep FDE at its supplied rank and keep demand at 7.5 (range 6.5-8.5), but score demand on engineering-embedded FDE postings only. Score pay as 6.5 (range 5.5-7.5) in aggregate and publish it split by employer type and region (rule 3).
   - *Would change if:* the engineer-titled share of the 50 newest Fast AI Jobs rows falls below 35% on two reads at least 31 days apart, or open roles at AI startups fall below 700; either would lower demand by one point (the thresholds are my judgement, D). A count of at least 1,000 engineering FDE roles at Big Tech and enterprises would raise the upper bound.
   - *Failure mode:* headline counts overstate engineering demand if title noise keeps growing, and a reader who applies to strategist, GTM or pre-sales roles under the label wastes effort.

2. **Skill tiers (D-004).** Adopt the suggested tiers in the skills table. Hold Must for python, apis, llm_apis and customer_scoping. Demote rag from Must to Important. Move retrieval_quality, access_controlled_retrieval, iam, domain_depth, executive_communication, rapid_prototyping, presenting, c2_tradeoffs and c4_responsible_ai to Should. Keep agents and customer_communication at Important as promotion candidates until the sample reaches 60 text-based postings. Add data_pipelines, cloud and typescript_js as Should, and sql, clearance eligibility and native language as segment-specific Optional.
   - *Would change if:* a full-text re-read of the 19 excerpt-based records lifts RAG or retrieval skills above 20%, or if Big Tech postings, which were not sampled, show different needs.
   - *Failure mode:* shares are lower bounds, so demoting RAG could mislead candidates for regulated-data roles (the OpenAI Healthcare posting asks for safeguards for protected health information); the one-tier cap limits the error.

3. **Pay benchmarks (D-015).** Publish bands by employer type and region instead of one median.
   - US frontier labs: posted base USD 180,000-320,000 plus equity (Anthropic 280,000-320,000; OpenAI 185,000-300,000).
   - Other US employers (Palantir and AI-natives): Levels.fyi total-compensation medians of USD 205,000 (all US) and USD 233,000 (Palantir); posted bases USD 120,000-200,000.
   - India: INR 2.5-4.0 million posted for 5-7 years; Levels.fyi median INR 1,674,888 total; Glassdoor Bengaluru average INR 1,699,778 (C).
   - Europe: no pay statistic was found; do not extrapolate from US or India figures.
   - Do not publish the USD 188K-against-145K comparison or the 0.4-0.8x equity factor until they are traced.
   - *Would change if:* a second Levels.fyi reading moves the US median by more than 15% from USD 205,000, a European band is published, or an equity dataset is found.
   - *Failure mode:* posted bands are ranges at posting time and not pay received; Levels.fyi figures are self-reported; equity is unpriced; only two labs were read, so lab bands may be unrepresentative.

4. **Access by background.** Leave the supplied access scores unchanged (backend 7.5, data scientist 4.5, frontend 7, non-technical 4, PhD 5). The sample supports engineers with customer-facing experience; no posting requires a PhD (0 of 28); degree lines are uncommon and six of eight accept equivalent experience; core postings ask for programming skill and none asks for a non-coding profile, while the strategist, creative, banker and GTM variants were not read for requirements and may suit non-technical profiles (D).
   - *Would change if:* hiring-outcome data show a different intake.
   - *Failure mode:* postings describe wishes, not who is hired, so the scores may still be mis-set for PhD and data-scientist entrants.

5. **Guidance for job seekers.** Apply to postings that state production-code ownership; screen out strategist, banker, creative, GTM and pre-sales titles unless they are the target; check sponsorship, travel and clearance before applying; early-career candidates should target new-graduate tracks (Palantir, Chalk) and 1-3 year India roles; experienced engineers should target lab Applied AI and FDE roles that ask for 4+ years, with shipped customer deployments to show.
   - *Would change if:* the share of engineer-titled roles keeps falling, or lab postings stop stating years.
   - *Failure mode:* strict screening can exclude legitimate hybrid roles, such as a forward-deployed solutions engineer inside a sales organisation, and sponsorship statements differ by posting and by date.

**Where the rules land.** `data/role_scores.json` (rules 1 and 4); `data/skills_ontology.json` (rule 2); `data/role_titles.json` (the alias table in item 1 of Detailed Findings); `career/02-roles/role-catalog.md` (rules 1 and 5); `career/09-compensation/pay-benchmarks.md` (rule 3).

### 1. Definition and variants (K1, K2)

**Definition (K1).** In the postings read, an FDE is a software engineer who builds, deploys and runs production software in or for a customer's environment, increasingly LLM applications and agents, and who owns the outcome beyond a demonstration.
- Anthropic's Applied AI postings ask for production experience with LLMs and for customer discovery [S22].
- OpenAI's Healthcare posting asks the engineer to own the solution end to end, with travel up to 50% [S37].
- Palantir's Colorado Springs posting describes 4-5 days a week on customer sites [S16].
- Three practitioner pieces draw one line against neighbouring roles: the FDE writes and owns production code, where a sales engineer or customer-success engineer does not [S35, S42, S43].
- PostHog's essay (C) gives 60-80% customer time, travel of 20-50% and 5+ years of experience; it is one company's view [S43].

K1 is confirmed. The posting evidence is grade A or B; the contrast with neighbouring roles rests on practitioner pieces at C. The verdict applies to the definition stated above.

**Risks.** Services drift (utilisation measures, endless engagements, learning that never reaches the product) [S41, S42, S35]; travel of 25-75% at some employers; dependence on clearance or sponsorship; title inflation that dilutes the label [S40]; exposure to venture funding, because 63.0% of cluster roles sit at Series A to D startups (M; the inference is D); opaque interview loops (item 6); and thin, search-limited evidence (Open Questions).

**Strongest expert objections, with evidence for and against.**

1. *"FDE is consulting with a better title."* [S41, S40]
   - For: 56% of the newest 50 rows are not FDE-titled engineers (M); services firms use the label at 5-12 and at 15+ years with executive-facing or advisory work [S38, S39]; KDnuggets and a16z make the case.
   - Against: lab postings pay USD 280,000-320,000 base and ask for production LLM experience, discovery and end-to-end ownership [S22, S37]; staff roles exist at Anthropic and Databricks [S23, S1]; Accenture's own listing says its role is not a consulting role [S16].
   - Reading: both are true. The cluster is heterogeneous, so score engineering-embedded roles only and screen with the item 1 test.
2. *"The growth figures are small-base artefacts and partly vendor-made."* [S41, S10]
   - For: KDnuggets flags small bases; Lightcast's figure is a floor and broader tech postings grew 13% [S7]; LinkUp counts description mentions [S9]; Paraform and Bloomberry figures carry a conflict of interest [S10]; roles per startup moved only from 2.70 to 2.85 (M).
   - Against: the platform count rose 29.3% in 103 days (M) and 7.9% in the 22 days to 2026-11-11 (M, from the brief's unverified 933); labs list staff and manager roles in 2026-10 and 2026-11 [S23, S1]; Indeed India reports about 19x [S8].
   - Reading: the direction is up and the magnitude is unreliable, so no growth multiple enters a score.
3. *"FDE is a career dead end or a services trap."* [S36, S41]
   - For: warning signs of services drift; travel; no exit data; a course vendor argues the case [S36].
   - Against: ladder titles exist up to Head of FDE; lab bases are high; the skills transfer, in KDnuggets' own conclusion [S41].
   - Reading: evidence on both sides is opinion. Screen for the warning signs and prefer roles where customer learning reaches the product.
4. *"The pay premium is overstated."*
   - For: dataset base medians of USD 172,000-183,000 [S3, S6, S10] and Palantir's posted bands of USD 135,000-200,000 [S16].
   - Against: lab bases of USD 280,000-320,000 [S22] and Levels.fyi total-compensation medians of USD 205,000 (US) and USD 233,000 (Palantir) [S4, S34].
   - Reading: untested, because the software-engineer comparator in K4 could not be reproduced. Treat any premium as employer-specific.

2. **Cluster definition.** The 933 and 1,007 counts are title-cluster counts. About half of the newest rows are not FDE-titled engineers, so scoring on the headline count overstates engineering demand.
3. **Missing and unverifiable claim text.** K8's wording was absent from the research specification, and the K2 alias "Frontier Agents Engineer" was not found anywhere.
4. **Levels.fyi India page.** The page header showed a United States label with INR currency while the URL and FAQ said India; verify before publishing the figure.
5. **LCA derivatives.** The two commercial LCA sites cover only page 1 of 5 for OpenAI and Anthropic, so counts are lower bounds; offered wages are floors and must not be quoted as pay bands.
6. **Untraced statistics in circulation.** Secondary sources repeat an 800% growth claim attributed to the FT, a claim that USD 9bn was committed to FDE capacity, a count of 224 roles at 39 companies and a claim that 68% of postings require travel. None could be traced; none is used. The dossier's own travel share is 46.4% of 28.
7. **Undated postings.** The three Anthropic Greenhouse pages show no posting date; their liveness rests on being open when read on 2026-11-14.
8. **Dated policy page.** Anthropic's candidate AI guidance was last updated on 2025-07-10, before the start of the evidence window, and may have changed since; other employers' AI-use policies were not found.
9. **Extraction method.** Where the fetch tool returned field extractions, the "verbatim" fragments in `jd` records are as returned by the tool and were not re-checked against raw text; records read before 2026-11-14 hold condensed keywords in `must`.
10. **Research capacity.** The planned parallel research slices could not run, so one researcher built the whole sample; a second pass should extend it to at least 60 text-based postings.
