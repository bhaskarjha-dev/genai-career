## Research Question

**Decision question.** What should a job seeker be told about what this job is, how much demand and pay it carries, which skills, proof and credentials it needs, how candidates are interviewed, how careers progress, and where the jobs are?

**Reading chosen.** I took "AI Infrastructure / GPU Platform Engineer" to mean the individual-contributor job of operating or building the GPU compute estate: clusters, host systems, schedulers, fabric, fleet automation and capacity. I excluded kernel and GPU-performance engineering, network-only roles, sandbox or runtime roles, research engineering, management, and application-side "AI production engineer" roles.

**Method.**
- **Searches and fetches.** 20 web searches and 21 page-fetch attempts on 2026-11-13. Search vocabulary covered "AI infrastructure engineer", "GPU infrastructure software engineer", "GPU cluster engineer", Slurm, Kubernetes, InfiniBand, NCCL, employer names, and India and Europe city names. Of the 21 fetch attempts, 18 returned a page, 2 OpenAI pages were blocked by bot detection (not circumvented) and 1 failed the tool's URL-provenance check.
- **Two passes.** Pass 1 built the sample from search extracts. Pass 2 opened as many sampled postings as possible on employer boards, which upgraded 12 to grade A, corrected pay and location fields, and removed one posting that had closed. `web_fetch` can open only URLs already surfaced by search, so ATS boards could not be enumerated. The sample is search-surfaced and over-represents large, English-language, tech-forward employers (frontier labs are 40% of it) and under-represents GCCs, small firms and non-English postings.
- **Candidate accounting.** 73 candidate postings were examined. 35 are in the sample, 1 is a supplementary duplicate (same job text, different requisition), and 37 were not carried: 22 were not this role (kernel or GPU-performance, network, sandbox, research, management, application-side "production engineer", generic Kubernetes platform), 11 were stale, closed or outside the window, and 4 were thin or replaced.
- **Falsification attempts.**
  - I searched for postings that make ML-modelling knowledge or a certification a Must and found none (ML-modelling knowledge is a Must in 0/35; 2/35 name any certification, both as preferred).
  - I tested the knowledge base's skill tiers against the sample: Git/CI, DevOps and Docker are not reproduced even at the highest estimate, while networking and observability rise relative to the knowledge base.
  - I tested liveness on employer boards: 3 of 16 conclusive checks found the posting closed.
  - I searched for India GCC postings and found MNC centre-of-excellence postings only outside the window.
  - I searched for a title-matching Levels.fyi page and for India pay by title and found neither.

## Recommendation

**Decision rules for job seekers.**
1. **SRE, production-engineering or platform background with Kubernetes in production.** Target variants A, B and D at labs and neoclouds. Add hands-on Slurm, the NVIDIA GPU operator and NCCL/InfiniBand basics, and put fleet-scale numbers on the résumé.
   - *Failure mode:* many postings ask for evidence of operating large fleets (for example 2K+ GPUs or 10K+ nodes), which a home lab cannot show.
2. **Linux kernel, OS-image, firmware or bare-metal provisioning background.** Target host-systems and node-lifecycle postings (OpenAI Compute Foundations Systems; Anthropic Node Infra), which ask for kernel, driver and firmware depth and no Kubernetes at all in the OpenAI case.
   - *Failure mode:* few such postings exist and they sit at senior or staff level.
3. **HPC or systems administrator.** Target variants C and D (HPC engineer, cluster engineer, SRE for research clusters). Lead with Slurm, InfiniBand, drivers, benchmarking (NCCL tests, HPL) and parallel storage.
   - *Failure mode:* lab and platform postings expect software depth (Go or Python, Kubernetes controllers).
4. **Backend engineer.** Target variant B (control plane, scheduler, platform). Some staff-level postings do not ask for GPU experience (Anthropic London Cluster Infrastructure; CoreWeave Compute Services), so Kubernetes and Go or Python depth can carry an application.
   - *Failure mode:* seniority bars (10+ years preferred at Anthropic staff level) and a screen for GPU specifics in the loop.
5. **Early career.** Expect very few openings (0/35 entry-level; about 1% on one platform). Route through adjacent SRE, HPC-operations or platform roles.
   - *Failure mode:* long search with low response.
6. **India.** Target NVIDIA India, Sarvam, AION, Soket and Deloitte USI. Ask recruiters for bands (0/10 post pay) and expect 6–10 years asked at AION and Deloitte USI.
   - *Failure mode:* GCC demand was not sampled, so the India picture is incomplete.
7. **Certifications.** Treat as optional. Consider NCP-AII or NCP-AIO for data-centre, partner and consultancy routes (Deloitte, Prime Intellect, Office Beacon), not for lab software roles.
   - *Failure mode:* $400–500 for a 5.7% posting signal.
8. **Before applying, confirm the posting is live on the employer's own board.** 3 of 16 conclusive checks found a closed posting that a mirror still showed.
   - *Failure mode:* some employer pages block automated checks, so check by hand.

**What would change these rules.** A title-level LinkedIn or Indeed count showing a different seniority mix; ATS-board enumeration contradicting the skill shares; India pay disclosures; GCC postings inside the window; or hiring-manager statements about proof of work.

### 4. Skills (n = 35; shares are bounded, not measured)

## Open Questions & Risks

**Strongest objections an expert would raise.**
1. *The sample is search-surfaced and lab-heavy (40%), so pay and seniority skew upward.*
   - For: no GCC or small-firm postings; labs' median midpoint ($360K) is almost double the neocloud median ($198K).
   - Against: employer-neutral platform medians ($175K; $171.6K) are reported alongside, and the Big Tech and neocloud medians ($209K; $198K) sit close to them.
2. *Skill shares are understated by truncated extracts, yet the full-read subset may overstate because it is a convenience subset.*
   - For: Terraform is named in 17.4% of extract-only postings and 66.7% of postings read in full.
   - Against: nothing removes the gap; both bounds are reported and the true share is unknown within them.
3. *"AI infrastructure engineer" is not one job.*
   - For: Slurm is named in 0/11 variant A postings and in 62.5% of variants B to D.
   - Against: GPU or accelerator work (83–94%) and Kubernetes (80–92%) form a shared core.
4. *Posted ranges are not pay.*
   - For: LCA medians for all OpenAI titles ($270K–$285K) sit below the top of posted bands.
   - Against: posted ranges and LCA medians overlap, and the top of the range is employer-stated on OpenAI's own pages.
5. *Aggregator counts include unrelated roles.*
   - For: the Fast AI Jobs category contains a fluids-systems engineer at a defence start-up and a systems engineer at a storage vendor.
   - Against: 446 listings under the narrower "GPU Infrastructure Engineering" heading are of the same order of magnitude.
6. *The liveness rate rests on 16 checks.*
   - For: 3 of 16 closed (about 19%; a 95% interval of roughly 7% to 43%).
   - Against: the direction matches a second, independent signal (the Anthropic Built In copy that outlived the employer posting).

**Risks.** Liveness is unconfirmed for the 22 sampled postings that were not opened (two of them blocked). Mirrors disagree on dates and some carry relative ages that cannot be anchored.
