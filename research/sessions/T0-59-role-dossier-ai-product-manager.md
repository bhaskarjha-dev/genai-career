## Research Question

**Decision question:** what must the knowledge base say about the AI Product Manager role on definition, demand, pay, skills, proof, credentials, interviews, careers and geography, and do its scores (demand 6, pay 6.5, durability 6, leverage 8) and skill tiers survive dated, graded evidence?

**Sub-questions:** what the job is and what it is called; how much demand and pay it carries (US, Europe, India); what postings require; how loops differ from general PM loops; what proof, credentials and routes apply; where sponsorship exists.

**Reading applied:** a posting counts if it is a product-management role (PM, Senior, Group, Principal, Staff, Lead or Director) whose product's core behaviour comes from an ML, LLM or agent system, or whose duties name AI-specific work (evaluations, model behaviour, agents, context, ML products). I excluded product marketing, product operations, program and project management, PM roles on billing, identity, IT or compute scheduling (even at AI labs), and postings whose body describes a different job.

**Method:**
- About 22 web searches and 14 page fetches on 2026-11-13. Five further fetch attempts were refused because the URL had not been returned by a search in the same session.
- Aggregate counts: JobsPipe, Fast AI Jobs, Weekday, hackajob, RemoteFront, Corvi. Pay: Levels.fyi, platform analyses, LCA aggregators. Loops, credentials and proof: prep sites, issuer pages, named practitioners.
- Sampling ran through a search engine with named-employer queries. This over-represents large, English-language employers whose boards are well indexed (Anthropic, Google, Microsoft, OpenAI, Mistral) and under-represents GCCs, enterprises, Europe beyond Mistral and Lovable, and non-English postings.
- Falsification searches: premium claims against measured pay; the "20% of LinkedIn PM jobs" claim; conflicting loop reports; AI-titled postings whose text describes another job; liveness re-opening of first-pass postings; and a search for AI-company PM postings that do not require AI depth (found three).

## Recommendation

1. **Search by employer type.** At labs and big tech search "Product Manager" plus AI surface names (Core Models, Gemini, Copilot, Agents). At startups, IT services and consultancies also search "AI Product Manager". *Failure mode:* label chasing, which misses most lab roles and surfaces mislabelled aggregator listings.
2. **Pick the archetype that matches you.** With 8–10 years of consumer or platform PM experience, AI-company roles such as OpenAI Shopping, DeepMind Discovery and Microsoft AI Copilot do not require AI depth, but show AI-tool fluency. With 3–8 years, build the AI-fluent builder profile that Sarvam, Lovable, Mistral and Uber ask for: a shipped LLM or agent feature, an eval artefact and a working prototype. *Failure mode:* assuming every "AI PM" role needs the same depth, or the same lack of it.
3. **Show evidence, not vocabulary.** Prepare one portfolio item with an eval method, a metric and the decision it changed, plus failure modes. *Failure mode:* demos without evals, or "vibe coded it" as the whole story.
4. **Do not plan on direct entry.** The sample has 1 entry-level posting in 41 and no 0–1 year bands on Weekday's list. Plan an internal transfer, a first-PM role at an early-stage firm, or a Technical PM title. *Failure mode:* months of applications to roles that ask for 5–10 years.
5. **Anchor pay on the right population.** Typical AI-skill PM base is $159k–$198k (C); lab bands of $240k–$490k fit 8–10-year senior roles only. In India the evidence is thin (₹18–46L IQR, C). *Failure mode:* anchoring on lab bands, on LCA filing wages, or on the 52% premium claim.
6. **Treat credentials as optional.** If you take one, AIB-C01 (business, beta) or AIF-C01 (technical literacy) after shipped work. *Failure mode:* certificate stacking in place of evidence; the AIB-C01 beta may change.
7. **Check location and sponsorship terms.** Anthropic and Mistral state sponsorship; Microsoft AI expects four office days a week; OpenAI three; Lovable's role is on-site in Stockholm. *Failure mode:* assuming remote work or sponsorship.

**What would change this:** ATS-level counts of AI-titled PM postings; full reads of the 17 undated postings; GCC and Europe samples beyond one or two employers; exact-title LCA counts; India AI PM pay from a named dataset.

## Detailed Findings

### 1. Definition, variants and aliases

From the postings, the job owns an AI-powered product's scope, quality bar and roadmap with engineering, design and research. Two archetypes appear [S9][S11][S12][S14][S15][S16][S17]:
- **(A) AI-fluent builder PM** (3–8 years; shipped LLM or agent products; evals; prototyping) at AI-native firms and model-facing teams.
- **(B) Senior product leader at an AI company** (8–10+ years of consumer or platform PM experience; AI depth preferred or absent) at labs and big tech.

By stack layer the variants are model-facing, platform and context, evaluation and quality platform, and application (section 11). Adjacent roles: Technical Program Manager, product marketing, AI engineer, solutions and deployment roles, product operations.

Sources: [S28][S29][S9]. **How AI PM loops differ from general PM loops (session probe):**
- They add an AI product-sense case, where the candidate sets quality bars for a probabilistic feature.
- They add an evaluation and technical-depth conversation: offline versus online evals, RAG versus fine-tuning, and cost, latency and quality trade-offs.
- They add a conversation with research or ML engineers about building evals.
- At AI-native firms they add a timed build.
- Classic product sense and execution rounds remain, and non-tech-native firms still lean on them.

Sources conflict on Google: one says the standalone technical interview was removed, another documents a technical round [S29]. A named head of product says his first interview asks for a portfolio walk-through to an end goal [S33]. The KB's "Product" track fits partly; extend it with AI product sense, an evals and technical-depth round, and a build round. No India-specific loop evidence was found (Gap).

### 14. Misconceptions and risks

- "AI PM" is not a protected title, and the two archetypes differ in what they require.
- A PM job at an AI lab is not automatically an AI-depth job (OpenAI Shopping, DeepMind Discovery), and many lab PM roles are not AI-product roles (billing, identity, IT, compute).
- Technical depth is not a coding test: programming appears in 2 of 9 opened postings.
- Aggregator counts overstate: about 60–75% of one checked page was not AI-specific; cached pages go stale; JobsPipe reports 9.5% of active postings visible for 45 days or more [S2][S6][S22].
- Title inflation: "Lead", "Principal" and "Staff" appear at small firms.
- Pay: lab bands and LCA filing wages are not typical pay.

## Open Questions & Risks

- Would full-text reads of the 17 undated postings change skill shares? Extract-only counts are lower bounds.
- Is the 22.8% AI-skill share stable? It covers only postings with disclosed pay (about 36% of PM postings).
- The label-noise estimate rests on one page of 20 listings and is wide (about ±20 percentage points).
- Staleness: 1 of 8 re-opened postings was down, so some unverified postings may be too.
- **Objection: a sample of 41 cannot size demand.** Agreed. It supports aliases, seniority, requirements and bands, not volume (KF1, KF3, KF4).
- **Objection: premium claims show a large premium.** The measured premium is about 22% on base pay. The 52% claim uses a different definition, and lab bands are not typical.
- **Objection: eval skills are over-weighted because labs dominate.** Labs are 12 of 41. Opened non-lab postings (Sarvam, Lovable, Uber) also require evals, but three opened lab and big-tech consumer roles do not. Enterprise coverage is thin (10 of 41).
- **Objection: prep-site loop reports are marketing.** They are grade C and conflict on Google; Anthropic's AI-use policy is grade A.
- **Objection: aggregator-surfaced postings inflate the "AI Product Manager" title share.** Likely, so KF1 splits by employer type.
- **Objection: archetype B roles (consumer PM at an AI company) are not AI PM roles.** Excluding J5, J6 and J11 moves the ai_literacy share from 46% to 50% (19 of 38) and the eval share from 27% to 29% (11 of 38) (M).
