# T0-60: Role Group Dossier: Evaluation, Security and Safety

This dossier tests the knowledge base's records for three roles against a dated sample of job postings, plus aggregate, pay, credential and interview sources. A first-pass sample of 51 postings (18 evaluation, 18 security, 15 safety) is the base for every skill share. Later searches added 19 supplementary postings (4 evaluation, 7 security, 8 safety), giving 70 `jd` records (22, 25 and 23). S-numbers refer to rows of the Sources & Evidence Ledger. Grades follow EGP-1.0: A is a primary page, B is platform or secondary data including excerpts of employer pages, C is single-source analysis, D is memory or judgement, E is untraceable, M is my own calculation, and P is practice-curated. I retrieved everything on 2026-11-13; the brief's anchor date is 2026-11-11. The 90-day window therefore starts on 2026-08-13 under the anchor or 2026-08-15 under the retrieval date. One posting (Google DeepMind, 2026-08-14) falls between the two and is kept and flagged. This file merges the first-pass report and its follow-up work; superseded records were replaced, not duplicated.

## Research Question

**Decision.** What must the knowledge base record for AI Evaluation Engineer, AI Security Engineer / AI Red Teamer and Frontier AI Safety / Alignment Researcher so that it can tell a job seeker what the job is, its demand and pay, skills, proof, credentials, interviews, progression and geography, with an India lens?

**Sub-questions.** Do K1–K41 hold? Do the skill tiers survive a posting sample? What do aggregates say about volume and pay? Is evals a title or a skill? How do fellowships and residencies work as routes into safety research, and how do labs compare with independent organisations?

**Scope applied.**
- Postings live or dated 2025-08 onward, with older or undated ones flagged. Other evidence is from 2025-03 onward; one MATS survey predates the window and is flagged.
- Roles are classified by what the job asks for. AI-for-security jobs (AI used to attack or defend, not securing AI) and contractor prompt-testing work are excluded and counted.
- "AI Evaluation Engineer" covers applied or product evals, lab model evaluation (benchmark design) and third-party evaluators.
- "Safety researcher" covers research engineer and scientist roles at labs, government institutes and safety organisations.

**Method.** 25 web searches in standard mode on 2026-11-13, over title families × employer types × regions, plus 7 page fetches: six postings opened in full on employer pages (Anthropic ×3, Microsoft AI, Backbase, Amazon) and one news article (Cybersecurity Dive). The other postings were read as ATS excerpts or aggregator mirrors. Skill shares are my counts over visible text for the first-pass sample (n = 18, 18, 15). They are lower bounds, because excerpts are truncated and skills implied by a title are over-represented. At these n the 95% interval half-width is about ±23–25 percentage points, so shares are cross-checks only. Postings added later (n = 4, 7, 8) enter employer, region, pay and freshness counts but not skill shares.

**Freshness rule.** A posting counts as "live or dated within 90 days" if I opened it live, or it carries an absolute date on or after 2026-08-13, or an aggregator showed a relative age of 30 days or less (crawl date not shown; low confidence). Everything else is flagged older or undated.

**Sampling bias.** Search-driven sampling over-represents large, English-language employers and aggregator-mirrored postings. Europe is thin, non-English postings were not searched, mirrors lag employer pages, and many postings are undated.

**Falsification searches.**
1. Whether evals is only a duty inside "AI Engineer": five postings list it as one duty among many, and an "8% of postings" statistic is E.
2. Whether safety postings require a PhD: none states one in visible text.
3. India safety postings: none found.
4. Large AI-security counts, to test K17: the SANS tracker contradicts the KB's band.
5. Whether independent organisations pay far below labs: METR and Transluce bands overlap lab bands.

**Exclusions.** Evals 5, security 7, safety 5. Examples: Salesforce Sr Director AI Engineering, Backbase Principal AI Engineer, NVIDIA offensive security with LLM agents, Microsoft red-team tooling that automates red-team work, Handshake contractor "AI Red Teamer", a London contract "adversarial tester" role, Apollo Senior Security Engineer, Goodfire forward-deployed roles.

## Recommendation

**Decision rules for job seekers.**
1. **Software or backend engineer with 3+ years.** Target applied-eval roles at Big Tech Copilot or agent teams, scale-ups and eval vendors, and India product firms or services. Publish one harness that shows judge-versus-human agreement statistics, a CI regression gate and an error taxonomy, and name tools as postings do (Promptfoo, Braintrust, LangSmith, DeepEval, Inspect, LM Eval Harness). *Failure mode:* with under 3 years, evals is usually a duty inside an AI Engineer job; 0 of 22 postings were entry-level.
2. **5+ years of AppSec, pentest or red-team work.** Target AI red-team and AI security roles. Build a red-team suite against a working agent with attack-success rates before and after mitigation, mapped to OWASP LLM Top 10 and MITRE ATLAS, plus an MCP security audit. *Failure mode:* without an offensive background most red-team postings will not convert, and AI-for-security postings can mislead keyword searches.
3. **Backend or DevSecOps seekers.** Defensive guardrail and prompt-security platform engineering is a route into AI security that does not require offensive experience. *Failure mode:* the sample for this variant is thin (one London requisition advertised by two recruiters, plus Menlo and o9).
4. **Safety research.** Go fellowship-first and produce one public empirical result, not an opinion piece. Apply to Anthropic Fellows (rolling), UK AISI, Apollo, Goodfire, METR, FAR.AI, OpenAI and Google DeepMind, and keep a parallel track in evals or AI security. *Failure mode:* volumes are small (about 600 technical FTEs), fellowship selection is uncertain, and conversion claims are COI.
5. **India-based seekers.** Concentrate on Bangalore, Hyderabad and Pune evals and AI security roles. The Anthropic Fellows route needs existing US, UK or Canada work authorisation and presence. FAR.AI states it hires outside the US and Singapore through an employer of record or as a contractor, so check the terms. *Failure mode:* contractor arrangements may not carry benefits or permanence, METR's H-1B sponsorship is aggregator-reported only, and India pay is mostly unposted.
6. **Third-party evaluators.** Treat METR, Transluce, Apollo and UK AISI as an employer type for evals work. They are research-engineer style and selective. *Failure mode:* posted bands are wide and sometimes conflict across mirrors.
7. **Certificates.** Keep all AI security certificates Optional. AAISM is reachable only after CISM or CISSP, at USD 509–649 including the application fee (C). SecAI+ suits services or consultancy channels. *Failure mode:* spend is wasted where employers do not ask; 1 of 18 base security postings names any certificate.

**Suggested KB changes (direction only; the rubric anchors were not given).**
- Evals: keep demand, widen pay upward (bimodal), promote judge design and golden datasets to Must for applied variants, and demote pytest, statistics and LLM APIs from Must.
- AI security: raise demand relative to band 3, keep pay mid-range with a wide upper tail, promote red teaming and prompt injection, add MITRE ATLAS, adversarial ML and guardrail engineering, and treat GRC as Optional (a separate role in SANS).
- Safety: keep demand low and pay wide. Demote PyTorch from Must, add empirical research experience, correct K39 (PhD not stated) and add the work-authorisation gate to the India notes.

**What would change these rules.** Platform title counts that contradict SANS; entry-level eval postings becoming common; interview reports showing a distinct eval loop; a larger safety-posting sample showing PhD requirements.

## Open Questions & Risks

**Strongest objections an expert would raise.**
1. *Truncated excerpts bias skill shares.* True; shares are lower bounds. The A-read subset (3 evals, 1 security, 2 safety postings) has full text. Tier changes are direction-only.
2. *Search-driven sampling over-represents large English-language employers and stale mirrors.* True. Anthropic roles are reported open a median of 62 days (C), and the Backbase requisition is more than 11 months old.
3. *SANS counts may include classic security engineers.* I did not read the SANS method. My exclusion rate (7 of 32 security postings read) supports title noise.
4. *Posted bands are not pay.* Levels.fyi totals for Anthropic exceed posted bases by a wide margin; use bands as bases only, and treat aggregator-verified bands (OpenAI) as secondary.
5. *23 safety postings across 8 organisations cannot measure safety demand.* Agreed; volume rests on C sources.
6. *Evals may be absorbed into AI Engineer.* Five excluded postings and the missing title time series keep K7 unverifiable.

**Further risks.**
- Anthropic COI on S1, S2, S7–S13 and S47.
- The two-day gap between the brief's anchor date and the retrieval date.
- Fellowship outcome rates are employer claims.
- The practitioner interview guides have unclear authorship, and the Anthropic loop blog is unattributed (D).
- Recruiter ads can repeat one requisition and inflate counts.
- METR's research band conflicts across two mirrors.
