## Research Question

**Decision question.** What should the knowledge base say about three governance-family roles (AI governance programme lead / manager, AI risk, audit and compliance specialist, responsible AI / AI governance engineer) on demand, pay, skills and proof, credentials, interviews, careers and geography, and which of K1–K42 still stand?

**Sub-questions.**
1. Posting volume, mix and trend.
2. Posted pay and survey pay, with a second source for each pay claim.
3. Skill tiers from postings.
4. Credentials.
5. Interview loops.
6. Entry routes, progression and exits.
7. Durability against rule changes (EU, US federal and state, banking guidance, India).
8. India and Europe.

**Scope as applied.** I used postings dated 2026-08-13 or later, or live with no date shown (flagged). Older postings are kept as flagged extras. Other evidence runs from 2025-01-01, except the LL144 study (2024-06-03) and the AIGP launch (2024-04), both flagged as older.

**Method.** About 60 searches and fetches on 2026-11-13.
- Three specialist-board listing pages and two board detail pages read in full (Parsons, Visa).
- Two Axial Search report pages.
- Title, employer and region searches covering the US, UK, Singapore, India, Canada, UAE, Germany, Switzerland and Ireland.
- Regulatory and credential checks (EU Omnibus including the EUR-Lex record, SR 26-2, LL144, DPDP, Colorado, ISACA, IAPP, ISO/IEC 42001 certification bodies).
- Pay from posted bands, Axial, IAPP, Indian press and a Levels.fyi search.
- Interview-guidance searches.
- Pages that could not be read: the Parsons Workday page and a Novartis PDF were blocked or redirected, one Cognizant locale returned a client error, and one law-firm page sat behind a bot wall. I did not try to get around any of them.
- JobsPipe and Fast AI Jobs were not queried. This is a gap against the brief.

**Sampling and bias.**
- Search engines and one keyword-driven board over-represent large employers and English-language US and India postings.
- Alias shares cannot be inferred from a keyword-selected sample.
- Board counts include duplicates and non-role items (KF14).
- Skill shares use only postings whose requirement text was visible. Partial snippets give lower bounds.
- n = 11, 8 and 8 for roles 1, 2 and 3, so shares carry about ±30 percentage points.
- Tier rule (my heuristic, not the knowledge base's): Must at 70% or more of read postings, Important 30–69%, Should 10–29%, Optional below 10%. Role-defining task wording can override it, and I say so where it does.

**Exclusions** (classified by what the posting asks for): at least 16 for role 1, at least 12 for role 2 and 12 for role 3. Reasons are in each sample summary.

**Falsification searches.**
1. Evidence that certificates are required: not found.
2. Evidence that EU dates stayed: not found; the delay is law.
3. Evidence of mass demand for responsible-AI engineers: not found.
4. Evidence that LL144 drives audit jobs: weak.
5. Evidence that US state-law demand is stable: not found; Colorado's act was repealed.
6. Evidence that Levels.fyi holds data for these titles: not found.

### D0. Reading depth, tier rule and caveats

- A posting counts as **read** when its requirement text was visible to me, either on an employer page I opened (two postings) or in a snippet or board summary. - Platform signals (Axial, n = 1,997) are used only as a cross-check and carry the vendor's conflict of interest.
- Tiers are suggestions with about ±30 percentage points of uncertainty at n of 8 to 11.
