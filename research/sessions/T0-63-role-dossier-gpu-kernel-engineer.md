## Research Question

**Decision question.** What must the role page for `gpu-kernel-engineer` say so that a job seeker learns what the job is, what it pays, what it demands, how people are interviewed, how careers progress and where the jobs are, and which of the knowledge base's current scores and skill tiers should change?

**Sub-questions.** The brief's 15 coverage items, plus two probes: which kernel languages and tools postings name (CUDA, Triton, CuTe and other tile languages, ROCm) and how the compiler overlap looks; and where the line falls between this role and inference or AI-infrastructure engineering, and which employers hire kernel work (labs, chip vendors, inference companies).

**Reading chosen for ambiguity.**
- A posting is *core* when kernel authoring or optimisation, or ML-compiler and kernel-DSL work, is the title or the first-listed duty.
- A posting is *adjacent* when kernels are one duty among several, or are only preferred.
- Everything else is *excluded* (the reasons and counts are in D1).
- A core posting is *W1* when it is dated on or after 2026-08-13, or when I opened it live on 2026-11-13. It is *W2* when it is older or undated. W2 records are kept for requirement wording and are not used for recency claims.
- The role ID covers kernel authors and ML-compiler engineers, as the knowledge base's K1 and K2 do.

**Method.**
- I ran 38 web searches (4 in extended mode) and 11 page-fetch attempts. Two fetches were refused by bot detection (OpenAI, the xAI Greenhouse board) and one was refused because the URL had not appeared in a search result.
- I screened 90 postings: 41 core, 3 internships, 15 adjacent and 31 excluded.
- Employers' own boards were the first target (Greenhouse, Ashby, Lever, Workday, careers pages). Most could not be fetched, so most records are snippets or mirrors (Getro-powered boards, Built In, VC portfolio boards, aggregators), graded B.
- I opened five primary pages: Anthropic's Greenhouse board API, Sarvam's careers page, NVIDIA's careers site (meta description only), Anthropic's research post on its take-home, and the Cursor and NVIDIA kernel-agent post. I also opened three secondary pages: the Inference Jobs tag page, one AnitaB.org mirror of an NVIDIA posting, and a recruiter guide.
- Other evidence: Levels.fyi pages (snippets), labour condition application aggregates, law-firm summaries of H-1B litigation, NVIDIA developer pages on CUDA Tile and a GPU MODE hackathon, and interview-guide sites.

**Sampling and its biases.**
- Search engines surface large employers and English-language pages.
- My queries named kernels, CUDA and Triton, which inflates those skills' shares.
- NVIDIA is over-represented because its postings are heavily mirrored.
- Mirrors drift on dates. NVIDIA's "Senior Inference Engineer, GPU Kernel Optimization" was first listed 2026-07-28 and refreshed 2026-10-31 [S14, S15]. Sciforium shows 2026-06-25 on one board and 2026-08-21 on another [S19].
- Skill shares are lower bounds because I often saw only part of a posting.
- Aggregator "posted N days ago" labels have unknown crawl dates, so I did not turn them into dates.

**Disconfirming searches.**
1. Entry-level and new-graduate kernel postings: only NVIDIA internships and new-graduate titles surfaced.
2. Evidence that Triton or tile languages displaced CUDA in requirements: CUDA is still named in 63.4% of core postings.
3. India kernel roles, to test the knowledge base's "no India evidence" (K5): I found 8.
4. Evidence that kernel agents already displaced hiring: the agent results are real [S6], but postings added agentic-optimisation duties instead [S14].
5. Evidence that a PhD is required: none in the 7 postings whose degree text I could see.

## Recommendation

1. **Pick the entry path by your evidence.** If you can show a merged kernel pull request, a competition or leaderboard entry, or a profiler-backed speed-up on named hardware, apply directly to kernel-titled roles. Otherwise start in adjacent performance or inference roles, or in internships and new-graduate programmes (KF1, KF7). *Failure mode:* an adjacent role may never touch kernels, so ask what share of time goes to kernel work before accepting.
2. **Learn in this order.** CUDA with C++ and Triton first (73.2% of postings name one). Then profiling with Nsight Compute and Nsight Systems (46.3% mention profiling of any kind). Then one specialism matched to your target employers: CUTLASS or CuTe, ROCm and HIP, MLIR and LLVM, or TPU and Pallas. Learn to read PTX or SASS (22.0%). *Failure mode:* learning a tile language your targets do not use, so check five target postings first.
3. **Build three public artefacts.** A GEMM or attention kernel benchmarked against a vendor library on a named GPU, with a roofline explanation. A correctness suite with stated tolerances across dtypes. At least one merged pull request in vLLM, SGLang, FlashInfer, Triton or CUTLASS. GPU MODE leaderboard or hackathon entries are an optional extra. *Failure mode:* no access to current hardware, so rent cloud GPUs and record the model you used.
4. **Prepare for a take-home and a profiler walkthrough, not generic puzzles.** Practise on Anthropic's open take-home and defend every number [S4]. Ask each employer in writing whether AI tools are allowed at each stage, because policies differ. *Failure mode:* AI-resistant take-homes are redesigned as models improve, so do not over-fit to one format.
5. **India.** Target Sarvam (senior), AMD (Hyderabad and Bengaluru, from 2 years), Intel Bengaluru, Google Bengaluru (compilers), recruiter-listed senior roles and Indian start-ups, and expect fewer postings (19.5% of the sample) and few posted bands. Use the Levels.fyi proxy and recruiter bands when negotiating, and check dates on the employer's own board. Early-career readers should use NVIDIA-style internships and new-graduate programmes or a strong open-source record. *Failure mode:* undated mirror postings may be closed, and averages from crowd-sourced sites understate total compensation at large vendors.
6. **Set pay expectations by employer type.** Posted US base is a median band of $190K to $287.5K. Frontier labs go higher, and private-lab equity is not in base. Levels.fyi's NVIDIA US median total compensation is $315K. *Failure mode:* posted ranges span levels (Anthropic's $280K to $850K), so read them as bands, not offers.
7. **Hedge for automation.** Add verification, numerics and compiler or DSL skills to hand-written kernel work, and follow agent benchmarks such as Cursor and NVIDIA's [S6]. *Failure mode:* if tile languages and agents commoditise routine kernels faster than new silicon creates hard ones, plan a move into compiler, runtime or verification roles.
8. **If you need sponsorship, verify it per posting.** Read each posting's visa statement and ask in writing. For the US, the status of the $100,000 H-1B payment is contested and changes quickly (V0), so check USCIS and the White House proclamation page before relying on any summary, including this one. *Failure mode:* a posting may offer sponsorship for some locations or levels only.

**What would change these rules.** A systematic scrape of employer applicant-tracking boards with monthly snapshots that moves the demand score; posted pay that diverges from the median band above; employers dropping CUDA from requirements; a court or agency decision on the H-1B payment.

### D2. Definition, variants and aliases

**Definition at the research date.** The job is writing and tuning GPU or accelerator kernels, or building the compilers and kernel languages that generate them, for training and inference. Anthropic's posting frames it as maximising GPU utilisation through custom kernels, fusion, communication and profiling [S1]. Sarvam calls its version a hard, narrow, high-leverage role that owns the kernel layer [S3].

**Three variants.**

1. Kernel authoring and optimisation (26 of 41): CUDA, Triton, CUTLASS or CuTe, HIP, Pallas.
2. Compiler or DSL engineering (10 of 41): XLA, MLIR, Triton and CUDA Tile compilers, Dynamo, TPU compilers.
3. Custom-silicon enablement (5 of 41): kernel libraries and runtimes for Trainium NKI, Cerebras, Graphcore and Intel accelerators.

**Boundaries with adjacent roles.**
- Inference and performance engineers reason about kernels and integrate them (Anthropic, Sarvam) [S2, S3].
- Runtime and cluster engineers rarely need kernels (a Perplexity GPU-cluster posting calls CUDA or Triton work "not required, but notable") [S19].
- OS-kernel and driver roles are not this role.
- Hourly kernel-evaluation gigs are a different market [S27].
- Qualcomm's "GPU Kernel Development Engineer" postings mix mobile GPU driver work with ML kernels; I classed them adjacent [S17].

## Open Questions & Risks

**Strongest objections an expert would raise.**

1. **"A 41-posting convenience sample cannot measure demand."** Agreed. The search method and keywords bias the sample towards NVIDIA, AI-native employers, the US and English. The sample does span 30 employers and seven employer types, so use the shares as role-conditional with ±15 pp.
2. **"Mirrors are not employer boards and their dates drift."** Evidence for: NVIDIA's inference posting (2026-07-28 versus 2026-10-31) and Sciforium (2026-06-25 versus 2026-08-21). Against: A-grade records exist for Anthropic, Sarvam and NVIDIA's careers page, and every record carries a window flag. Only the 16 W1 records support recency claims.
3. **"Skill shares from partial text are lower bounds, and search terms inflate CUDA and Triton."** Agreed. Stratification shows the effect: CUDA is named in 85% of kernel-led postings but 0% of custom-silicon postings.
4. **"Posted ranges are not pay, and Levels.fyi covers all software engineers."** Agreed. NVIDIA's posted bands and Levels.fyi base agree, but equity at private labs is outside both, and India sources conflict by method.
5. **"Kernel agents will compress the role."** Evidence for: Cursor and NVIDIA's results and Anthropic's take-home redesigns. Against: postings that add agentic duties, new silicon, a speed-up measured against agent-optimised baselines rather than expert libraries, and Anthropic's statement that humans lead with unlimited time. Unresolved.
6. **"The author is Anthropic's model."** Anthropic evidence (S1, S2, S4) is marked COI. The other 40 core postings and the pay, skills and geography findings do not depend on Anthropic.
7. **"India evidence is thin."** Agreed: 8 postings, 1 opened, 2 of them W1, and no employer-posted bands.

**Open questions.**
- Are kernel-titled postings rising? Monthly snapshots of employer boards are needed.
- Will tile languages cut CUDA C++ requirements by 2027?
- What do Indian employers pay for kernel roles?
- Do GPU MODE ranks or hackathon placements predict interview outcomes?
- Will the US H-1B payment survive litigation, and how will agencies implement it?
