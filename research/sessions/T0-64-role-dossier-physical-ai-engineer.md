## Research Question

**Decision question.** What should the knowledge base record about the Physical AI / Robotics AI Engineer role for job seekers in the US, Europe and India? The record covers what the job is, demand and pay, skill tiers, credentials, entry routes, interview shape, geography and sponsorship. This session tests K1–K13.

**Reading chosen.** The brief's role is a robot-learning engineer: someone who trains, evaluates and deploys learned robot policies (VLA models, imitation and reinforcement learning, sim-to-real). Classical robotics software, controls, autonomous-driving and defence-autonomy roles are adjacent roles. Platform counts for "Physical AI" are mostly those adjacent roles, and I use them only as market context.

**Method.**
- 22 web searches and 22 page-fetch attempts: 18 pages read, 1 blocked by bot detection (DeepMind 7543224, not circumvented), and 3 refused by the fetch tool because the URL had not been returned by a search.
- I read employer boards directly where search returned them: Greenhouse (Figure, Skild, Agile Robots, Apptronik), Workable (Samsung SDS America, Origin, Weekday, Laplacian), SAP SuccessFactors (Wipro), the Siemens careers site, 1X's careers page and the Ashby public posting API (Physical Intelligence).
- Mirrors were used where the employer page was not returned: NVIDIA (Workday not opened), Amazon, Boston Dynamics, Qualcomm, Bosch, Genesis, UnitX and others. Their grade is B or C.
- The sample has 48 `jd` records. 35 came from a first pass and 13 from a second pass that targeted gaps (staff-plus levels, GCC-type employers, humanoid OEMs, board-level counts). 12 of the 48 have complete requirement text (a "full-text set"). The rest are snippets, so their skill shares are lower bounds.
- Sampling bias: search engines favour large, English-language, US employers, and my queries targeted VLA, robot-learning, embodied-AI and "Physical AI" vocabulary. The sample therefore over-represents the model-centric variant and under-represents ROS, C++ integration and controls roles.
- Classification was by what each posting asks for. About 80 further listings were seen and not sampled (approximate tally, not a running count): hardware, manufacturing, test, operator, programme-management and infrastructure roles; scientist-only titles; about 10 other Figure "Helix" titles; and one stale page. Examples read in full and excluded as not this role: Figure "Helix AI Engineer, Backend" and Skild "Robotics Engineer, Deployment & Testing".
- Quotation limit: requirement text in `jd` records is paraphrased, not verbatim, because of a one-short-quote-per-source limit. Tool, framework and platform names are exact. Maintainers should re-open the URLs for exact wording.
- Freshness caveat: the fetch tool can return cached copies of unknown age. In this session it served Figure pages for "Robot Learning" and "Modeling" as open, while a Figure board listing fetched later the same day does not list them. "Read on 2026-11-13" therefore does not prove "live on 2026-11-13". Only postings that carry a posting date inside the window count as verified-fresh.
- Falsification searches: humanoid and physical-AI shutdowns and funding slowdown; a C++/ROS share check against platform data; a check on a viral Bangalore demand figure; a posting-date and liveness check on DeepMind and Figure pages; a check of the Figure "Helix" title family against its board.

## Recommendation

**Decision rules for job seekers (as of 2026-11-13)**

1. **Search by activity, not title.** Use "robot learning", "robotic learning", "VLA", "embodied AI", "manipulation policy", "whole body control", "research engineer, robotics" and "physical AI". Do not treat "Helix AI Engineer" as a signal of robot-learning work. Failure mode: exact-title search misses most roles, and prefix searches return backend and infrastructure jobs.
2. **Read employer boards and check dates.** Treat undated or cached pages as unconfirmed, because only 4 of 48 sampled postings carried an in-window date. Use job alerts on the boards of the main employers. Failure mode: applying to closed roles.
3. **Build one real-robot proof.** Train or fine-tune a manipulation or locomotion policy on physical hardware. Report trials, success rate and a failure taxonomy, and label simulation-only results as simulation. Failure mode: robot access costs money. The fallback is a simulation project with a stated real-to-sim evaluation, labelled as such.
4. **Prioritise Python, PyTorch, robot learning, imitation learning and RL, one simulator (Isaac Sim, MuJoCo or ManiSkill) and real-robot experience.** Treat C++ and ROS 2 as variant-dependent: needed for runtime, integration, deployment and services roles, optional for pure model roles. Failure mode: dropping C++ or ROS while targeting humanoid-OEM or integration roles fails screens there.
5. **US applicants.** Compare total compensation, not base, because bands are employer-wide. Check sponsorship and export-control screens before applying. Failure mode: assuming a band midpoint is the offer, or applying to a US-person-only role.
6. **Europe.** Target Munich, Zürich and London, and expect relocation because remote roles are rare. Failure mode: relying on remote work.
7. **India.** Expect few roles: IT-services labs (Wipro), a captive-style R&D unit (Siemens Energy India), startups and an OEM (Addverb). Industrial variants add MLOps and digital-twin skills. Failure mode: planning around posted pay or remote US roles, which this sample does not support.
8. **Early career.** Only 2 of 48 postings are explicitly junior. Apply to research-engineer and junior titles at startups and lead with open-source and project evidence. Failure mode: applying only to senior-titled roles.
9. **Credentials.** Do not spend on certifications (0/48 name one). An MS or PhD helps at labs and large firms.
10. **Answer screening questions with explicit years and platforms.** State years of RL, the robot platforms and simulators you used, and your real-robot deployments. Failure mode: vague wording fails knock-out questions and keyword matching.

**What would change these rules.** A sustained fall in tracked embodied-AI roles, several more startup shutdowns, or a larger full-text sample showing C++ or ROS above 50%.

## Detailed Findings

### 1. Definition and variants (K1, K2, K8)

- Core work in the sample: train, evaluate and deploy learned policies or robot foundation models on physical platforms, and close the sim-to-real gap.
- Variants:
  - Model-centric robot learning and VLA work (Figure, Skild, Physical Intelligence, Genesis, NVIDIA GEAR, 1X).
  - Reinforcement learning for locomotion and whole-body control (Figure, Apptronik), where dynamics and control become Must.
  - Deployment and applied robotics (Skild, DeepMind applied robotics, Agile Robots, Boston Dynamics Stretch).
  - Industrial and enterprise physical AI with digital twins and MLOps (Siemens Energy India, Samsung SDS America, Wipro).
  - Simulation, synthetic-data and evaluation tooling (UnitX, NVIDIA simulation, Boston Dynamics behaviour-cloning tools).
  - World-model and generative physical AI (Hedra).
- Boundaries: controls, runtime, firmware, fleet-operations and perception-only roles at the same employers are adjacent roles. The Physical Intelligence board lists controls, runtime software, ML infrastructure, build and fleet roles separately [S13, S84].
- Title churn: Figure job 4660876006 was titled "Tooling" in an earlier search snapshot and "Android" in the board listing I read, under the same job ID [S3, S66].

## Open Questions & Risks

- Role-proper volume cannot be isolated from the wider physical-AI market.
- 44 of 48 sample records carry a freshness flag; skill shares are lower bounds, and the 12-posting full-text set is small.
- The sample over-represents VLA and robot-learning variants because of query design.
- Wide, employer-wide pay bands and sparse total-compensation data may mislead score maintainers.
- Funding-cycle risk: a downturn could cut startup hiring quickly.
- Several sources are vendors or sponsored content (a recruiting vendor, a sponsored article).
- Cached fetch results mean some "read" postings may already be closed.

- "Helix AI Engineer" is a department prefix at Figure and the same $200k–$400k band appears on a backend payments role. Any demand or pay figure built from the Helix title family overstates robot-learning demand.
