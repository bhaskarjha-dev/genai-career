## Research Question

**Decision question.** Which routes into GenAI roles work for DevOps and SRE engineers, product managers, IT-services engineers, and students and new graduates? Which target roles fit each group, which skill and proof gaps must close, how long transitions take, how they fail, and what the entry-level evidence says for the US, Europe and India. Question type: evaluative and causal (D-008).

**Readings chosen.** "Europe" covers the UK (ISE, Adzuna, Prospects, High Fliers) and, for general employment only, the EU as a whole (Eurostat). "IT-services engineers" covers TCS, Infosys, Wipro, HCLTech, Cognizant and Accenture where data existed. "Students and new graduates" covers the US, UK and India entry markets, the master's-abroad routes to the US, UK and Canada, and early-career programmes at AI companies. Role names follow the brief's IDs.

**Method.** More than fifty tool calls across the session. Seven sources were opened in full. Posting samples: 11 inference, SRE and MLOps postings; 6 AI-PM and PM postings; 2 FDE postings plus one vendor count. These are convenience samples and do not represent their markets. The final round of searches covered internal-mobility evidence, Accenture's reporting, BLS duration of unemployment, OPT outcomes, research-assistantship and REU outcomes, and competition outcomes.

**Falsification searches.** (a) the authors' own caveats and their February 2026 update (K3); (b) ISE's response to Adzuna and its stabilisation finding (UK); (c) NACE's two AI-skill measures (US); (d) ACS sampling intervals for by-major unemployment (US); (e) training counts against disclosed usage (Wipro; Accenture's trained staff against its AI-professional headcount); (f) causal degree returns against raw premia; (g) the status of the US post-study and H-1B proposals; (h) employer-side time-to-fill against candidate-side transition claims (K1); (i) employer case studies and platform statistics against independent outcome data (competitions).

**Known gaps.** No primary US or UK immigration text was opened. No independent transition data exist for the four groups. No AI-specific graduate hiring series was found for the EU. No independent outcome data were found for competitions, fellowships or bootcamps. Each gap appears under Absences.

## Detailed Findings

### 1. DevOps and SRE engineers

**Target-role fit.** Grades refer to the overlap evidence.

| Role | Overlap | Gaps | Evidence | Grade |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| inference-engineer | High: serving, Kubernetes, SLOs, autoscaling | Serving frameworks (vLLM, SGLang, TensorRT-LLM); GPU scheduling; quantisation; benchmarking | 11 extracts: serving 11/11, Kubernetes 6/11 [S33] | B (M) |
| ai-infrastructure-engineer | High | GPU fleet operations; networking; cost per GPU-hour | Same sample; title variants in the posting set [S33] | B (M) |
| ml-platform-engineer | High | Kubeflow, MLflow, model registry, feature stores | One posting names Kubeflow, MLflow and KubeAI [S33] | B |
| forward-deployed-engineer (infrastructure family) | Medium | Customer-facing record; 5+ years | OpenAI FDE gate [S36]; FDE family counts [S36] | B / C |
| ai-enablement-engineer | Medium–high | Internal tooling; adoption metrics | No posting evidence in session | W |
| ai-evaluation-engineer | Low–medium | Evaluation design; labelled data | Vendor PM sample only [S35] | C |
| gpu-kernel-engineer | Low | CUDA or Triton kernel work | Not in sample | not tested |

**Ranking and reasons.** (1) inference-engineer, because the serving stack overlaps most; (2) ai-infrastructure-engineer; (3) ml-platform-engineer; (4) the FDE infrastructure family, only with a customer-facing record; (5) ai-enablement-engineer. Model research is not the gap: training is absent from all 11 extracts.

**Skill and proof deltas.** Skills: model-serving frameworks, GPU scheduling, quantisation, benchmarking, cost per token. Proof: one served model with measured latency, throughput and cost, plus a runbook and SLO dashboards.

**Route (gate format).** Evidence tags: B = established source; C = vendor, practitioner or survey; M = my tabulation; P = practice-curated.

| Gate | Timing | What to produce | Evidence tag |
|---|---|---|---|
| Internal ask (K1) | Weeks 1–8 | Request an inference or GPU-platform assignment and record the answer | P; employer-side speed only [S60]; bridges exist [S26] (B) |
| G1 measured v0 | By month 3 | One open-weight model served with vLLM or Triton on GPU nodes; latency, throughput and cost per 1,000 tokens recorded | B for the skill set [S33]; P for the metrics |
| G2 operable v1 | By month 6 | Kubernetes autoscaling on GPU node pools; SLO dashboard; alerts and a runbook | B: Kubernetes 6/11, observability 4/11 [S33]; P for the runbook |
| G3 second system and external proof | By month 9 | A second serving stack (quantised model or router) and one public write-up or upstream fix | P; outcome evidence absent (A4) |
| G4 offer or funnel diagnosis | By month 12 | An offer, or a weekly funnel review by role family | P; funnels differ [S33], [S34], [S36] |

**Transition evidence.** No candidate-side transition data were found, and the four K1 accounts were not located. Employer-side vendor data show internal candidates filling roles faster (about 20 days against 49 for external) [S60]. A vendor survey reports that 27% of HR leaders made avoidable external hires because of skills-data gaps [S61]. These describe employers, not individual transitions.

**Senior option.** Claude Corps does not fit this group, since it requires under two years' experience. The Anthropic Residency targets experienced technical professionals and suits senior SREs with checkable public work [S53]. It is a route for that profile, not a general transition route.

**Interview differences.** No employer-published guide was found (A10). Vendor pages on FDE roles were read only as summaries, so no interview format is recorded here.

### 3. IT-services engineers (India)

**Target-role fit.**

| Role | Overlap | Gaps | Evidence | Grade |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| applied-ai-engineer | High (delivery and integration) | GenAI application patterns; evaluation; deployment | Infosys AI-aware disclosure, a self-defined label [S26]; Accenture AI professionals [S62] | B |
| ai-enablement-engineer | High (internal tooling and adoption) | Adoption metrics; product sense | Bridge programmes [S26]; AI-tool use in promotion [S65] | B |
| ai-evaluation-engineer | Medium (QA-to-evaluation route) | Evaluation design; labelled data | No posting evidence in session | W |
| ai-solutions-architect | Medium at 5+ years | Architecture across client systems | GCC senior skew [S29] | C |
| forward-deployed-engineer | Medium at 5+ years | Customer-facing record | FDE gate [S36] | B |
| ml-engineer | Low–medium without an ML background | ML fundamentals; training | Not tested | W |

**Ranking and reasons.** (1) applied-AI engineer through client delivery or internal bridges; (2) AI-enablement engineer; (3) AI-evaluation engineer for QA and test leads; (4) solutions architect at 5+ years; (5) FDE at 5+ years with customer-facing work.

**Routes.** Three routes appear in the evidence.
- Internal GenAI practice or bridge programme. Infosys describes bridge programmes for switching career field inside the firm, without counts [S26] (B). Accenture reports nearly 110,000 AI and data professionals and says it exceeded its goal of doubling the FY2023 base of 40,000 to 80,000 by the end of FY2026 [S62] (B). Its CEO reports about 550,000 staff trained in generative-AI fundamentals, out of about 780,000 [S63], [S65] (C / B).
- GCC at four or more years. Mid-to-senior talent is above 77% of GCC hiring [S28], [S29] (C).
- Product companies. Not evidenced in this session (D).

**Skill and proof gaps.** The main gaps are GenAI engineering practice beyond training, evaluation, and deployment with measured usage. TCS's Prime track reviews AI or data projects in the technical round, which signals the proof standard (C) [S23].

**What holds people back.** Three documented pressures, each sourced to a secondary or company-confirmed report. Accenture's $865 million restructuring came with a plan to phase out roles with nonviable skills [S64] (B). Accenture has told senior staff that regular use of internal AI tools will matter for leadership promotion [S65] (B). TCS's chief executive has said job reductions reflected a skills mismatch and limited scope for redeployment, as reported by MediaNama [S47] (C). The same article says some senior employees have struggled to move into tech-heavy roles; that is the article's own wording, not a company statement [S47].

**Evidence of successful moves.** None measured (A9). Accenture and Infosys report training and headcount counts, but no move rates.

**Route (gate format).**

| Gate | Timing | What to produce | Evidence tag |
|---|---|---|---|
| G1 measured v0 | By month 3 | One internal GenAI use case with an eval harness and a baseline | B for bridge existence [S26]; P for metrics |
| G2 operable v1 | By month 6 | A deployed internal tool with usage measured against licences or seats | C [S27]; P |
| G3 second system and proof | By month 9 | A reproducible repository and deployed demo, reviewed to the Prime-track standard | C [S23]; P |
| G4 offer or move | By month 12 | Internal move to a GenAI practice, or a GCC offer at 4+ years | C [S28], [S29]; K1 unverified |

**5.3 Degrees and master's returns.** Altonji and Zhu estimate causal returns for 121 graduate degrees from Texas administrative data [S3]. The average effect on log earnings is 0.159, with a standard deviation of 0.176 across degrees, and internal rates of return vary less across fields. The PEER report, as summarised by Fortune, gives cost-adjusted returns of +6% for computer science, +2% for computer engineering, +4% for electrical and mechanical engineering, +13% for an MBA, +41% for law and +173% for medicine [S4]. ## Open Questions & Risks

The open questions are these. First, whether an internal move beats an external one: candidate-side data are missing, and only employer-side speed exists. Second, whether inference-role gates loosen: the posting sample is small. Third, which US post-study rules will be in force in 2027: the OPT and H-1B positions are at V0. Fourth, whether UK advertised declines map to hires; ISE's 2026 finding suggests they may not. Fifth, whether TCS's FY26 fresher offers were 25,000 or 44,000. Sixth, whether India's AI-entry share exceeds the US's on any basis other than job titles. Seventh, whether Claude Corps leads to AI roles: it has no outcome data, and its first cohort is reported for October 2026.

## Discovered Concerns

- **TCS fresher counts conflict.** The company call reports over 25,000 FY26 offers [S22]; secondary outlets cite 44,000 onboarded in FY26 [S23]. Check the TCS annual report.
- **Axial's "66% IC" mislabel.** The subtotal is mid plus senior, and the window cannot be confirmed from a report dated 2026-05-22 [S6].
- **JobsPipe's AI definition** includes "data scientist" and publishes no counts [S5].
- **US post-study figures conflict.** OPT counts range from about 240,000 (May 2025, vendor) through 300,000 (Bloomberg Law) to 418,000 (2024, Bloomberg Government) [S37], [S75]. One tracker calls the USD 100,000 H-1B fee blocked by court order, and another calls it vacated [S37].
- **NACE measures conflict.** Its AI-skill measures differ [S7], [S8]. Its conversion history also conflicts: the 2026 report implies about 56% for the 2023–24 cohort, while a secondary summary of its 2025 report gives about 51% [S9], [S10]. Treat the year-on-year rise with care.
- **Advertised versus actual graduate hiring.** Adzuna's −45.6% and ISE's "stabilised" measure different things [S14]–[S16].
- **Conflicted sources.** JobsPipe (own platform); Axial (recruiter); the datainterview course (vendor); Valletta and Bloomberry (vendors); Cognizant–Pearson (IT-services firm); ICRIER (OpenAI-supported); PwC (AI services); Deloitte (campus consultancy); Nasscom–Zinnov (industry body); TeamLease Digital and Xpheno (staffing firms); CodePath (programme provider); Fuel50 and Betterworks (vendors); Kaggle statistics (platform); Alma (vendor); the Meta case study (vendor-hosted).
- **Non-comparable AI-training labels.** Infosys's "AI-aware", TCS's "AI-skilled", Wipro's "AI Master" and Accenture's "trained in generative-AI fundamentals" are different constructs [S23], [S26], [S27], [S63].
- **Canaries vintages.** The series reads 13% (August 2025), 16% (late 2025 abstract) and 19% (June 2026). Cite the vintage with any figure [S1].
- **Undated sources.** Several sources carry no date in the extract (Georgetown CEW, EIG on ACS, the Accenture–Reuters report, the ASEE REU paper, IIIT Delhi, the Kaggle case study). They are used only for the period stated and not for time-sensitive claims.
- **Claude Corps details.** Salary and cohort timing come from secondary outlets. Verify them on the programme page before acting.
- **IIT Guwahati CSE median withheld.** The department table gives a CSE median of 51 LPA, but its column alignment comes from PDF text extraction. It stays out of the data until someone checks the source page [S55].
- **Unsourced claims excluded.** The claims that AI PM postings "tripled" and that AI skills appear in 35% of entry postings were not traced to a primary source, so they are not used.
- **Date discrepancy.** The brief dates the session 2026-11-11; the session date is 2026-11-13.
