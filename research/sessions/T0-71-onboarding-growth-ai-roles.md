## Research Question

**Decision question.** What should a new hire in a GenAI role do in the first 90 days and the first two years to pass probation, deliver visible results and progress? What does the evidence say about promotion, retention, performance expectations and burnout?

**Reading chosen.** "GenAI role" means engineers or scientists who build or operate LLM-based systems (ML engineer, applied scientist, AI engineer). Evidence on software engineers using AI tools is used for expectations and productivity, with transfer caveats. "Probation" means formal probation plus the informal first-90-day judgement.

**Sub-questions.** These are the brief's eight items: published expectations, probation and attrition, AI-tool productivity, first 90 days, promotion, tracks, sustainable pace, and decision rules.

**Scope applied.** The window is 2024-01-01 to 2026-11-11, plus peer-reviewed organisational and occupational-health research of any date. Pay benchmarks and job search are excluded. Promotion raises are included only to test K4.

**Method.**
- I ran 48 web searches in standard mode and opened 15 pages. One page was blocked by a bot wall (SAGE, for S2). Three fetches were refused because the URL had not appeared in earlier results, so I re-found those pages by search.
- A first pass tested K1–K10. A second pass opened the WHO release, Anthropic's study and two employer reports, the NBER paper, a developer-burnout paper, and India appraisal and labour-law sources.
- DEEPEN: METR to its primary pages, Cui, Paradis and Peng to their papers (Peng via a citation), SignalFire to its primary report, Pave to its blog.
- WIDEN: WHO/ILO, Pencavel, Haas and a developer-burnout survey for occupational health; the UK government framework; Indian labour-law and appraisal sources.
- FALSIFY searches:
  1. Public probation rubrics for AI roles (none found).
  2. "10× engineer with AI" claims (no controlled study found).
  3. "Growth beats pay" (foundit's June survey points the other way; its November survey points the same way).
  4. The source of "8.6 years to senior" (not found).
  5. Early-turnover statistics (untraceable).
- Anthropic documentation, pricing, research and employer reports are used for mechanics, employer practice and one adverse finding. Each such row is COI.

## Recommendation

Most elements below are B, C or P because AI-role outcomes are not measured. Each rule carries a failure mode.

| # | Decision rule | Tag | Failure mode / when not to apply |
|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | By day 14, get written 30/60/90 criteria, one problem, one metric and a sponsor. If none are given, send your written understanding and ask for correction. A manager who will not state criteria is a finding. | B + D (day-14 trigger) | Reads as insecurity where little is written down. Bring a draft yourself. |
| R2 | Ship one small, reviewed production change in weeks 2–4 with a named mentor. Ask questions early. | B/C | Fast shipping into a poorly understood system raises incident risk. Keep changes low-risk. |
| R3 | By day 30, write a one-page problem statement with metric, non-goals and sponsor sign-off. Map stakeholders: sponsor, domain experts, data owners, platform and security. | B (RAND) / P | Sign-off without a real metric leaves the problem misunderstood. |
| R4 | In days 31–60, build a regression evaluation set from real failures (start at 30–100 cases), run it in CI, and validate any LLM judge on human labels. | P | Unrepresentative set, overfitting, biased judge, or a manager who does not value it. Vendor eval platforms change (OpenAI's guide notes its Evals platform is being deprecated). Check R1 first. |
| R5 | In days 61–90, measure one cost or latency cut on your own traffic, behind the evaluation gate. | P (A for price facts, COI) | Silent quality loss without R4. Price multipliers and tokenisation change. Savings may be immaterial at small scale. |
| R6 | Use AI tools and log where they help. Report measured before-and-after results, never multipliers, and count shipped and used outcomes rather than code volume. Ask what AI training and autonomy you get. Use comprehension-seeking prompts for unfamiliar libraries. | B | Under-use where AI use is reviewed (Meta, Google, Shopify, GitLab, Accenture). Over-reliance erodes debugging skill. |
| R7 | Keep sustained hours near 50 a week, review at 55, and keep a weekly rest day. Before accepting on-call, ask for rota size, pager volume and compensation or time off. | A/B (hours) / P (on-call) | In 70–80 hour start-up cultures a ceiling may cost perceived commitment. Choose the employer accordingly. |
| R8 | Ask for level criteria and timeline. Keep a quarterly evidence log of scope and impact. If criteria are unpublished or no path exists after two review cycles, treat that as a finding. | C | Promotions can be title events with small raises. |
| R9 | Try a bounded lead role before committing to people management. | C/B | Sales-to-engineering transfer is untested. Some employers cap the IC track below staff level. |
| R10 | In India, read the appointment letter (mandatory under the Labour Codes per the Ministry release) for probation length, extension clause, notice and confirmation criteria. Ask for written confirmation. | C (practice) / B (Labour Codes) | State rules and the Industrial Relations Code may change what applies. This is a pointer, not legal advice. |

**What would change these rules.**
- A published GenAI probation or performance rubric, or probation-outcome data for AI roles.
- Independent data on AI-role promotion speed or attrition.
- An outcome study of eval suites or cost cuts.
- AI-engineer-specific burnout or on-call measurement, or a replication of the developer-burnout findings.
- Enactment of pending Indian working-hours amendments.
- A review standard that counts AI-usage volume, which would change R6.

## Alternatives Considered

| Option | Verdict | Reason | Failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|
| Maximise output with AI agents from day one | Rejected as a default | No controlled evidence for large gains. Gains attenuate before release, burnout is linked to AI-driven demands, and review criteria are unpublished. [S19]–[S25][S72][S74] | Over-claiming, unreviewed code, skill loss. |
| Spend 90 days learning and ship nothing | Rejected | Ramp-up studies favour early small contributions with a mentor. [S56][S57] | Invisible progress at review. |
| Lead with a large architectural change | Rejected | RAND's root causes are problem misunderstanding and technology focus. [S3] | Months of work on the wrong metric. |
| Take the management track early | Not preferred | Management layers are thinning. Top performers can make weaker managers. [S13][S59] | Fewer manager roles, and mismatch. |
| Leave at 12–18 months if growth stalls | Conditional | A vendor survey ranks "no growth" first; three Indian surveys put growth level with or ahead of pay, and one puts pay slightly ahead. SignalFire reports faster promotions at start-ups. [S8][S9][S11][S75][S82][S83] | Mobility costs, and a new probation. |
| Work 70–80 hours to signal commitment | Rejected | Health risk and diminishing output. [S48][S49] | Burnout, errors, attrition. Only defensible if you choose that culture knowingly. |

- No ladder or handbook found defines probation criteria for a GenAI role. A vendor ML onboarding template exists (Zavvy, 2023-12-18) [S38]. It is not an employer rubric.

### 3. AI-tool productivity

| Study | Design | Sample | Finding | Grade |
|---|---|---|---|---|

| METR survey, 2026-05-11 [S20] | Self-report, convenience sample, about 2% response | 349 technical workers | Median 1.4–2× value, 3× speed. Seven respondents reported ≥10× on two measures. Of those, two were checked against public outputs and judged overstated. | B |
| Demirer, Musolff and Yang, NBER WP 35275, May 2026 [S72] | Matched event study with AI-usage telemetry; three tool generations | 100,000+ GitHub developers in the PDF version; a secondary post on the later abstract says 500,000+ | Commits up 30%, 180% and 240% (landing-page abstract) or 40%, 140% and 180% (PDF) for autocomplete, interactive agents and autonomous agents. App marketplaces show a sharp rise in new apps but no rise in total usage. Working paper, not peer-reviewed; the versions differ in magnitude, and both show attenuation. | B |
| DORA 2025 [S25] | Correlational survey | About 5,000 | 90% use AI. AI adoption is positively linked to throughput, with instability still higher. More than 80% self-report productivity gains. | B |
| Anthropic, 2026-01-29 [S24] | RCT, learning a new library (COI) | 52, mostly junior | Quiz 50% against 67% in the text (about 65% in the figure); d=0.74, p=0.01. | Anthropic internal data, 2026-10-16 [S70] | Descriptive and staggered-adoption design (COI) | 192 adopters, 74 non-adopters | Commits per engineer about 5× the rate of six months earlier. METR's population is expert maintainers on familiar code, and METR reports that developers decline to work without AI, which breaks task-level randomisation. The NBER paper is the strongest evidence that volume metrics overstate shipped output.

### 6. Tracks

- SignalFire's 2026 report: about 12 engineers per manager at Tech Majors (from 10), about 15 at start-ups, thinner senior management in most functions, and "Super ICs" at manager-scale scope [S13] (C).
- Anthropic's employer report: about 17 engineers per product manager, managers committing code about twice as often, and individual contributors who orchestrate agents needing manager-like knowledge. Junior technical hires fell from 27% to 9% of technical hires and junior headcount from 21% to 14% of technical departments [S70] (B; COI; employer self-report).
- A Harvard résumé-and-postings study of 62 million workers finds junior employment fell at AI-adopting firms relative to non-adopters from 2023, mainly through slower hiring, while senior employment held or rose [S73] (B; firms flagged by "AI integrator" postings).
- Benson, Li and Shue (QJE 2019, sales workers): firms weight current performance heavily in promotion, and the managerial quality of marginally promoted workers falls as pre-promotion sales rise [S59] (B; transfer to engineering untested).
- GitLab has a separate Engineering Manager ML role (5+ years in ML or PhD, plus leadership experience) alongside a Staff ML engineer IC role [S32] (A).
- SignalFire's primary text names data engineering and DevOps as the fastest to leadership (2–3 years). The "AI engineers under three years" figure appears only in a chart that CIO Dive describes [S11][S12] (C).

- Set a weekly-hours review trigger at 55 [A for the risk, P for the trigger].
- Take one rest day a week [P].

## Open Questions & Risks

- Does AI-usage volume enter review scoring? Meta excluded it for 2025. Accenture records platform use for senior promotions. Leaderboards at some employers invite Goodhart effects.
- Do the newcomer meta-analyses hold in vertical-collectivistic cultures such as India? The 2025 paper tested culture as a moderator; I did not read the result.
- Pricing multipliers, vendor eval tooling and AI-use rules change quickly. Re-check at V2 and V3.
- Junior pipelines are shrinking at some employers [S70][S73], which may raise the stakes of a probation outcome for early-career hires.

## Discovered Concerns

- Job-security anxiety: 66% of India-based AI/ML respondents expect a layoff or significant headcount cut in their team within 3–6 months (n=1,552, July 2026, self-selected) [S69]. Probation may coincide with restructuring.
- Early-career squeeze: Anthropic reports junior technical hires fell from 27% to 9% of its technical hires [S70], and a Harvard study finds AI-adopting firms hired fewer juniors [S73]. This is context for job seekers and falls under the job-search sessions.
- AI-usage leaderboards ("tokenmaxxing") at some employers [S28] are a Goodhart risk for any review metric built on usage.
- METR's own experience suggests controlled evidence on current tools may stay thin [S19].
- OpenAI's guide says it is deprecating its Evals platform [S81]. Eval tooling built on a vendor platform may need migration.
- K4's 8.6% is a half-year figure [S14]. Label drift in the knowledge base is worth checking elsewhere.
- Indian state working-hours rules diverge from the OSH Code [S46][S47].
- A reported 5% lowest-rating practice at one large services firm [S80] is outside the AI scope but bears on performance expectations in services.
