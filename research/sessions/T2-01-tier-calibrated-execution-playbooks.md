# T2-01 — Tier-Calibrated Execution Playbooks across 6 Tiers and 5 Personas

> **How to read this file.** `U:T0-##` and `U:T1-##` reference upstream dependency sessions. Grades **A–E** follow the T1-02 EGP-1.0 rubric. `↑` marks an inherited grade from an upstream session. `[D]` marks a design judgement or model output from this session. The temporal anchor is 2026-10-25.
>
> **What this file is.** A synthesis of seven upstream research sessions into actionable, stage-by-stage career execution playbooks for five starting personas targeting six compensation tiers. Every prescription traces to at least one upstream finding.

## 2. Research Question

**Decision.** How should concrete career execution playbooks be structured to guide professionals through the 12-stage funnel based on their specific starting background (5 Personas) and target compensation tier (6 Tiers)?

**Sub-questions:**
1. Which lifecycle stages does each persona×tier combination actually traverse, and in what order?
2. What are the exact milestone gates, time allocations, and deliverables for each persona at each stage?
3. Where in the funnel do tax/legal decisions (T0-07), visa/relocation decisions (T0-08), and interview execution protocols (T1-03) become load-bearing?
4. What are the critical failure points in the first 90 days (T0-09) for each persona, and what preventative measures apply?
5. How should a practitioner calibrate their portfolio proof-of-work for Tier 1 (Growth) versus Tier 4 (Elite)?
6. How does the execution path for an Indian engineer pursuing US remote Tier 3 roles differ from one pursuing domestic Bangalore Tier 1 roles?
7. Which stages can senior practitioners accelerate or bypass?

**Method.** Pure synthesis of seven upstream sessions (T1-01: 738 lines, T0-06: 954 lines, T0-07: 618 lines, T0-08: 539 lines, T0-09: 760 lines, T0-18: 1,503 lines, T1-03: 494 lines). No new primary research was conducted. All evidence grades are inherited and marked `↑`. Design judgements are marked `[D]`.

## 4. Recommendation

### 4.1 The Persona × Tier Viability Matrix

Not all 30 permutations are viable within 12 months. This matrix identifies high-volume corridors (●), plausible-but-extended corridors (◐), and implausible targets (○).

| Persona \ Tier | T0 Entry (≤₹15L / $80K) | T1 Growth (₹15–30L / $80–150K) | T2 Senior (₹30–75L / $150–312K) | T3 Staff (₹75L–1.5Cr / $312–450K) | T4 Elite (₹1.5–3Cr / $450–800K) | T5 Apex ($800K+) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Backend/Systems SWE** | ○ (overqualified) | ● (fastest corridor) | ● (primary target) | ◐ (12–18 mo) | ○ (requires Staff+ track record) | ○ |
| **2. Data Scientist/BI** | ◐ (BI only) | ● (DS primary) | ◐ (production gap) | ○ | ○ | ○ |
| **3. Frontend/Full-Stack** | ○ (overqualified) | ● (AI product eng) | ◐ (senior AI product) | ○ | ○ | ○ |
| **4. Non-Technical** | ◐ (eval/annotation) | ◐ (AI PM/ops) | ○ | ○ | ○ | ○ |
| **5. PhD/Researcher** | ○ | ◐ (applied eng) | ● (applied scientist) | ◐ (research eng) | ◐ (frontier lab) | ○ (lottery) |

**● = High-volume corridor** — supported by upstream evidence of active hiring, realistic timelines, and documented transition paths.
**◐ = Plausible but extended** — requires either a two-stage transition, exceptional circumstances, or a timeline exceeding 12 months.
**○ = Implausible within scope** — either structurally mismatched or requires multi-year positioning.

[D] This matrix is a design judgement synthesised from T0-06's archetype roadmaps, T0-06's time-to-offer model, and T0-01's demand data.

Every persona playbook below follows the same structural spine, adapted per persona and tier:

**Phase 1: Orientation & Diagnostics (Weeks 1–2)**

- Make the entity/tax structure decision if India-resident (T0-07)

## 5. Alternatives Considered

| Alternative | What it offers | Why not the default | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A1. Single universal playbook** | Simplicity; one document to maintain | Fails the starting assumption: a ₹15L entry role and a ₹1.5Cr remote contract require radically different skill-building, legal structures, interview preparation, and onboarding strategies | U:PIPELINE §T2-01 KNOWN; confirmed by synthesis |
| **A2. Tier-first organisation** (6 tier playbooks, each covering all personas) | Natural for readers who know their tier but not their persona | Forces content duplication: the Backend SWE build phase is the same regardless of whether the target is Tier 1 or Tier 2; what differs is the interview prep and negotiation | [D]; T0-06's archetype roadmaps are persona-first |
| **A3. Pure matrix** (30 cells, each self-contained) | Complete coverage | Massive duplication; 22 of 30 cells are either implausible or duplicative; readers would face a wall of content | [D]; T2-01 spec acknowledges "highlighting the primary high-volume transition corridors" |
| **A4. Stage-first organisation** (12 stage guides, each covering all personas) | Aligns with T1-01's directory spine | Scatters each persona's journey across 12 files; a reader must reconstruct their own path | T1-01 KF11 (personas traverse 2–3 clicks, not 12) |
| **A5. Freelance-only track** | Deep coverage of T0-18's findings | Misses the 80%+ of users targeting employment; freelance is better as a branch within each persona playbook | T0-18 F6 (referral-dominant acquisition is persona-independent) |

**Decision [D].** Persona-first organisation with tier-specific callouts within each persona playbook. This mirrors T0-06's structure, avoids duplication, and lets readers identify their persona once and follow a single thread. The matrix (§4.1) provides the tier cross-reference. Freelance/fractional is integrated as a branch at Stage 6, not as a separate track.

## 6. Detailed Findings: The Five Persona Playbooks

### 6.0 Shared Infrastructure: The Milestone Gate System

Every persona uses the same four gates from T0-06 D3 (↑D), adapted below. Gate timing assumes Tier B (15–20 h/week); multiply by ~1.5× for Tier A (8–10 h/week) and ~0.5× for Tier C (35–40 h/week).

| Gate | Due (Tier B) | Required Artifacts | Verification | Upstream Anchor |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **G1 Measured v0** | Month 3 | Running LLM application over a real corpus. ≥50 labeled eval cases (≥30 hand-written from real work). ≥2 baselines compared. Error taxonomy from ≥100 reviewed traces. Written "what broke" memo (500+ words). | Reproduces headline eval numbers from README in <30 min | U:T0-06 D3, U:T1-03 KF3 |
| **G2 Operable v1** | Month 6 | Eval suite in CI with regression threshold. LLM judge validated on ≥100 human-labeled examples. Tracing + cost/latency view (p50/p95). Deployed to ≥1 real user. One postmortem. | CI badge, judge-agreement table, live URL, postmortem doc | U:T0-06 D3, U:T0-09 F6 |
| **G3 External Proof** | Month 9 | Second system with a *different failure surface*. One public write-up with numbers. One external contribution (OSS PR, talk, community review). ≥5 mock interview loops. ≥15–20 targeted applications. | Public links, mock feedback notes, application tracker | U:T0-06 D3, U:T1-03 R1 |
| **G4 Convert or Re-plan** | Month 12 | ≥1 offer or internal role change, **OR** a written funnel diagnosis and re-plan. | Offer letter, or diagnosis reviewed by one practitioner | U:T0-06 D3, U:T1-03 KF1 |

**Gate failure rules [D]:**
- G1 missed by >4 weeks → reduce scope (single retrieval use case, not a multi-tool agent)
- G2 missed by >4 weeks → assess whether the blocker is skills (fixable) or available time (move to Tier A timeline)
- G3 missed and <5 applications sent → the bottleneck is search, not prep; begin applying now
- G4 missed with no offers → run the funnel diagnosis: where do you lose (screen? take-home? onsite? offer?) and change one variable (loop type, level, or tier)

### 6.0.2 First 90 Days: Universal Survival Checklist

From T0-09's evidence-based findings, applicable across all personas. Persona-specific adaptations are in each playbook below.

**Week 1–2: The Expectations Contract**
- [ ] Written success metrics for 30/60/90 days, agreed with manager [D, from U:T0-09 F8, R5]
- [ ] Data access verified: can you touch the actual production data? If not, escalate immediately [U:T0-09 F4]
- [ ] Compute/API budget confirmed in writing: monthly spend limit, GPU quota, model tier [U:T0-09 F5]
- [ ] Approval workflow mapped: who signs off on model deployments? What policy applies? [U:T0-09 F7]
- [ ] Identify the "golden 20" — 20 real failure cases from production logs for your first eval set [U:T0-09 F6, U:T0-06 KF4]

**Week 3–4: First Measured Artifact**
- [ ] Ship a regression eval suite over the golden 20 cases, running in CI [U:T0-09 F6, R5]
- [ ] Quantify run-to-run variance: pass^k with error bars on at least 5 runs [U:T0-09 F6]
- [ ] First cost audit: cost per task at current usage × projected scale [U:T0-09 F10]

**Month 2: Credibility Staking**
- [ ] Deliver one measurable improvement (even small: a 40% token-spend cut, a 5-point eval improvement) [U:T0-09 F10]
- [ ] Report non-deterministic results upward using the "variance band" framing, not point estimates [U:T0-09 F6, R6]
- [ ] First internal presentation: "here is what we measured, here is what broke, here is the plan" [D]

**Month 3: Scope Lock**
- [ ] Narrow to one deliverable that will be production-ready by Day 90 [U:T0-09 R5]
- [ ] If the original scope is infeasible, renegotiate now — not at the probation review [U:T0-09 F3]
- [ ] Document every blocker and resolution in a running log (this is your probation defense) [D]

### 6.1 Persona 1: The Systems Engineer Pivot

**Profile.** Backend/Distributed Systems SWE, 5+ years (Java/Go/C++), targeting Senior/Staff AI Inference Engineer or Applied AI Engineer.

**Why this persona pivots fastest [↑B/C].** Experienced SWEs are the dominant feeder into AI engineer titles (U:T0-06 KF3). Their distributed-systems literacy (consensus, caching, load balancing, observability) transfers directly to inference serving, batching, and GPU scheduling. The binding new skills are LLM-specific patterns and evaluation methodology.

**Target tiers and corridors.**

| Target Tier | Feasibility | Timeline (Tier B) | Key differentiator |
|---|---|---|---|
| **T1 Growth (₹15–30L / $80–150K)** | ● High | ~4–6 mo | Overqualified; use as a stepping stone only if switching geographies |
| **T2 Senior (₹30–75L / $150–312K)** | ● High | ~6 mo (3.5–8) | Primary target for most; lateral level move |
| **T3 Staff (₹75L–1.5Cr / $312–450K)** | ◐ Extended | ~10–14 mo | Requires Staff-level scope artifacts + infrastructure depth |
| **T4+ Elite** | ○ | >18 mo | Requires established AI track record |

**Stage-by-Stage Execution (Tier B: 15–20 h/week)**

**Stages 1–2 (Landscape + Exploration): 1 week [D]**
Largely skip. You already know the tech industry. Spend one weekend reviewing:
- T0-01's verified role taxonomy: understand AI Engineer vs ML Engineer vs Applied Scientist distinctions
- T0-02's compensation tiers for your target geography
- The three interview loop types (T0-06 D1): applied-AI startup, big-company ML/AI, frontier lab

**Stage 3 (Skills): Weeks 1–8**
Skip foundations-first sequencing (U:T0-06 R1 rule 6). Start building immediately.

| Week | Focus (15–20 h) | Deliverable |
|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | LLM serving architecture: KV-cache math, continuous batching, prefill/decode disaggregation | Notes + a toy vLLM deployment on Modal (free credits) |
| 3–4 | RAG fundamentals: chunking, embedding, retrieval, reranking | Working retrieval pipeline over a real corpus from your domain |
| 5–6 | Evaluation methodology: golden sets, LLM judges, pass^k | 30+ hand-labeled eval cases; 2 baselines compared |
| 7–8 | Agent patterns: tool calling, approval loops, context management | Add tool use to the retrieval system; trace every call |

**Comparative advantage in evals [↑B]:** Your regression CI and observability instincts map directly to eval-in-CI and anomaly detection on model outputs (U:T0-06 KF4).

**Stage 4 (Credentials): Skip [D]**
For Backend SWEs, portfolio trumps certifications. If targeting a specific cloud provider (GCP, AWS), consider the ML specialty cert as a signal, but only after G2 is passed.

**Stage 5 (Portfolio): Weeks 9–16 → G1 (Month 3)**
Build the two systems that will carry your interview candidacy.

*System 1 (G1 target): Inference-serving evaluation harness*
- A production-grade LLM application with traced inference, cost tracking, and a CI-gated eval suite
- Must demonstrate: batching, caching, cost/latency trade-offs, regression detection
- Calibrated for target tier:
  - **Tier 1–2:** One system, one eval suite, one postmortem [D]
  - **Tier 3:** Add a load test, a capacity model, and a published write-up [D]

*System 2 (G3 target): Different failure surface*
- If System 1 is retrieval-based → System 2 is agent-based (or vice versa)
- Must include: a failure mode that System 1 did not have, a new eval methodology

**For Tier 3+ targeting US remote from India:**
Begin O-1A evidence accumulation now (U:T0-08 KF1). Your systems work qualifies for:
- Criterion 5 (original contributions of major significance): inference optimisation with measurable impact
- Criterion 6 (scholarly articles): a technical blog post or conference paper counts if it's in a qualifying venue
- Criterion 8 (high salary/remuneration): US remote Tier 3 salary itself qualifies

**Stages 6–7 (Discovery + Application): Months 4–7**

*Channel strategy (from T1-03 R1):*
- 60–70% referral: leverage your existing SWE network; ask former colleagues now at AI companies
- Identify 3–5 target companies by loop type (infrastructure vs application track)
- Begin applying at ~70% build completion (U:T0-06 KF11)

*India-specific (if billing internationally):*
- If contracting: register as sole proprietor under §44ADA before first invoice (U:T0-07 KF2–KF4)
- File GST registration + LUT (U:T0-07 KF6)
- Set up an FEMA-compliant AD bank account with proper purpose codes (P0802) (U:T0-07 KF7)
- **Critical:** Stay below ₹75L receipts or plan the cliff carefully (U:T0-07 KF3)

**Stage 8 (Networking): Ongoing, not a phase**
- Contribute to 1–2 AI communities (Latent Space, MLOps Community, local meetups)
- Target: one external contribution by G3 (OSS PR, talk, or community review)

**Stages 9–10 (Interview Prep + Execution): Months 5–9**

*Track selection (confirm with recruiter):*

| Target company type | Correct track | Core preparation |
|---|---|---|
| AI infrastructure (Anthropic, Databricks infra, OpenAI infra) | Infrastructure | KV-cache math, decode roofline, continuous batching, GPU scheduling (U:T1-03 R2) |
| Applied AI (most startups, vertical AI) | Application | RAG with ACL enforcement, agent loop control, eval architecture (U:T1-03 R2) |
| Big-tech ML/AI (Meta, Google) | Classic SWE + AI overlay | Standard system design + AI add-on; algorithmic coding (U:T1-03 R2) |

*45-minute system design blueprint (from T1-03 R3):*
- 5 min requirements → 4 min targeted estimate → 9 min HLD + walk → 16 min two deep dives → 7 min eval/safety/ops → 4 min wrap
- **Your edge:** You already think in systems. The rescue move at 0:09 ("stop drawing; do the four-line estimate") is natural for you.

*Behavioral preparation:*
Bank 4 STAR-AI stories (U:T1-03 R4, §6.2). For Backend SWEs, prioritise:
- B7 ("most expensive compute mistake") — you have these stories
- B9 ("re-architect mid-project because model behaviour changed") — map to a system migration
- B11 ("how do you evaluate whether an LLM feature is working") — your eval harness is the answer
- B1 ("model failed catastrophically in production") — translate a production incident to AI context

**Stage 11 (Negotiation): Upon receiving offer(s)**

Follow T1-03 R5's phased protocol:
1. Never disclose current compensation
2. Let them make the first offer
3. Counter 10–20% above with a single data-backed email
4. Negotiate level, not just comp: level-median step is +38–46% per level (U:T1-03 KF7)
5. Risk-adjust all private equity using ERDM: 0.5–0.8× for frontier labs, 0.4–0.8× for Series C–D (U:T1-03 KF8)

*Tier-specific negotiation:*

| Tier | Negotiation focus | Key lever |
|---|---|---|
| **T1** | Base salary alignment to market; sign-on to cover forfeited vest | Levels.fyi P50–P75 for target level |
| **T2** | Total comp including equity; vest schedule shape | Ask for Google-style (66% at 24 mo) vs Amazon-style (20% at 24 mo) (U:T1-03 §6.5) |
| **T3** | Level negotiation (Staff vs Senior); equity due-diligence | Run the full 9-question equity checklist (U:T1-03 §6.6) |

*India→US remote negotiation:*
- Structure as contractor under §44ADA (up to ₹75L) for maximum take-home (83–84% of billing; U:T0-07 KF4)
- At ₹75L+, evaluate LLP or EOR based on T0-07's crossover analysis
- Budget for EOR fee drag (~₹6.9L/year) only if the client requires it (U:T0-07 KF5)

**Stage 12 (Onboarding / First 90 Days)**

Apply the universal checklist (§6.0.2) plus Backend-specific adaptations:

| Day | Backend SWE-specific action | Persona vulnerability |
|---|---|---|
| 1–3 | Map the existing inference/serving architecture; identify observability gaps | Tendency to "fix the infra first" instead of shipping a feature |
| 7–14 | Ship the regression eval suite using your CI instincts | Under-investing in eval because "the system works" |
| 15–30 | Deliver a measured cost reduction (even 10–20% token saving) | Over-engineering: building a perfect serving layer instead of a working prototype |
| 30–60 | Own one end-to-end feature with eval gates | Scope creep into infrastructure work that delays user-facing impact |
| 60–90 | Production postmortem of a real failure; systemic prevention | Not documenting the non-determinism management approach for the team |

## 8. Discovered Concerns

- **Compound cliff risk for India→US remote contractors.** A contractor billing $80K/year to a US client faces the §44ADA cliff (~₹75L at ₹96/$) at almost exactly the billing level where freelance AI work becomes viable. The cliff penalty (₹11.8L) is large enough to make the $75K–$94K billing range a **dead zone** where higher revenue produces lower take-home. This interaction between T0-07's tax findings and T0-18's rate findings was not anticipated by either upstream session and deserves a dedicated planning worksheet.

- **Evidence-building for visa applications and portfolio development are the same work, done at the same time, but documented differently.** A practitioner building O-1A evidence (criterion 5: original contributions; criterion 6: scholarly articles) is simultaneously building portfolio proof-of-work for interview candidacy. The playbooks should encourage maintaining a single evidence ledger that can be formatted for both purposes, avoiding duplicated effort.

- **The behavioral interview veto risk is under-prepared by most technical practitioners.** T1-03 KF4 establishes that behavioral "No Hire" is a near-automatic veto at Anthropic and Google, but T0-06's archetype roadmaps allocate zero explicit time to behavioral preparation. The playbooks above add behavioral prep as a concurrent activity, but the risk of under-investment remains high, especially for PhDs and Backend SWEs who may dismiss behavioral rounds as "soft skills."

### 11.3 Geographic Arbitrage: India→US Remote at Tier 2–3

This is the highest-ROI corridor but the most complex to execute. Three regulatory regimes run simultaneously:

| Timeline | Action | Regime | Upstream |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Before first invoice** | Choose structure: §44ADA (≤₹75L) or LLP/Pvt Ltd (>₹75L) or EOR | Indian tax | U:T0-07 KF2–KF5 |
| **Before first invoice** | GST registration + annual LUT (Form RFD-11) | Indian GST | U:T0-07 KF6 |
| **Monthly** | FEMA Export Declaration Form (EDF) to AD bank within 30 days of month-end | Indian FEMA | U:T0-07 KF7 |
| **Within 9 months of invoice** | Ensure foreign-exchange realisation (new FEMA regime from 1 Oct 2026) | Indian FEMA | U:T0-07 KF7 |
| **For US client** | W-8BEN to the US client; understand treaty benefits | US tax | U:T0-07 KF8 |
| **If targeting US relocation** | Begin O-1A evidence building 12–18 months before filing | US immigration | U:T0-08 KF1–KF6 |
| **If earning $80K+ / ₹75L+** | Plan billing around the §44ADA cliff or commit to jumping past ₹94L | Indian tax | U:T0-07 KF3 |
